HOW TO READ TABLE 3.14 (this file's reading, not the report's words): its header is
flattened across lines. The eight numbers in each row are Rural 2009-10, 2011-12,
2022-23, 2023-24, then Urban in the same order, per capita kcal per day. So the All-India
row's second and sixth numbers are the 2011-12 rural and urban figures.
WHAT THESE NUMBERS ARE: household consumption converted to energy with a conversion
table, i.e. an estimate of energy acquired by households, not a measured intake of
individuals.

3. Chapter Two, 2.9.6.0: how household intake of energy is calculated
https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/Nutritional_Intake_in_India_L.pdf
===============================================================================

"2.9.6.0 The quantities of food recorded as consumed by the household are converted into the 
equivalent amounts of energy, protein and fat on the basis of a Nutrition Conversion Table as 
prepared by a committee constituted by the Ministry of Statistics & Pr ogramme Implementation 
in 2025. The table as included in this Chapter gives the amount of energy, protein and fat content 
per unit of different foods in the Indian diet. However, the actual intake of nutrients depends on 
how these foods are processed and/or cooked in the surveyed households."

[NOTE] From the same chapter, on the conversion table used till 2011-12 being revised:

"requirement of revising the nutrient conversion table that was used till 2011-12. With a view to 
take up this exercise, MoSPI constituted a Committee for Preparation of Nutrient Conversion 
Table and Related Tasks through an office order dated 29 January 2025. The nutrient conversion 
table as given below will be used for this report."

as ‘per capita’. The ‘per consumer unit’ calorie intake estimate considers the difference in calorie 
requirements among persons on account of age and gender differences, and thereby serve as a 
sharper indicator of adequacy of intake than the ‘per capita’ figures."

"3.3.0 The 66 th round (2009-10) and 68th round (2011-12) surveys used two different schedules , 
namely, Sch. Type 1 and Sch. Type 2.  in two different sets of households to measure household 
consumption expenditure. Of these, Type 1 used a ‘30 days’ reference period for all food items 
while Type 2 used 30 days for some item groups and 7 days for others. From the 68th round (2011-
12), it was decided to adopt the Modified Mixed Reference Period (MMRP) in which consumption 
expenditure on edible oil, egg, fish and meat, vegetables, fruits, spices, beverages, refreshments, 
processed food, pan, tobacco and intoxica nts would be recorded for a reference period of “last 7 
days”; clothing and bedding, footwear, education, institutional medical care and durable goods 
for a reference period of “last 365 days” and the remaining items for a period of 30 days. Thus, for 
the purpose of c omparison of trends in nutritional intake, the estimates utilizing the data of 
Schedule Type 2 for the years 2009-10 and 2011-12 are considered here."
