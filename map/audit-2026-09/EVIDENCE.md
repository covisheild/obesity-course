# Consolidated candidate gaps (conductor's merge of audits A–F)

Letters in brackets say which independent audits raised it: A clinical, B policy, C causal system,
D professional competencies, E India landscape, F blind first-principles derivation.

G1. Settings as delivery platforms: no subject is built around schools (food standards, canteens, PE),
    anganwadis/ICDS, workplaces, health facilities as settings, or faith/community institutions. [F, B, D, C, E]
G2. Physical-activity environment: built environment, urban planning, walkability, active transport,
    municipal/urban local body governance; activity transition from labour-saving technology. [C, B, F]
G3. Early life: infant and young child feeding, breastfeeding promotion/protection, formula-marketing law,
    parental feeding practices, rapid infant weight gain, growth monitoring; paediatric complications. [C, B, F, A]
G4. Clinical: (a) treating complications and deprescribing as weight falls (T2D, HTN, lipids, PCOS, OA, GERD);
    (b) cardiorenal (HFpEF, AF, CKD); (c) older adults, sarcopenic obesity, menopause; (d) opening the
    conversation: asking permission, 5As, shared decision-making; (e) multidisciplinary clinic / chronic-care
    design; (f) post-bariatric weight recurrence and care; (g) supplements and commercial weight-loss products;
    (h) drug-induced weight gain beyond psychotropics. [A, F]
G5. Leadership, people/project/financial management, negotiation, institution building, community engagement
    and mobilisation, sustaining oneself over decades. [D, F]
G6. Designing policy instruments (not just evidence on them): marketing restrictions to children, public
    food procurement standards, school-food rules, menu labelling, retail/zoning; double-duty actions across
    undernutrition programmes (PDS, PM POSHAN, ICDS); benchmarking government policy (Food-EPI). [B, F]
G7. Equity blind spots: gender lens (women's time use, intra-household food allocation), tribal populations,
    cultural norms (fasting, festivals, hospitality feeding, body-size ideals), linguistic diversity. [C, F, D]
G8. India landscape named/current: CDSCO and GLP-1 prescriber rules; FSSAI school-children regulations 2020;
    NFHS-6; Poshan Tracker; 2025 Indian obesity definition; NNMB; RBSK/RKSK; NITI Aayog, ICMR-NIN, NHSRC;
    Time Use Survey; AYUSH; the private sector as the main provider. [E, F]
G9. Health-promotion theory, health literacy, social marketing and mass-media campaign design. [D, B, F]
G10. Other determinants: psychosocial stress/ACEs; smoking cessation; alcohol; heat/climate; no named
     determinants framework (Foresight, ANGELO, Global Syndemic) anywhere. [C]
G11. Structure: ~67/195 rungs are research methods; 11 of 19 Expert subjects are methods; the change-making
     subjects (S34, S37, S39, S41, S47, S50, S51) stop at Advanced; S43 at Intermediate; implementation
     taught to district scale only. [F]

## Top gaps (ranked for the goal: attack obesity from the clinic to policy in India)

1. **Managing complications, including deprescribing (T2D, HTN, lipids, PCOS, OA, GERD)**, PARTIAL (rows 61–66). The Lancet framework defines treatment success as improvement in organ manifestations, so Harsh can currently diagnose clinical obesity but cannot treat to its endpoint. It is also ABOM's heaviest clinical block (A.10 6% + B.9 7%), and most Indian obesity patients arrive through a diabetes clinic.
2. **Cardiorenal criteria: HFpEF, AF, thrombosis, CKD/albuminuria**, ABSENT (rows 79, 119, 120). These are several of the Lancet Commission's 18 adult criteria. S20-R3 BUILD asks him to operationalise preclinical versus clinical obesity in an Indian sample, and that cannot be done without them.
3. **Older adults, sarcopenic obesity and menopause**, ABSENT (rows 55–56, 116–117). India combines a low-muscle phenotype with rapid ageing and a mass generic-semaglutide market. Incretin-driven lean-mass loss is most dangerous in exactly this group, and no rung covers it.
4. **Opening the conversation: asking permission, the 5As, shared decision-making**, ABSENT (rows 5, 87, 88, 114). This is step 1 of the Canadian guideline, OMEC PC5 and ABOM B.10. It is cheap to learn, and it is what he would teach primary-care doctors and CHOs to do at scale.
5. **Designing a multidisciplinary clinic and a clinical chronic-care model**, PARTIAL (rows 29, 31, 72, 124). S22-R3 builds a clinic slot but specifies no team, equipment, follow-up or recall system, or telemedicine. Scaling in India will depend on task-shared, protocolised follow-up.
6. **The Indian clinical-guidance landscape**, PARTIAL (rows 12, 105–108). No clinical obesity guideline is named. S20-R4 expects him to adjudicate Indian definitions without knowing the Misra 2025 Stage 1/Stage 2 scheme, the ESI–OSSI algorithm or the 55-statement consensus.
7. **After bariatric surgery: weight recurrence, adjunct OMMs, dumping, post-operative self-harm**, ABSENT/PARTIAL (rows 52, 104, 129). There is an Indian consensus on this (2025), and S25-R2's gate asks him to take over the medical follow-up of surgical patients.
8. **Supplements and commercial weight-loss products**, ABSENT (rows 38, 100). India's large unregulated Ayurvedic and slimming market is where the map's own S23 prose says patients go. The topic also connects to the FSSAI and advertising regulation already in S48.
9. **Women's reproductive-years management: PCOS treatment, infertility, contraception on OMMs, stopping GLP-1 before conception**, PARTIAL (rows 54, 66, 102). This matters for the intergenerational argument the map already makes in S16 and S26.
10. **Paediatric complications and early-feeding counselling**, PARTIAL (rows 76, 81, 113). The map's own premise is that "India's obesity problem is arriving through its children", yet S26 has no paediatric complication, puberty or feeding-style content.
11. **Drug-induced weight gain beyond psychotropics, and weight-neutral prescribing**, PARTIAL (row 53). This is a fast, low-cost win in any medical outpatient department (insulin/SU, β-blockers, steroids, antiepileptics).
12. **Other organ complications (IIH, asthma/OHS, urinary incontinence, lymphoedema/lipoedema, male hypogonadism, skin)**, ABSENT (rows 43, 65, 118). These need only recognise-and-refer depth. They could go in S22-R2 or S61.

- **OMEC wording.** The item wording comes from WebFetch's rendering of the OMEC PDF. Half of it is confirmed word for word by a second page. The 2019 Kushner paper was seen only as an abstract.
- **Canadian guideline.** Only the post-operative chapter and the CMAJ summary were opened; the chapter titles come from the chapters page.

- **EASO.** No curriculum or syllabus was reachable, so EASO is not mapped.

## Top gaps (ranked by importance for eliminating or substantially reducing obesity in India)

1. **Public food procurement and service standards** (A6, B20, D13, F5; ABSENT). This is a WHO best buy and an Acceleration Plan action, yet no rung covers it. The Indian state is a very large food buyer and provider through PM POSHAN, ICDS, hospitals, hostels and canteens, so this is a lever government controls directly and industry cannot easily block.
2. **Double-duty design of India's undernutrition programmes** (B6, D11, E10, E1; PARTIAL). "Undernutrition" appears in no rung. Nutrition standards for PDS, PM POSHAN, ICDS and take-home rations are where India's obesity and undernutrition policies collide, and the map treats this as a conversation (S37-R2) rather than a design skill.
3. **Marketing restrictions to children, including digital and packaging** (A3, B10–B12, D8, D9, F7; PARTIAL, with packaging ABSENT). The evidence and the regulator map are strong, but no rung asks Harsh to draft or defend an Indian restriction. It is a best buy and an Acceleration Plan target, and the South Asia Food-EPI rated India's restriction weak.
4. **Built environment and active transport** (C3, C4, E4, F17; ABSENT). Two of MOVING's six areas and two WHO "other recommended" PA interventions have no rung at all. Rapid Indian urbanisation makes this a durable, population-scale lever with climate co-benefits (see gap 10).
5. **Government-policy benchmarking and accountability (Food-EPI)** (D3, E7, A10; PARTIAL). The map teaches benchmarking of industry but not of government. India already has a Food-EPI baseline (Lancet Reg Health SE Asia 2024), and updating it is a publishable project in Harsh's own lane that is useful for accountability.
6. **School food environment standards and school PA/PE** (B5, B12, D12, F18, C1; PARTIAL/ABSENT). S26-R3 frames school work around stigma and screening. The standard-setting side (canteens, the vicinity rule India already has per FEPI-SA, physical education) is missing, although the S48-R3 court process already pulls schools in.
7. **Retail, food-service and quick-commerce regulation** (B17, B18, D5, D15; PARTIAL). These are measured well (S38) but not regulated. Zoning around schools was rated "high" achievability by the India Food-EPI panel. Municipal licensing and aggregator rules are not taught.
8. **Early food environment and breastfeeding protection** (A5, F2, F8; PARTIAL). This is a best buy and an Acceleration Plan target. The map covers breastfeeding only as confounded epidemiology.
9. **Mass media and social-marketing competence** (A9, B24, F6, F15; PARTIAL). The map argues against awareness campaigns, but WHO lists them as best buys and Indian policy cites the Best Buys. Harsh needs to be able to run a campaign well as well as argue for structural levers.
10. **Global Syndemic framing and climate co-benefits** (E1, E14; PARTIAL, with the climate depth ROUTED). One C/K pair in S37-R3 only. The climate frame is where food-policy money and allies are moving (S37-R3's own claim).
11. **GLP-1 public-sector prioritisation and procurement** (G3, G4; PARTIAL). With generics in the Indian market (S24-R1), the policy question is who gets the drug from the public system and at what procured price. WHO names pooled procurement, tiered pricing and voluntary licensing, and none of these is taught.
12. **Menu labelling** (B3, D7, F11; ABSENT). Cheap and plausible for chains and aggregators, and S38-R3 already scrapes delivery menus, so the gap is small to close.

Lower-priority gaps: healthy-food subsidies and tariffs (B8, B9); portion and pack-size limits (B15, F12); health-in-all-policies and national coordination (B21, D30, D33); a statutory health-promotion agency (D29); business-model and investor levers (E8); nutrition-workforce density (A11); salt (B16); urban agriculture (B22); transparency and RTI (D24).

## Top gaps (ranked for Harsh's goal of reducing obesity in India at every level)

1. **Physical-activity environment: built environment, walkability, transport, urban design, access and safety (ABSENT across 11 of Foresight's 13 PA-environment variables, ANGELO micro/macro-physical, and two of GS's four driving systems).** This is the largest structural hole. FB names walkability, sedentary employment, motorised transport and access to exercise as its first intervention starting points. India's rapid motorisation and urban growth will play out here, and S38's GIS and audit skills stop at food.
2. **Activity-saving technology and the secular decline in occupational, domestic and transport activity (ABSENT/PARTIAL).** PO names this as one of the two direct drivers in LMICs. The map teaches only the occupational paradox. Harsh cannot currently describe or measure India's activity transition (e.g. with time-use data), even if he concludes, following S30-R3, that it matters less than diet.
3. **Gender norms, women's time use and intra-household food allocation (ABSENT/PARTIAL; the econometrics are routed).** Obesity is higher in Indian women, and women's mobility, safety, domestic labour and food allocation shape both intake and activity. The map contains no gender lens at any rung.
4. **Early-childhood behavioural determinants: infant and young child feeding and breastfeeding, parental feeding control, children's influence on diet, early activity patterns, physical education (ABSENT/PARTIAL).** DOHaD biology is excellent (S16), but the modifiable behaviours in the first years are missing. So is how to deliver change through ICDS, POSHAN and IYCF, which S26-R3 names as the vehicle.
5. **Psychosocial stress as a social determinant, including ACEs and job strain (PARTIAL/ABSENT).** It is a leverage point in FB (Map 25), but the map gives it one bullet inside the sleep subject, with no instrument, no social sources and no intervention.
6. **No named determinants framework (PARTIAL).** Foresight, ANGELO, the Global Syndemic and the socio-ecological model never appear. One Intermediate-rung bullet (e.g. in S44-R2 or S37-R2) requiring Harsh to locate any cause on Foresight or ANGELO would cheaply close the "is every cause covered?" question for the future.
7. **Smoking and smoking cessation (ABSENT).** India has a large tobacco-using population and NTCP cessation services, and weight gain after quitting is a routine counselling and confounding issue. Tobacco appears only as a policy analogy.
8. **Cultural norms on body size and food in India (fatness as prosperity or health, festive and hospitality feeding, religious fasting) (ABSENT/PARTIAL).** Stigma is taught thoroughly, but the opposite cultural pressure, which drives overfeeding and under-recognition, is not.
9. **Alcohol as an intake determinant (ABSENT).** It is a Foresight food-consumption variable. Alcohol calories and state excise policy are relevant to Indian men, and the map mentions alcohol only as a post-bariatric complication.
10. **Heat and climate acting on physical activity and food prices (ABSENT/ROUTED).** This is a GS-named pathway, and a growing one for India. Planetary health is partly in S37-R3 and otherwise routed to S61, but the heat-to-activity link appears nowhere.

Lower priority, and defensible to leave ABSENT or at reading level given weak evidence or low leverage: infections/adenovirus, maternal age, assortative mating, fecundity, individualism, predisposition to activity, and product differentiation. Microbiome and endocrine disruptors are already handled at a proportionate reading level in S19-R3.

## Top gaps (ranked by importance for reducing obesity in India at population scale)

1. **Leadership (ABSENT).** ASPHER A5.1 and A5.7, CoL C8.2 and C8.6, and the NMC syllabus area "Public Health Leadership" (N-C18) all require it. "Leader" does not appear anywhere in the 4,456-line map. Moving obesity nationally means leading teams, consortia and eventually programme components. The map produces a trusted adviser, not a leader.
2. **Negotiation and conflict management (PARTIAL).** NMC N-S4 lists it explicitly, as does GCF G17. Negotiation appears only for authorship and data-use agreements. Policy change in India runs through negotiations with the GST Council, FSSAI, other ministries, states and industry. The map teaches analysis of these actors (S39, S47) but not how to bargain with them.
3. **Community engagement, mobilisation and power-sharing (PARTIAL).** Relevant items: Ottawa O-A3, CoL C5.2–C5.5, ASPHER A1.6 and A2.5, NMC N-C11. Participation in the map means officials (S45), patients and frontline workers (S36-R3). Communities, Panchayats and urban local bodies, SHGs, community-based participatory research, community needs and asset assessment, and accountability to communities are all missing. Structural policy in a democracy also needs public demand, which the map never builds.
4. **People, project and financial management (ABSENT/PARTIAL).** Relevant items: ASPHER A8.2, A8.4, A8.5, A8.7 and A8.8; CoL C7.2, C7.3 and C7.6; NMC N-S3 and N-S5; EPHF E-P4. The map asks for a multi-district hybrid trial (S50-R3), an owned cohort (S54-R4) and a delivered PIP activity (S51-R3). None of these can be run without hiring, supervising, project planning, risk management and expenditure accounting, and no rung teaches them.
5. **Health-promotion theory: settings approach and health literacy (PARTIAL).** Relevant items: Ottawa O-SET and O-A4, ASPHER A2.2, GCF G-HL. "Health promotion" never appears. Settings are covered piecemeal (school, city) and the workplace is missing. Health literacy goes untaught even though front-of-pack label comprehension and the Supreme Court's school "nutrition literacy" question (S48-R3) depend on it.
6. **Cultural and linguistic competence (PARTIAL).** Relevant items: ASPHER A7.5, CoL C3.2, GCF G3. Stigma-safe language is Expert-level (S35), but India's diversity of language, religion, caste, region, gender and tribal communities is never treated as a communication or design skill.
7. **Designing health communication campaigns, social marketing and IEC (PARTIAL).** Relevant items: ASPHER A7.4, NMC N-P1. The map rightly ranks campaigns as a weak lever (S34-R3). 8. **Organisational strategy, change management and institution building (PARTIAL).** Relevant items: CoL C2.6 and C8.6, ASPHER A5.8 and A8.1, extra row X5. Lasting national change usually needs an institution (a centre, unit or technical-support group). The map builds assets but not the organisation that holds them. S55-R3's "administrative capture" warning suggests this is deliberate, which is **a decision for Harsh**.
9. **Public-health informatics (PARTIAL).** Relevant item: CoL C1.7. ABDM, the NCD portal and HMIS appear only at awareness level (S51-R2). Routine obesity surveillance at population scale will run on these systems.
10. **Workforce development and planning for obesity care (PARTIAL).** Relevant items: EPHF E-P1, CoL C4.6, ASPHER A9.6. Teaching residents is strong (S57). Training frontline cadres (CHOs, ASHAs) and planning the obesity-care workforce (dietitians, counsellors) are missing.
11. **Crisis communication (PARTIAL, X1).** Normal media relations are covered; rapid response in a crisis is not.
12. **Partnership evaluation (ABSENT, A6.5); risk and contingency planning (ABSENT, A8.8/C7.9); personal wellbeing and resilience (ABSENT, G24/A10.3).** These are lower priority, but a 30-year programme depends on the last one.
13. **Emergency preparedness (ABSENT).** Relevance to obesity is low; recorded for completeness only.

## Top gaps (ranked)

1. **Settings are missing (R7: schools, anganwadis/ICDS, workplaces, faith and community institutions; 0 rungs).** The map says obesity is arriving through children, but has no competency to design, cost or regulate school food and physical activity or anganwadi nutrition. These are the delivery platforms that already reach almost every child.
2. **Built environment and urban governance are absent (walkability, active transport, open space, zoning or licensing near schools, municipal bodies).** This is the only population-scale lever on physical activity and the urban food environment beyond the food-outlet census, and India is urbanising fast.
3. **Early-life feeding is missing (IYCF, breastfeeding promotion, formula-marketing law, rapid infant weight gain, growth monitoring).** The map's own developmental argument (S16) points here, yet infancy has no competency; the only mention is that the breastfeeding literature is confounded.
4. **Leading organisations is missing (team, project and budget management; institution building; negotiation; sustaining oneself; M3/M7/M8/M12).** A 30-year elimination goal needs someone who builds and runs units, programmes and coalitions, not only a body of papers.
5. **Equity blind spots: tribal populations (absent) and gender (only a stratifier).** The equity-protective design the map asks for (S17 R3) cannot be done without them, and the tribal gap matters most in Harsh's own state.
6. **Regulation beyond front-of-pack labels and GST is thin (statutory marketing restriction, drug and remedies-advertising law, formula-marketing law, school-food rules).** The lever hierarchy in S34 R3 ranks marketing restriction above labelling, but the map only teaches the evidence on marketing, not how to design the instrument.
7. **The private sector and AYUSH are absent as health providers (commercial weight-loss clinics, unsupervised GLP-1 channels, AYUSH practitioners).** Most Indian care is delivered privately, and the map treats the system as almost wholly public (S51).
8. **Culture and social norms (fasting, festivals, hospitality and feeding norms, body-size ideals) appear only in passing.** They shape dietary counselling, message design and stigma in India, and 4.10 and 16.5 are only partial.
9. **Whole-of-government work above district level, and legislators, are thin (13.4, 13.5, 13.8).** Convergence is taught only at district level (S51 R3), but taxes, school food and urban policy are decided by state cabinets and legislatures.
10. **Older adults and sarcopenic obesity are absent.** This is a clinical safety gap in an ageing population; weight loss in older adults can do harm. It ranks lower for a prevention-led goal.
11. **Movement-building and mass-media campaigns are partial.** The map deliberately ranks awareness campaigns low (S34 R1) and that is defensible, but building public and political demand for structural policy (16.6) is a separate skill it does not teach.
