- **Journal:** Value in Health 2014;17(1):5-14 (e-pub Dec 2013). PMID 24438712

- **Quotes:**
  - "A BIA addresses the expected changes in the expenditure of a health care system after the adoption of a new intervention." (ScienceDirect full text, extractor)
  - "A BIA can be freestanding or part of a comprehensive economic assessment along with a CEA." (ScienceDirect full text, extractor)

Item 5 below gives primary sources that make the contrast explicitly.

### 2. HTAIn (Health Technology Assessment in India), Department of Health Research

- **Official site:** https://htain.dhr.gov.in/ (OPENED). Title: "Health Technology Assessment in India (HTAIn)". Body: Department of Health Research, MoHFW, Govt of India.
  - Site mission quote: "collate and where needed generate evidence related to the clinical effectiveness, cost-effectiveness, and safety of medicines, devices and health programs." (extractor)
  - Manual page https://htain.dhr.gov.in/htain-manual.php (OPENED): "Cost effectiveness reviews the technology for its value of money and affordability for the larger population of the country." (extractor)
- **HTAIn Manual:** "Health Technology Assessment in India: A Manual", DHR, October 2018. https://htain.dhr.gov.in/images/pdf/HTAIn_Manual.pdf (OPENED, PDF)
  - Budget impact appears as a proposal-checklist item: "Would a budget impact analysis be done and if it is done, at what level would be undertaken? (E.g., local, state, national)" (extractor)
  - Discounting: "Discounting – The discounting rate chosen with its justification" (extractor). The manual gives no specific rate.
  - The extractor found no threshold or GDP-per-capita text. That could be because the long PDF was cut off, so treat it as "not found", not as confirmed absent.
- **Process Manual (2022):** https://htain.dhr.gov.in/images/pdf/Process-Manual-2022.pdf (OPENED). It says assessment should follow "the HTA methods manual using the Indian reference case for economic evaluation" (extractor). It lists Budget Impact Analysis as an activity but gives no threshold or rate.
- **HTAIn/DIAMOnDS Guidelines (June 2021):** https://htain.dhr.gov.in/images/pdf/HTAIn_GUIDELIN_S_New.pdf (OPENED). No threshold, BIA method or discount rate. It covers institutional structure only.
- **Indian Reference Case (the methods source):** Sharma D, Prinja S, Aggarwal AK, Rajsekar K, Bahuguna P. "Development of the Indian Reference Case for undertaking economic evaluation for health technology assessment." *Lancet Regional Health – Southeast Asia* 2023. DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lansea.2023.100241. Full text OPENED at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10485782/
  - **Threshold (2023):** "Currently, a formally recognised cost-effectiveness threshold (CET) for India is not available. However, for the purpose of analysis, researchers may use one-time gross domestic product (GDP) per-capita until a CET is determined for India."
  - **Discounting:** "Costs and outcomes occurring beyond one year should be discounted to present value using a common annual discount rate of 3%." Sensitivity range: "varied from 0% to 5%".
  - **Budget impact:** "separate documents should be prepared on three important aspects including modelling, budget impact analysis (BIA) and cost-effectiveness thresholds (CET), in near future." The reference case therefore mentions BIA only as a future separate document. It does not specify a BIA method.
- **Newer threshold evidence (bonus, relevant):** Chugh Y, Jyani G, Kar SS, … Rajsekar K, Prinja S. "Estimating the Willingness-to-Pay per Quality-Adjusted Life-Year to Aid Health Technology Assessment in India." *Value Health* 2026;29(3):457-466 (e-pub Oct 2025). DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jval.2025.10.007. OPENED (PubMed abstract).
  - "mean weighted WTP/QALY was estimated to be INR 2 12 307 (US$ 2535)"
  - "The WTP/QALY ranges from 1 to 1.52 times the gross domestic product per capita. This could be considered as the cost-effectiveness threshold for health technology assessment in India."
  - Note that "could be considered" is a proposal, not an adopted official threshold.

### 5. Budget impact of GLP-1 / anti-obesity medications: cost-effective but unaffordable

All OPENED via PubMed abstracts (verbatim). No Indian study was found.
- **Pearson SD, Whaley CM, Emond SK (ICER).** "Affordable access to GLP-1 obesity medications: strategies to guide market action and policy solutions in the US." *J Comp Eff Res* 2025;14(9):e250083. DOI https://doi.org/10.57264/cer-2025-0083
  - "…the massive and likely ongoing cost of treating such a large segment of the population, even though use of the treatments is estimated to be cost-effective over a lifetime."
- **Ramachandran S, Urick B, Thomas T.** "ICER report demonstrates both the value and challenges in financing of weight loss medications." *J Manag Care Spec Pharm* 2026;32(6):753-757. DOI https://doi.org/10.18553/jmcp.2026.32.6.753
  - "…were found by the Institute for Clinical and Economic Review (ICER) to be cost-effective compared with lifestyle modifications alone. However, even limited uptake exceeds ICER's annual budget impact threshold…"
- **Estler B et al.** "Cost-Effectiveness and Budget-Impact Analysis of Semaglutide in Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction and Obesity in the German Health-Care System." *PharmacoEconomics* 2026;44(9):1077-1092. DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s40273-026-01621-4
  - "Semaglutide appears cost-effective for HFpEF with obesity… Yet it generates substantial budget impact, indicating that affordability and pricing will be critical…"
- Also relevant: Hennessy S et al. *JAMA Cardiol* 2026;11(3):229-238, DOI https://doi.org/10.1001/jamacardio.2025.5243. It projects that semaglutide for secondary CVD prevention in the US would "increase annual health care spending by $23 billion". Note that this paper found it NOT cost-effective at the net price ($148,100/QALY), so it does not fit the "cost-effective yet unaffordable" framing.
