1. **gap** · `must_know[4]`, "Government releases on edible oil use two terms, 'basic customs duty' and
   'effective customs duty', and they are not the same charge."; illustration 3's
   `analogy_breaks_when` (from compression hole 32; confirmed in part, and partly closable).
   - The held texts never say what the effective duty is made of, so the point asserts a difference
     it cannot show. One reading is supported by two held texts together, though no text states it.
     The 11 Jun 2025 release set the basic duty at 10 per cent, and the 24 Sep 2026 release cuts it
     "from 10%", so it was 10 per cent throughout. The 8 Dec 2025 backgrounder then puts the
     "effective customs duty" on the same crude oils at 16.5 per cent. The analogy's "it does not say
     which basic duty went with that" is therefore too strong. There is also a third term: the
     24 Sep 2026 release's title and body say "Import Duty" / "Import duties" while its figures are
     basic duty (the conductor's C16 note raises the same thing).
   - Fix: must_know[4]: "Releases on edible oil say 'import duty', 'basic customs duty' and
     'effective customs duty'. In December 2025, when the basic duty on crude oils was 10 per cent, a
     government backgrounder put the effective duty at 16.5 per cent. The held texts do not say what
     makes up the difference, so the landed-price rule here uses basic duty only." Mirror it in the
     analogy. Harsh to decide whether to obtain the customs notification that itemises the charges.
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 1 and 4).
   - Fixer: must_know[4] rewritten to name all three terms, the 10 per cent basic duty in Dec 2025 and the backgrounder's 16.5 per cent (said "without a date": the backgrounder says only "raised ... from 5.5% to 16.5%", so I did not assert the two held at the same time), and that the rule uses basic duty only; analogy mirrored, now uses the June 2025 entry. Harsh: customs notification itemising the charges still not held.
