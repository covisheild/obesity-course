# Audit defects · S37-R1-C12 · School meals and anganwadi food

Auditor: Task 3, auditor a2, 25 Sep 2026. Field indices are 0-based. "Recurring" means the kind is
on `check/SELFCHECK.md` or appears in `books/B0/defects/`, `books/S01-R1/defects/` or
`books/S02-R1/defects/`.

**Quotes.** All 21 `definition.references` quotes and all 15 quotes in `illustrations[].numbers`
found verbatim (`nfsa_2013.txt`, `s37_pib_1812421.txt`, `poshan2_guidelines_2022.txt`,
`pib_2251769.txt`), checked by script because `build.py` skips `illustrations[]`. Each number quote
states its number. Also checked by hand: all six Schedule II rows (500 / 12-15, 500 / 12-15, 800 /
20-25, 450 / 12, 700 / 20, 600 / 18-20); the guidelines' 3.2.1 table and its "under revision" note;
the funding table's column order (General, Salary, Supplementary Nutrition), so the 50:50, 90:10 and
100:0 read correctly; PIB 1812421's norms table ("Salt & condiments / As per need", no sugar row),
its date (1 Apr 2022), and that it is the Minister of Women and Child Development's written reply in
the Lok Sabha. Nothing rests on the withdrawn PM POSHAN 2023 guidelines, and no Centre-State split is
given for PM POSHAN, as the facts intake requires. The anganwadi norms are quoted as the 2022 table
and said to be under revision, as required. No quote was edited.

**Arithmetic.** Every PM POSHAN upper-primary quantity is 1.5 times the primary one (150/100, 30/20,
75/50, 7.5/5), as the figure's checks state. No other working.

**Practice.** None; C12 is not among the book's quantitative concepts (item 10).

**Figures.** `pm-poshan-food-norms` plots the eight gram figures from the text, bars from zero,
ratios 1.5 hold. It matches the text.

**Currency.** PM POSHAN and Poshan 2.0 were both approved for 2021-22 to 2025-26; that period ended
31 Mar 2026. `must_know[6]` tells the reader to check, and PIB 2251769 (14 Apr 2026) describes
Poshan 2.0 as running "as on March 2026". Re-check for a 2026 continuation of either scheme and for
the revised supplementary-nutrition norms.

1. **gap** · `must_know[0]`, "The government says the Schedule II norms were revised in January 2023";
   illustration 1, "It says the norms in Schedule II 'have been revised in January 2023'" (from
   compression hole 54; confirmed).
   - The backgrounder's scope is supplementary nutrition at the anganwadi: "Supplementary Nutrition
     is provided to Children (6 months to 6 years), Pregnant Women, Lactating Mothers and Adolescent
     Girls in accordance with the nutrition norms contained in Schedule-II ... These norms have been
     revised in January 2023." It says nothing of the school rows (450 / 700). C12 says "the Schedule
     II norms" (all rows). C13 says "supplementary-nutrition norms" in one place and "the anganwadi
     take-home-ration norms in the Act's Schedule II" in another. That makes three scopes in two
     sections.
   - Legal basis, which the hole asked for: section 37(1) of the held Act lets the Central Government
     amend Schedule II by notification, laid before Parliament. No such notification is held, and the
     held India Code text of Schedule II still prints the 2013 figures. So the held texts do not show
     whether the January 2023 revision amended the Schedule or only the scheme's norms.
   - Fix (C12, and C13 to match word for word): "A government backgrounder of April 2026 says the
     supplementary-nutrition norms for anganwadi beneficiaries, which follow Schedule II, were revised
     in January 2023. It gives no new figures and does not say whether the school-meal rows changed.
     The Act lets the Central Government amend a Schedule by notification (section 37); no such
     notification is held."
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 7: one fact told two ways across sections; SELFCHECK 1).

2. **error** · `must_know[1]`, "Every norm in Schedule II and in the school-meal table is an amount to
   reach. None is a ceiling. When a meal is criticised for too much energy, sugar or fat, there is no
   legal limit it has broken."
   - This contradicts the section's own illustration 2, which quotes the 2022 anganwadi guidelines:
     "Only jaggery should be used for sweetening; white sugar should not be used." An anganwadi meal
     sweetened with white sugar breaks that rule. The guidelines are issued under the Act (sections
     4(a), 5(1)(a), 6 and 7). The point is true of Schedule II and the PM POSHAN norms table only.
   - Fix: "Every number in Schedule II and in the PM POSHAN norms table is an amount to reach, and none
     is a ceiling. The only rule on sugar in the documents held is the anganwadi guidelines' ban on
     white sugar, which limits the kind of sweetener, not the amount."
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 5: the record contradicts itself).

3. **gap** · `must_know[4]`, "Mothers, children under three and severely malnourished children get a
   take-home ration." (from compression hole 53; confirmed, with correction).
   - Two sources are merged without saying which is which. Schedule II's row says "Children (6 months
     to 6 years) who are malnourished", with no "severely". The 2022 guidelines (3.3, the quote in
     reference 11) say "SAM children" get THR, and their norms table says "Severely malnourished
     children". So "severely" is the guidelines' word, not the Act's. The point also leaves out
     adolescent girls aged 14 to 18, whom the same guidelines sentence includes (the retrieval item
     has them).
   - Fix: "Under the 2022 guidelines, pregnant women and lactating mothers, children from 6 to 36
     months, children with severe acute malnutrition, and adolescent girls aged 14 to 18 where
     covered get a take-home ration. Schedule II's row says only 'malnourished'."
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 1: a quote made to stand behind a merged claim).

4. **gap** · `definition.text` table rows "lower primary classes" / "upper primary classes"; Exercise 1,
   item 3, "A child in class VI" answered "on the upper primary norm" (from compression hole 51;
   confirmed).
   - No held text says which classes are lower and which upper primary. PIB 1812421 says only
     "children of classes I to VIII", and the NFSA says "up to class VIII". The Exercise 1 answer
     rests on an unstated split, and C13's problems do too.
   - Fix: obtain a source for the split (the Ministry of Education's scheme text, or the Right of
     Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009) and state "lower primary is classes I to V,
     upper primary VI to VIII" with it. Otherwise change item 3 to "a child in upper primary" and say
     the split is not in the documents held. Harsh to decide.
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 3 and 14).

5. **gap** · `definition.text`, "Section 5(1)(b) entitles every child up to class VIII, or aged six to
   fourteen, to one mid-day meal on every school day."
   - The section limits the meal to "all schools run by local bodies, Government and Government aided
     schools" (in reference 3's quote). "Every child" over-reaches: a child in a private unaided
     school has no entitlement under it.
   - Fix: "... to one mid-day meal on every school day, in schools run by local bodies or the
     government, or aided by it."
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 4: beyond what the instrument says).

6. **gap** · `must_know[3]`, "A take-home ration is a prepared food, not grain. The 2022 guidelines
   forbid raw rice, wheat or dal as a take-home ration." (compression hole H9, batch r3, raised against
   C14; the C12 half).
   - C14 then allocates grain to the programme (the Wheat Based Nutrition Programme), and the two read
     as a contradiction. The held guidelines resolve it: "Food ingredients used in Supplementary
     Nutrition shall comprise cereals, pulses, millets (coarse grains), nuts and some fat" (3.4), and
     THR "shall be prepared using locally available ... food ingredients" (3.6).
   - Fix: add to must_know[3], "Grain still goes in, as an ingredient of the prepared food (3.4)." C14's
     auditor carries the matching sentence there.
