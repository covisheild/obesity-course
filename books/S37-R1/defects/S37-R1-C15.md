1. **`definition.text`, `must_know[7]`, Exercise 1: the book's two words leave a case unnamed**
   (from compression hole r3-H11)
   - Confirmed. "Single-duty" is a change that "neither aims at nor checks the other"; "working
     against" is one that raises the other's risk. A change that acts on one burden and has been
     checked and passes do-no-harm has no name, and the text does not relate the WHO's three levels to
     the two words beyond "do no harm ... is the check that catches it". Nothing says what evidence
     shows that a change "raises the risk".
   - Fix: redefine single-duty as "acts on one burden and does not aim at the other" (checked or
     not), and add: "Do no harm is the check that sorts a single-duty change from one working against
     the other burden; retrofit and de novo produce double-duty actions. A change is shown to work
     against the other burden only by an outcome measured on that side." Hawkes 2020 is not held, so
     none of this may be attributed.
   - **gap**.
   - Fixer: definition, must_know[7] and Exercise 1(c) now define single-duty as "acts on one burden and does not aim at the other"; added the do-no-harm/retrofit/de-novo mapping and "shown to work against ... only by an outcome measured on that side"; illustration 2 now says "On its design, read it as working against". Nothing attributed to Hawkes.
   Verifier: closed
