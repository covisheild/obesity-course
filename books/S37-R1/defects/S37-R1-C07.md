1. **error** · `definition.text`, first words, "Its headline figure is the monthly per capita
   consumption expenditure (MPCE)" (from compression hole 1; confirmed).
   - "Its" has no antecedent: the definition never names the survey. "HCES" is used in the figure
     caption, Ex 1 and P4 and is never expanded in this section.
   - Fix: open the definition with two sentences: "India's Household Consumption Expenditure Survey
     (HCES) is run by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI). Its headline
     figure is ..." (splitting the original's 31-word sentence keeps the mean-length rule).
   - Recurring: yes (a pronoun whose referent was cut, S02-R1-C05 item 4; acronym not expanded,
     SELFCHECK 14).

2. **error** · `simplified_explanation`, "Two government surveys tell you most of what is known ...
   The second survey takes the food each household said it used"; `must_know[4]`, "Both surveys
   count what a household bought or used"; `definition.text` on Report 594 (from compression hole 3;
   confirmed).
   - Report 594 is not a second survey. Its title page reads "Report no. 594 (HCES: 2022-23 &
     2023-24)", and 2.9.6.0 converts "the quantities of food recorded as consumed by the household"
     in that survey. It is a second use of the same HCES data, so the two numbers are not
     independent confirmations of each other.
   - Fix: simplified: "One government survey, used two ways, tells you most of what is known ...
     The survey's second use takes the quantities of food each household recorded ..."; must_know[4]:
     "Both uses of the survey count what a household bought or used ..."; definition: "The National
     Sample Survey's Report 594, built on the same HCES rounds, converts ...".
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 1 and 4: a claim beyond what the source says).

3. **error** · `simplified_explanation`, "Across the same years its average hardly moved: about
   2,200 kilocalories a person a day." (from compression hole 2; confirmed).
   - The spending years in the section are 2011-12 and 2022-23; the calorie figures used are 2011-12
     and 2023-24 (Table 3.14). "The same years" is wrong.
   - Fix: "Between 2011-12 and 2023-24 its average hardly moved: about 2,200 kilocalories a person a
     day in rural India."
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 4a: a figure applied to a year it was not measured for).

4. **gap** · `must_know[5]`, "Before you set two rounds side by side, check that they measured the same
   way ... Report 594 used a conversion table from a committee set up in 2025. Ask which table each
   calorie figure came from."; illustration 1 and P12, which compare rural 2,233 (2011-12) with 2,212
   (2023-24).
   - The section asks the reader a question it does not answer for its own central comparison. The
     held Report 594 says the conversion table "used till 2011-12" was revised by a committee
     constituted on 29 January 2025 and that the new table "will be used for this report"; the held
     excerpt does not say whether Table 3.14's 2009-10 and 2011-12 columns were recomputed with the
     new table. The comparison may mix two tables. (3.3.0 does say 2009-10 and 2011-12 use Schedule
     Type 2, the MMRP basis, so the reference period matches.)
   - Fix: add to illustration 1, after the 2,233 / 2,212 sentence: "The report does not say, in the
     pages held here, whether its 2011-12 figure was worked out again with the 2025 conversion table,
     so treat 'almost no change' as approximate." Add the same caveat to P12's answer.
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 5: the record does what it says to check before doing).

5. **gap** · `must_know[5]`, "The surveys before 2009-10 used a different reference period for some
   foods." (from compression hole 4, residual; confirmed and closable from a held source).
   - The point says nothing about the rounds the section actually compares. Statement 2's note
     answers it: "for the years 2009-10, 2011-12 and 2022-23, these are based on Modified MRP
     (MMRP)" (already reference 16 of this record).
   - Fix: add "The factsheet puts 2009-10, 2011-12 and 2022-23 on the same basis, the modified mixed
     reference period (MMRP)." The held texts say nothing on other design changes in 2022-23, so say
     no more.

6. **error** · `illustrations[1].analogy_breaks_when`, "The 2023-24 shares come from a press release and
   a chart, grouped into eight food categories. The 2022-23 factsheet has fifteen."
   - Neither count is in a held source. The held PIB 2097601 is text only, with no chart, and names
     food groups only in its two share paragraphs. Statement 5 of the factsheet has ten food groups
     (21 groups in all); Statement 6 has thirteen. "Fifteen" appears only in the source file's own
     header note, which is not source text.
   - Fix: "The 2023-24 shares come from a press release that names only a few food groups. Only
     groups named the same way in both, such as these two, can be compared."
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 1: a claim with no words behind it).

7. **gap** · `must_know[2]`, "Beverages, refreshments and processed food is the largest food group in
   Indian household spending." (from compression hole 5; confirmed).
   - No year and no comparison figure, so "largest" cannot be checked.
   - Fix: "In 2023-24 beverages, refreshments and processed food was the largest food group in both
     rural and urban spending: 9.84 per cent of rural MPCE, followed by milk and milk products at 8.44
     (PIB, 30 January 2025)." The 8.44 is in the paragraph already quoted in reference 8's source
     ("followed by milk & milk products (8.44%)"); extend reference 8's quote to carry it.
   - Recurring: yes (a figure given without its year; this book's brief).

8. **gap** · `must_know[0]`, "Cereals' share of rural MPCE halved between 2011-12 and 2022-23 while the
   rupees spent on them rose." (from compression hole 6; confirmed).
   - The rupees are nominal (factsheet: "expressed as nominal values"), and no price index is held,
     so a real rise cannot be claimed. The point says "rose" bare, while illustration 1 qualifies it.
   - Fix: "... while the rupees spent on them rose, in each year's prices." Do not add a price
     index; none is held.
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 4).

9. **gap** · `must_know[2]`, "It includes purchased cooked meals." (from compression hole 7;
   confirmed).
   - Ex 2, P11 and the "junk food" framing need to know how much of the group is packaged snacks or
     drinks. Neither held source gives any split within the group.
   - Fix: add "Neither the factsheet nor the 2023-24 release splits the group further, so no share
     of it can be called snacks or sweet drinks."

10. **style** · P7 [6] answer, "The report itself calls this kind of change slight."
    - Report 594's "slight increase" (3.3.1.1) is about 2009-10 to 2023-24 (urban 2,123 to 2,240,
      117 kcal), not the 34 kcal the problem computes.
    - Fix: "The report calls the change from 2009-10 to 2023-24 slight; this one is smaller still."

11. **style** · figure `s37-r1-c07-rural-shares.png`.
    - The x axis spaces 2011-12, 2022-23 and 2023-24 evenly, so an eleven-year gap and a one-year gap
      look the same. Book 0 F2 teaches this as a way a figure misleads; illustration 2's point is
      exactly that the one-year move is small.
    - Fix: add to the caption "The first gap is eleven years, the second one year."
