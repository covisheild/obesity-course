## Part 2 — A tax on sugar-sweetened drinks, from the text alone

**The text never mentions a tax, sugar-sweetened drinks, or where any tax is levied.** Everything below is assembled from pieces, and I mark each inference.

- **Stage.** A sweetened drink is a packaged food, which C03 describes as several chains meeting at a manufacturer or brand owner. That is the processing stage of C01's eight. C04 says retail price is the farm-gate price plus everything added after it. A tax is one more addition, made by the state, at whichever stage it is charged. C02's mandi fees are the model in the text for a state charge levied at one stage. The text does not tell me whether such a tax would fall at processing, at retail sale, or anywhere else. **My inference:** it acts at processing or retail. It does not act at the farm: nothing in the text links it to sugarcane's FRP in C09.
- **Whose decision it changes.** The state's own decision comes first. F3 tells me the GST Council recommends GST rates. Whether a drinks tax would be GST is not stated. After that, the decision changed is the manufacturer's or brand owner's: what price to set, how much sugar goes in (which moves sugar's place in the 5(2)(b) ingredient order), and pack size. Then the retailer's: whether to stock it, what mark-up to take, and where to place it. In C05's terms, it changes **economic access (affordability)**, which is set "before the consumer arrives". The consumer's pick comes last, from a shelf whose prices others have changed. So the tax acts on the consumer only through those upstream decisions.
- **What I cannot say from the text.** How much of the tax reaches the shelf price. C04 shows that each actor's mark-up covers costs and margin, but nothing says how actors pass a new charge on. How much drinking changes: C05 says "Do not supply the number yourself". ## Part 3 — What this changes in what I do, say, accept or refuse

1. When someone says "the food system", I ask which of the eight stages they mean. I refuse to let it mean farming.
2. I ask what line was drawn around any "food system" figure: household waste in or out, imports in or out.
3. I quote "food system" as HLPE's 2014 definition, with the year.
4. I keep loss (before retail, excluding retail) apart from waste (retail, food service, household). I refuse a single "lost and wasted" number without asking which it is.
5. I correct anyone who quotes 55 kg per person per year as a measurement. It is UNEP's medium-confidence estimate, and it includes peels and bones.
6. I never add NABCONS's farm-operations and market-level percentages into a total the study itself does not give.
7. In a chain, I count the hands and the charges. Mandi fees and commission are paid again at a secondary mandi.
8. I do not read the first ingredient as "most of the product". I add up sugars that appear under separate names before ranking them.
9. I do not read a missing country of origin as meaning Indian. I do not read the address on a pack as the maker's.
10. For every percentage mark-up, I ask for its base. The same rupees give a larger percentage on buying price.
11. I refuse "farmers get a third, so middlemen pocket two-thirds". The remainder pays for costs, not only margins.
12. I quote a farmer's share only with its food, route and period, and I check who counted as "farmer".
13. When an argument blames the eater, I name the four food-environment elements and who set each. I say that advice reaches only the last decision.
14. I treat a patient's report of what is sold nearby, and at what price, as evidence.
15. I write out the supply identity before arguing. A rise in stocks is subtracted and a fall is added.
16. I refuse to treat availability (568.8 g a day) as intake, or a national figure as a state or family figure.
17. I do not add IDR and SSR to get 100 unless exports are zero.
18. I ask "self-sufficient in which food?" Edible oil is 56 per cent imported.
19. I say "points" or "per cent" every time. A falling budget share is not a falling amount eaten.
20. I do not quote "beverages, refreshments and processed food" as spending on junk or packaged snacks.
21. I know the headline HCES shares leave out free grain.
22. I do not accuse Indian food policy of "ignoring nutrition". It was built against a grain shortage and succeeded on its own measure. Asking it to do nutrition work is a change of job, to be argued as a change.
23. I do not judge the PDS ration as a whole diet. The Department calls it supplemental.
24. I do not treat MSP as what farmers get. I ask how much was bought at it.
25. I do not list sugarcane among crops the government buys.
26. A change to the ration basket is also a change to procurement.
27. I refuse "cereal exports prove people are well fed".

### (2) "Add pulses and edible oil to the PDS ration"

This needs a split verdict, component by component. C17 says to locate each component separately.

**Edible oil: working against the other burden**, by the book's own test. Oil is counted as fat at 37 kJ a gram. It adds energy and, as far as the book tells me, nothing else. Overweight among women rose from 20.6 to 24.0 per cent between NFHS-4 and NFHS-5. C15: "A change that adds energy and nothing else, in a population where overweight is rising, fails that check." It would help only if it replaced oil the household already buys. The book gives no evidence on substitution.

**Pulses: potentially double-duty, as a retrofit, but only if designed that way.** Tur dal has 21.7 g protein per 100 g against rice's 7.94. So pulses raise protein, which acts on undernutrition. If they are added on top of the cereal, they also add energy (13.84 kJ/g). Added on top, they are at best single-duty and may fail do-no-harm. If they replace part of the cereal at equal energy, as in C15 exercise 1(b), they pass do-no-harm and act on undernutrition.

**Other points, from the text:**

## Part 3. What this changes in what I do, say, accept or refuse

- **Duty cuts.** I will refuse "duty halved, so oil X per cent cheaper". A cut of 5 percentage points in the duty moves the landed price by about 4.5 per cent, and says nothing about the shop price. I will ask whether a duty is basic or effective, and ask for its date.
- **MSP margins.** "50 per cent MSP margin" is over cost, including imputed family labour. It is a third of the price and not profit. I will not subtract a paddy MSP from a rice economic cost.
- **Cost of free rice.** Free ration rice costs the Union about Rs 41.73 a kg (2025-26 budget estimate), all of it subsidy. A higher MSP raises the subsidy, not what the household pays.
- **What is a legal right.** The grain quantity is a right under the NFSA, section 3(1). The zero price is a Cabinet decision to the end of 2028. The Act's grain is cereal only. I will always say which household type I mean when I quote an entitlement.
- **Ration energy.** I will quote a ration's energy as energy handed over, not intake. I will always state the month length, household size, table and factor (4.18 or 4.184), and whose requirement I divided by.
- **Norms are floors.** Schedule II norms set no maximum, so "too much" breaks no law. The anganwadi norms were revised in January 2023. I will not quote 500 or 600 kcal without saying so.
- **The state decides menus.** What the state buys and serves is nutrition policy. I will quote the state's own "calorie sufficiency, neglecting quality and diversity". I will not add up beneficiaries across schemes. Allocated is not lifted, and lifted is not eaten.
- **Both burdens.** I will say both burdens in the same breath. I will not call 24.0 per cent "the obesity rate". I will ask do-no-harm out loud about any energy-adding change.
- **Checking a policy tool.** For any tool I will ask: where does it act, who runs it, what does it rest on, and what does it say it is for. I will check whether a scheme has lapsed; PM POSHAN and Poshan 2.0 were approved to 2025-26.
- **Locating a proposal.** For any proposal I will name the stage, the actor, and what it changes on offer. In clinic, I will ask what the child is served before advising the parent.
- **Reading a label.** I will not read the absence of an origin on the label as "Indian". I will treat "Marketed by" as the brand owner, not the maker. I will mark every link as checked, seen or unknown, and leave the unknowns in.
