# S37-R1-C12 · School meals and anganwadi food

**Definition.** The National Food Security Act, 2013 gives meals as well as grain, each free of charge and each
"so as to meet the nutritional standards specified in Schedule II".

Section 4(a) entitles every pregnant woman and lactating mother to a meal during pregnancy and
for six months after the birth, through the local anganwadi. Section 5(1)(a) entitles every
child from six months to six years to an age-appropriate meal through the anganwadi. Section
5(1)(b) entitles every child up to class VIII, or aged six to fourteen, to one mid-day meal on
every school day. That meal is served in schools run by local bodies, by government, or with
government aid. Section 6 adds meals for children who suffer from malnutrition.

An anganwadi is
a child care and development centre set up under the Integrated Child Development Services
Scheme (section 2(1)). Section 7 has the States run these schemes under Central guidelines,
"including cost sharing".

Schedule II sets the standard for each group as energy in kilocalories and protein in grams.

```table
| group | type of meal | energy (kcal) | protein (g) |
| children, 6 months to 3 years | take-home ration | 500 | 12-15 |
| children, 3 to 6 years | morning snack and hot cooked meal | 500 | 12-15 |
| children, 6 months to 6 years, who are malnourished | take-home ration | 800 | 20-25 |
| lower primary classes | hot cooked meal | 450 | 12 |
| upper primary classes | hot cooked meal | 700 | 20 |
| pregnant women and lactating mothers | take-home ration | 600 | 18-20 |
```

Two Union schemes deliver meals of these kinds. Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman (PM POSHAN),
earlier the Mid-Day Meal Scheme, is run by the Ministry of Education. It gives one hot cooked
meal in government and government-aided schools, to classes I to VIII and to the pre-school
class, Bal Vatika. Its energy and protein norms are Schedule II's two school rows. It was
approved for 2021-22 to 2025-26.

Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0 is
run by the Ministry of Women and Child Development. Its guidelines of 2022 issue under sections
4(a), 5(1)(a), 6 and 7 of the Act. It provides supplementary nutrition at anganwadi centres, as
a take-home ration (THR) or as a hot cooked meal (HCM) with a morning snack.

**In plain terms.** The food law of 2013 promises meals as well as grain, all free.

A pregnant woman gets a meal through her local anganwadi. So does a mother for six months after
the birth. An anganwadi is a government child care centre. Every child
from six months to six years gets a meal there too. Every child in classes I to VIII of a
government or government-aided school gets a mid-day meal on each school day.

Two Union schemes deliver these. PM POSHAN runs the school meal. Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0
runs the anganwadi food. Some groups get a hot cooked meal (HCM) at the centre. Others get a
take-home ration (THR): a prepared food handed out to be eaten at home.

The Act sets each meal's size in two numbers only: energy, in kilocalories, and protein, in grams.
Each is an amount the meal must reach. Nothing in the Act sets an upper limit on energy, sugar,
fat or salt. That is how a law written against hunger looks.

**Must know points for you.**

- "Anganwadi children get 500 kilocalories" is the old norm. The government says the Schedule II norms were revised in January 2023, and the revised figures are not in the documents this book holds. Quote 500 only with its date and that sentence, and look for the revised norms first.
- Every norm in Schedule II and in the school-meal table is an amount to reach. None is a ceiling. When a meal is criticised for too much energy, sugar or fat, there is no legal limit it has broken. The fix has to be a new norm, not enforcement of an old one.
- The anganwadi guidelines say their own programme was "driven by calorie intake rather than on balanced diets". They say supplementary nutrition is designed to "bridge the gap" between the recommended allowance and what people actually eat each day. In committee, quote the scheme's own words before you argue that it was built for a deficit.
- A take-home ration is a prepared food, not grain. The 2022 guidelines forbid raw rice, wheat or dal as a take-home ration. So it is a product made or bought by the State, with a recipe and a supplier. That makes its recipe and its supplier a lever on what young children and mothers eat.
- Mothers, children under three and severely malnourished children get a take-home ration. Children from three to six get a morning snack and a hot cooked meal at the centre. Schoolchildren get a hot cooked meal. When a mother tells you what her child eats at the anganwadi, ask which of the two it is.
- Centre and States share the anganwadi food bill. The 2022 guidelines set the Centre's share of supplementary nutrition at 50 per cent in States with a legislature. It is 90 per cent in the north-eastern and Himalayan States, and all of it in Union territories without a legislature. No document held gives PM POSHAN's split. Do not guess it.
- Both schemes were approved for 2021-22 to 2025-26. The legal floor is the Act, which has no end date; the schemes that deliver it do. Before you describe either scheme as it is today, find the current approval.
