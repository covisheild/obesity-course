# S37-R1-C16 · The main Indian food policy tools, on one map

**Definition.** A food policy tool is any means by which a government changes what is grown, stored, traded,
processed, sold, served or eaten. Some tools rest on a statute. Others rest on a government
decision to run a scheme, or to set a price or a duty until the next change.

India's main food policy tools, placed at the stage of the food system where each acts, as the
texts held for this book state them on 24 September 2026:

```table
| Stage | Tool | Run by | Rests on |
| Production | Minimum support price (MSP) for 22 mandated crops | Union Government, on the recommendation of the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP) | a decision announced each season |
| Production | Procurement at MSP | Food Corporation of India (FCI) and State agencies for cereals; two national cooperative federations for pulses and oilseeds | the same decisions, and a price support scheme for pulses and oilseeds |
| Production | Crop missions: oilseeds, oil palm, millets | Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare, with the States | schemes approved by the Union Government |
| Storage | Buying, holding and moving the central pool of grain, and its buffer stock | FCI | an Act of 1964, under which FCI was set up in 1965 |
| Storage and trade inside India | Limits on how much of a grain traders may hold (wheat, May 2025 to April 2026) | Government of India | an order; the release announcing it names no Act |
| Trade across the border | Import duty; export restriction | Government of India | a duty change; its customs notification is not held here |
| Processing and retail | Food standards and labels | Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) | Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 |
| Provision | Rations through the public distribution system (PDS) | Department of Food and Public Distribution, with the States | National Food Security Act, 2013 (NFSA) |
| Provision | School meals, Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman (PM POSHAN) | Ministry of Education, with the States | a centrally sponsored scheme, 2021-22 to 2025-26 |
| Provision | Anganwadi food, Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0 | Ministry of Women and Child Development, with the States | a scheme for 2021-22 to 2025-26 |
| Tax | Goods and services tax (GST) rates | recommended by the GST Council | the Constitution, Article 279A |
```

The two federations are the National Agricultural Cooperative Marketing Federation of India and
the National Cooperative Consumers' Federation of India. They buy under the Pradhan Mantri
Annadata Aay Sanrakshan Abhiyan. An export restriction is the other trade tool. No order imposing
one is among this book's sources, so none is described.

The Essential Commodities Act, 1955 appears in this book's sources only through other instruments.
The NFSA defines a fair price shop, the ration shop, as one licensed by an order under section 3
of that Act. It also names the Public Distribution System (Control) Order, 2001 as made under
it. The Act's own text is not among this book's sources, so what its section 3 empowers is not
described here.

Each tool's own text states what it is for. Read that way, the production, storage and trade
tools state aims about supply, prices and farm incomes. The provision tools state nutrition
aims, and where they name a problem it is hunger or undernutrition. In this book's sources, obesity
is named only as a theme of two Poshan 2.0 awareness campaigns in 2025.

**In plain terms.** The earlier sections of this book took the state's food tools one at a time. Here they sit on one
page, so you can see which stage of the food system each one touches.

First, a word. Book 0 used *instrument* for a legal text: a statute, a rule, a regulation or a
notification (`B0-R0-C43`). People who write about policy use the word more loosely, for any
tool a government uses — a price, a purchase, a duty, a meal. This book calls those *policy
tools*. It keeps *instrument* for the legal text a tool stands on. That matters, because a tool
with no statute behind it can end when its scheme period ends.

Walk the chain from the field. At the farm, the government announces a support price for 22
crops and buys some of them at that price (`S37-R1-C09`). Missions pay for seed and planting of
oilseeds, oil palm and millets.

In storage, the Food Corporation of India buys, holds and moves grain. In 2025 the Government
also limited how much wheat traders could hold. At the border, a duty makes imported oil
dearer or cheaper. At the factory and the shop, the food safety regulator sets standards and
decides what a label must say.

At the plate, the state itself hands out rations, school meals and anganwadi food (`S37-R1-C11`, `S37-R1-C12`). And
across all of it sits tax.

For each tool, ask four things. Where in the chain does it act? Who runs it? What does it stand
on, a statute or a scheme with an end date? What does its own text say it is for?

Ask the last question and a pattern shows. The tools on the farm, in storage and at the border
say they are for supply, prices and farmers' incomes. The tools at the plate say they are for
nutrition, and where they name a problem it is hunger or undernutrition. That fits the history in
`S37-R1-C08`. Obesity turns up in these texts only as a theme of two awareness campaigns in 2025.

**Must know points for you.**

- "India has no nutrition policy in agriculture" and "India's food policy is nutrition policy" are both wrong as stated. Read each tool's own purpose. The price, storage and trade tools state aims about supply, prices and farm incomes. The provision tools state nutrition aims, and where they name a problem it is hunger or undernutrition, not obesity.
- For any food policy tool, write four things before you argue about it. The stage it acts at. The body that runs it. What it stands on: a statute, or a scheme approved for a period. And its stated aim, copied from its own text with the date.
- Never repeat a duty, a price or a limit without its date. A Cabinet release of 3 October 2024 put the duty on edible oils at 20%. The basic customs duty on crude palm oil was 10% from June 2025, and 5% from the release of 24 September 2026. All three texts are still online.
- "Import duty" can mean the basic customs duty, or what a text calls the effective duty. They are different numbers for the same oil. Say which one you mean, and check which one your source gives.
- The same food is touched by tools that pull different ways, run by different bodies. The oilseeds mission wants more oil grown at home. The 2026 duty cut wants cheaper oil in the shop. Before you propose a change, name which body owns the tool you want changed.
- Check what each tool stands on before relying on it. The food safety law and the food security Act are statutes. PM POSHAN and Poshan 2.0 were approved as schemes for 2021-22 to 2025-26. Whether and how they continue after March 2026 is not in this book's sources, so look for the continuation before you cite either as current.
- Do not name the Act behind a stock limit from memory. The 2025 wheat stock limits were imposed by the Government of India, and the release announcing them names no Act. Find the order itself and read what it says it is made under, or say that you have not seen it.
- The map tells you where a tool acts, who runs it and what it says it is for. It does not tell you what the tool does to what people eat. A price tool with no nutrition aim can still change what people eat. That is a separate question, and it needs evidence.
