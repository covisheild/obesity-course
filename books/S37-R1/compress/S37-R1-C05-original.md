# S37-R1-C05 · Who decides what is available, at what price, in what place

**Definition.** The food environment is the part of the food system where a person meets it. The
definition used here is that of the High Level Panel of Experts on Food Security and
Nutrition (HLPE). The food environment is the physical, economic, political and
socio-cultural context in which consumers engage with the food system to make their
decisions about acquiring, preparing and consuming food. It names
four key elements that influence food choice: availability and physical access
(proximity); economic access (affordability); promotion, advertising and information; and
food quality and safety.

Each element is set, before the consumer arrives, by decisions taken by actors further up
the supply chain and by the state. The HLPE states that the decisions of actors at any
stage influence the types of food available and accessible. It also states that consumer
behaviour is shaped by the existing food environment as well as by personal preference.

In the Indian chains the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) studied in 2024, these decisions are
observable. State Agricultural Produce Marketing Committees (APMCs) fix the fees and
commission charges at the first sale. Processors and aggregators contract for crops before
harvest. Integrator companies market the birds of most broiler farmers. Processors spend on brand
promotion. Retailers set the final mark-up. The consumer chooses among the options these
decisions leave.

This is an observed feature of how the chains are organised, not a derivation. It would be
overturned by evidence that what people buy does not depend on what is stocked, priced,
promoted and sold near them.

**In plain terms.** Stand at a vegetable cart or a kirana shelf and look at what is there. Somebody decided what
would be on it before you walked up.

A farmer decided what to plant. A company may have signed for the crop before it was even
sown. A trader decided which grade of onion went to which city. A state market committee set
the fee on the first sale. A processor decided the recipe, the pack size and how much to
spend on the brand. A shopkeeper decided what to stock, at what mark-up, and what to put at
eye level.

The place where all these decisions meet you is called the food environment. It has four
parts that affect what you pick. What is there, and how near? What does it cost? How is it
promoted? What quality is it?

You still choose. But you choose from a shelf that others filled. So when you want to know
why people eat what they eat, ask about the shelf as well as the person.

**Must know points for you.**

- "People eat what they choose" leaves out half of it. People choose among what is there, at the price it is, promoted as it is, in the place it is sold. Others decided each of those first. The HLPE's own wording is that consumer behaviour is "also shaped by the existing food environment". Say both halves when you explain a diet.
- For any food on a plate, ask four questions in order. What was there, and how near? What did it cost? How was it promoted? What quality was it? Then name who decided each. A question you cannot answer is a gap in the evidence, not a gap in the person.
- Advice reaches only the last decision: the pick from the shelf. It does not change what was stocked, what it cost, how it was sold or where. When a plan relies on advice alone, name the decisions it leaves alone. The World Cancer Research Fund keeps a list of ten policy areas called NOURISHING. It files advice and counselling in health care under behaviour change, apart from the food environment.
- In clinic, treat a patient's account of what is sold near home, and at what price, as evidence about their food environment. Do not talk past it. It tells you which advice they can act on.
- In India a state body sets part of the price at the first sale. APMCs fix mandi fees and commission charges, and on the RBI's onion route to Delhi these were 1 and 4 per cent. The state is also a seller in its own right, through school, hospital and public canteens.
- Some choices are made before planting. Processors contract potato farmers, pre-harvest contractors fix mango prices before harvest, and around 70 per cent of broiler farmers sell through integrator companies. A policy aimed at farmers' crop choices has to deal with whoever signed the contract.
- This method names who decides. It does not measure how much any one decision changes what people eat. When someone claims a shelf rule or a price change will cut intake by a stated amount, ask for the study that measured it. Do not supply the number yourself.

**Exercise 1** (critique). A district obesity plan contains one line on food.

> Obesity will be tackled through awareness camps that teach people to choose healthy
> foods.

Using the four elements of the food environment, find what the line assumes and say what
it leaves out. Name one actor, other than the person, that the plan could have addressed
for each element.

**Exercise 2** (teaching). A first-year resident has ten minutes and a whiteboard. They ask why the department keeps
talking about the food environment when the patient is the one eating. Explain it with a
drawing, and end on what the idea does not tell you.
