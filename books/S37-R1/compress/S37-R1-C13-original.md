# S37-R1-C13 · What a ration, a meal or a take-home ration provides

**Definition.** A food entitlement is stated as a mass of food, over a period, for a unit of people. Under
section 3(1) of the National Food Security Act, 2013 (NFSA) a person in a priority household
is entitled to five kilograms of foodgrains a month. An Antyodaya household is entitled to
thirty-five kilograms a month for the whole household. A school meal under PM POSHAN (Pradhan
Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman) is set in
grams of each food per child per day. What an entitlement provides is the energy and protein
in that food, per person per day.

Getting from one to the other is a chain of unit conversions. First put the entitlement into
grams per person per day. Divide a monthly amount by the number of days you take the month to
have. Divide a household amount by the number of people in the household. Then multiply the
grams by the food's energy and protein per 100 grams, and divide by 100. For a meal of several
foods, work out each one and add them.

The contents per 100 grams come from a food composition table. India's is the Indian Food
Composition Tables 2017 (IFCT) of the Indian Council of Medical Research's National Institute
of Nutrition (ICMR-NIN). Its values are per 100 g of the edible portion. It gives raw milled
rice 7.94 g of protein and 1491 kJ of energy per 100 g. IFCT prints energy in kilojoules only.
The figures are physiologically available energy, worked out by the Atwater system. IFCT
converts them to kilocalories with 1 kcal = 4.18 kJ.

The result can be set against a norm or a requirement as a share: the energy provided divided
by the energy in the norm. Schedule II of the NFSA sets a lower primary hot cooked meal at 450
kcal and 12 g of protein. ICMR-NIN's 2020 adult requirement is 1660 kcal a day for a sedentary
reference woman of 55 kg and 2110 kcal for a sedentary reference man of 65 kg.

The arithmetic gives the energy in the food handed over. It does not give the energy any
person eats. That depends on who in the household eats the ration, on cooking and waste, and
on what the ration replaces in the rest of the diet.

**In plain terms.** A ration is written as a weight of food. Five kilograms of grain a month, for each person in a
priority household. A hundred grams of grain a day, for each child at a primary school. What
you usually want to know is how much energy and protein that is, for one person, on one day.

You get there in two moves. First, turn the entitlement into grams per person per day. Divide by
the days in the month. If the amount is for a household, divide by the people in it too. Second, look the food up in India's food composition tables. They say how much
energy and protein 100 grams of it holds. Multiply your grams by that, and divide by 100.

One trap sits in the tables. They give energy in kilojoules, not kilocalories. The tables
themselves say to divide by 4.18 to get kilocalories. Book 0, B4, taught you 4.184. When you
convert a number from these tables, use the table's own 4.18 and say that you did.

The answer is the energy in the food handed over. It is not what anybody ate. The grain is
shared at home, cooked, sometimes wasted, and eaten alongside everything else.

**Figure.** A primary school meal built to the PM POSHAN food norms, in kilojoules. The 100 g of raw milled rice gives 1,491, the 20 g of tur dal 276.8, and the 5 g of oil at most 185. The 50 g of vegetables is not counted. The total, 1,952.8, is already above the norm of 450 kcal, which is 1,881 kJ. About three quarters of it is the rice.

**Must know points for you.**

- The Indian Food Composition Tables give energy in kilojoules only. Read 1491 for raw rice as kilocalories and every answer comes out 4.18 times too big. A month's ration then seems to feed a grown man completely. Read the unit over the column before you read the number under it.
- The priority-household entitlement is five kilograms of grain a month. As raw milled rice, it holds about 595 kilocalories and 13 g of protein a day for the person it is issued to. That uses the 2017 tables' average and a 30-day month. Quote it with both.
- Convert a table's kilojoules with the factor the table used. The Indian tables use 4.18, not Book 0's 4.184. Say which one you used, and never use both in one calculation, or a difference in the fourth digit will look like a finding.
- An Antyodaya entitlement is 35 kg for the whole household. What it gives one person depends on how many people share it, which the Act does not fix. Never quote a per-person figure for it without the household size you assumed.
- "The ration supplies a third of a person's energy" compares the ration with a reference 55 kg sedentary woman. It is not a share of any real household member's need. A child's need is smaller and a labourer's is larger. So say whose requirement you divided by, or do not give the share.
- This arithmetic gives the energy in the food handed over, and stops there. It cannot say who in the household eats the ration. It cannot see cooking losses and waste, or what the ration pushes out of the rest of the diet. The Department of Food and Public Distribution itself calls the ration supplemental. So never present a ration's energy as a person's intake.
- A primary school meal built to the PM POSHAN food norms meets the 450 kcal and 12 g norms on paper. About three quarters of its energy is cereal. The norms fix the amounts. They do not fix where the energy comes from. That is the opening for anyone who wants to change the meal without changing its energy.
- The anganwadi take-home-ration norms in the Act's Schedule II were revised in January 2023, and the revised figures are not quoted here. Any THR sum built on 500 or 600 kilocalories uses the older norm, and must say so.

**Exercise 1** (teaching). A health secretary has one page and asks you what the free PDS ration gives a person in a
priority household, in energy and protein. Answer first. Then say what the figure does not
tell her, in a way she can repeat in a meeting.

**1.** A food holds 1,400 kJ in every 100 g. How many kilojoules are in 250 g of it? And in 35 g?

**2.** Using 1 kcal = 4.18 kJ, turn 2,090 kJ into kilocalories. Then turn 450 kcal into kilojoules.

**3.** A household receives 7 kg of grain a month. Taking a month as 30 days, how many grams is that a
day? If four people share it equally, how many grams a day is that for each?

**4.** A priority household member takes the 5 kg a month entitlement as whole wheat. The Indian Food
Composition Tables 2017 give whole wheat 1347 kJ and 10.59 g of protein per 100 g. Taking a
month as 30 days, how much energy, in kilocalories, and how much protein does the entitlement
hold a day? Use the tables' own factor.

**5.** The PM POSHAN food norms give an upper primary child 150 g of foodgrains a day. They add 30 g of
pulses, 75 g of vegetables and 7.5 g of oil and fat. The nutrition norms are 700 kcal and 20 g
of protein.

Build the meal from three foods and leave the vegetables out. Raw milled rice has 1491 kJ and
7.94 g protein per 100 g. Tur dal has 1384 kJ and 21.70 g per 100 g. Count the oil as fat, at
37 kJ a gram. Does the meal meet each norm?

**6.** Build the same upper primary meal again, with whole wheat in place of the rice. Whole wheat has
1347 kJ and 10.59 g of protein per 100 g. Keep the rest: 30 g of tur dal, 7.5 g of oil counted
as fat, no vegetables. What happens to the energy and the protein?

**7.** Schedule II of the National Food Security Act, 2013 sets a take-home ration for pregnant women
and new mothers. It is 600 kcal a day. The Poshan 2.0 guidelines say the food is served on 25
days a month, on average. The ration is handed out twice a month. How many kilocalories should
each hand-out carry? How many kilojoules is that, with the Indian tables' factor? Say which
version of the norm you used.

**8.** An Antyodaya household takes its 35 kg a month entitlement as raw milled rice (1491 kJ per 100
g). Work out the energy per person per day, in kilocalories, for a household of seven and for a
household of three. The two household sizes are made up for the problem. Take a month as 30
days.

**9.** Here is a worked answer. Find the step that broke.

```working
entitlement: 5 kg of raw milled rice a month
rice: 1,491 kJ per 100 g
5 times 1,491 = 7,455 kJ a month
7,455 divided by 30 = 248.5 kJ a day
248.5 divided by 4.18 = 59.4 kcal a day
```

"So the ration gives under 60 kcal a day, which is next to nothing."

**10.** Here is a worked answer. Find the step that broke.

```working
Antyodaya entitlement: 35 kg of raw milled rice a month
35,000 divided by 30 = 1,166.7 g a day
1,166.7 times 1,491 divided by 100 = 17,395.5 kJ a day
17,395.5 divided by 4.18 = 4,161.6 kcal a day
```

"So each Antyodaya beneficiary gets about 4,160 kcal a day, nearly twice a sedentary man's
requirement of 2,110. The ration is too generous."

**11.** A briefing note says this.

> The free PDS ration meets more than a third of every beneficiary's daily energy needs.

Decide what to compute, compute it, and say what your answer does not establish.

**12.** In a meeting somebody says this.

> If the PDS gave bajra instead of rice, poor households would get fewer calories, and that
> would help with obesity.

Bajra is 1456 kJ and 10.96 g of protein per 100 g in the Indian Food Composition Tables 2017.
Decide what to compute, compute it, and say what your answer does not establish.
