# S37-R1-C14 · The state as a food provider

**Definition.** The Indian state does not only regulate food. Through three channels it also buys, stores,
cooks and hands out food, to a large share of the population.

The first is the public distribution system (PDS) under the National Food Security Act, 2013
(NFSA). On 29 November 2023 the Union Cabinet decided to give free foodgrains to about 81.35
crore (813.5 million) beneficiaries under the Pradhan Mantri Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana (PMGKAY) for five years
from 1 January 2024. The grain goes out through fair price shops, of which the Department of
Food and Public Distribution (DFPD) counts 5.51 lakh (551,000). For 2025-26 the DFPD's annual report
tabulates an allocation of 554.11 lakh tonnes (55.411 million tonnes) of foodgrains under the
targeted public distribution system (TPDS). Once other welfare schemes are added, the table
comes to 607.40 lakh tonnes in all. The same report's text gives 595.05 lakh tonnes for the
whole; its own table adds to 607.40.

The second is PM POSHAN (Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman), the school meal scheme earlier
called the mid-day meal. It provides
one hot cooked meal to children in government and government-aided schools, approved for
2021-22 to 2025-26. A Press Information Bureau (PIB) release of 1 April 2022 gives 11.80 crore (118 million) children of classes I to
VIII in 11.20 lakh (1.12 million) schools. The DFPD allocated it 22.31 lakh tonnes of foodgrains for
2025-26.

The third is supplementary nutrition at anganwadi centres, under Mission Saksham Anganwadi
and Poshan 2.0. As on March 2026 the mission's tracking system followed nearly 14,03,170
(about 1.4 million) anganwadi centres and about 8,95,29,425 (about 89.5 million) eligible
beneficiaries. The DFPD's grain for it runs
through the Wheat Based Nutrition Programme (WBNP), 23.05 lakh tonnes for 2025-26.

In each channel the state decides which foods go in: which grain, which recipe, what may and
may not be added. Each such decision changes what very many people eat. So what the state
buys and serves is nutrition policy, whether or not it is called that. Hostels, public
hospitals and government canteens are the same lever. The DFPD allocates grain to welfare
institutions and hostels, 5.83 lakh tonnes for 2025-26. Meals in hospitals and canteens are
taken up at rung 2.

**In plain terms.** The earlier sections showed what each scheme promises one person: a ration, a school meal, an
anganwadi meal or packet. This one steps back and asks how big the whole thing is.

It is very big. The ration reaches about 81 crore people, that is about 810 million. The school
meal reaches crores of children and the anganwadi programme crores more. The Union government
moves tens of millions of tonnes of grain a year to feed these schemes.

That makes the state one of the largest buyers, cooks and grocers in the country. It decides
which grain goes into the ration. It writes the rules for what an anganwadi may cook, down to
whether it may use white sugar. Each of those choices changes what millions of people eat.

So a decision about a scheme's menu is a decision about the country's diet. It is nutrition
policy even when nobody calls it that. The same is true of the meals the state serves in
hostels, hospitals and canteens, which rung 2 takes up.

**Must know points for you.**

- "The state regulates food; people choose what to eat" misses most of Indian food policy. The state is also a buyer, cook and grocer, for the ration's 81.35 crore beneficiaries alone. A change to what a scheme hands out changes what millions eat. Treat it in a committee as a diet decision, not an administrative one.
- Never add the beneficiaries of the ration, the school meal and the anganwadi to get "people the state feeds". The same child can sit in all three counts, and the counts come from different years. Measure scale in grain from one table for one year, or give each count separately with its date.
- For 2025-26 the Union government allocated 607.40 lakh tonnes of foodgrains, about 61 million tonnes, by its Food Department's own table. The report's text says 595.05. When text and table disagree, quote the table and say which figure you used.
- As on March 2026 the anganwadi programme tracked about 14 lakh centres and 8.95 crore eligible beneficiaries. A single line in its guidelines, such as "white sugar should not be used", reaches all of them. Read scheme guidelines as menus, because that is what they are.
- The anganwadi guidelines say in their own words that the earlier programme focused on "calorie sufficiency, neglecting quality and diversity of diets". Quote the state's own sentence when you argue that its food schemes were built for hunger. It carries more weight in a meeting than any outside critic.
- Allocated is not lifted, and lifted is not eaten. PM POSHAN was allocated 22.31 lakh tonnes for 2025-26 and had lifted 15.68 by December 2025. So grain figures tell you what the state moved, never what anyone ate. Do not cite them as intake.
- A guideline is not a plate. The Union writes the norms and the States run the kitchens. Before you claim a scheme serves millets or avoids sugar, ask for evidence of what was served, not what was written.
