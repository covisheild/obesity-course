1. **`illustration.body`, `simplified_explanation`, practice 6 and 10 answers, figure `lipid-fraction-line`, the "reconstruction" of the rule**
   - **Claim:** "Run the assumed fraction through the arithmetic once, to see the classic rule's
     number appear. 0.87 times 37 = 32.19 ... the arithmetic reproduces the paper's own statement of
     the rule."
   - **What the sources say:** Hall 2008 says only that the rule "can be traced back to a calculation
     that assumes exclusive loss of adipose tissue consisting of 87% fat (1,2)". It does not say
     which energy value for fat that calculation used. The match with 37 is an accident of rounding:
     - FAO footnote 9 (held, and taught in Book 0 E2) says 37 is "the commonly used rounded value"
       and that fat's precise value is 37.4 kJ/g. That gives 0.87 × 37.4 = 32.54.
     - Hall's own figure for body fat, which he says "is the same as the energy density of fat",
       is 39.5. That gives 0.87 × 39.5 = 34.37.
     - In kilocalories, 0.87 × 9,000 = 7,830, not the rule's 3,500 ÷ 0.4536 = 7,716 (compression
       hole 12 found this one).
     - The rule converted exactly is 32.28 MJ/kg, not Hall's printed 32.2.

     So "reproduces" and "to see the classic rule's number appear" state a derivation the source
     does not give. The only factor that lands on 32.2 is the one rounded down.
   - **Smallest fix:** keep the working. Replace the gloss with: "32.19 lands close to 32.2. That is
     partly luck: with FAO's precise 37.4 the same sum gives 32.5, and with Hall's 39.5 it gives 34.4.
     Hall does not say which figure for fat the original calculation used. What the sum shows is the
     size of the effect an 87% assumption has, not how the rule was built." Make the same change in
     the practice 6 and 10 answers and in the line figure's caption.
   - **Tag:** error. **Recurring** (SELFCHECK 1 and 4: a quote made to stand behind more than it says).
