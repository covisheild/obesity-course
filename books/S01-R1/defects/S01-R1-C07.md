# S01-R1-C07 · Sizing an imbalance, and the static rule — audit defects

Auditor: Task 3, 23 September 2026. Sources opened: `thomas_2013_3500kcal.txt`,
`hall_2011_lancet.txt`, `hall_2008_ijo.txt`, `nist_sp811_pound.txt`, `openstax_anatphys_2e.txt`.
Every existing `quote` was found verbatim in its mapped file and states the number it is cited for
(3500, 22, 1439, 4.535 923 7 E-01); no quote field needed changing. Every practice, exercise and
figure number was recomputed in Python (results below each item). "Recurring" = a kind already
found in `books/B0/defects/` and listed in `check/SELFCHECK.md` (item number given).

1. **error** (from compression hole 40) · `definition.text`. "converts into a change in fat mass
   at that fixed rate", then "3,500 kilocalories ... for each pound of body weight changed". Hall
   2008 (held) states the rule as "a cumulative energy deficit of 3500 kcal is required to lose 1
   pound of body weight", derived by assuming "exclusive loss of adipose tissue consisting of 87%
   fat". The rule is stated for weight and assumes all of it is fat tissue; the section switches
   between the two. Fix: after the pound sentence add "The rule is stated for body weight, and it
   assumes every kilogram of that weight is fat tissue." **recurring** (SELFCHECK 7).

2. **gap** (from compression hole 41, reclassified from error) · `definition.text` vs C02. The two
   origin stories are the same story, and the held sources say so: Hall 2011's "derived by
   estimation of the energy content of weight lost" carries its reference 7, which is Hall 2008;
   Hall 2008 says "The origin of this rule can be traced back to a calculation that assumes
   exclusive loss of adipose tissue consisting of 87% fat (1,2)", and its reference 2 is
   Wishnofsky 1958. Nothing is false, but the reader cannot see the link, and "It is not a fact
   anybody measured about fat" sits oddly beside "estimation". Fix: one sentence: "Wishnofsky's
   estimate is the calculation section 2 describes: tissue taken to be 87% fat, times fat's energy
   per gram." Add a `hall_2008_ijo` reference, locator "opening paragraph", quote `The origin of
   this rule can be traced back to a calculation that assumes exclusive loss of adipose tissue
   consisting`.

3. **gap** (from compression hole 42, reclassified from error) · `illustration.body`. "What they
   actually lost, measured, was 20.1 pounds", then a quote giving 7.4 and 27.6 (27.6 − 7.4 =
   20.2). The paper does state 20.1: "Subjects lost 20.1±11.3 lbs, 7.4±12.6 lb less than the
   27.6±16.0 lbs predicted by the 3500 kcal rule." The figure is right; the quote stops one clause
   short of it, and a mean of differences need not equal the difference of means. Fix: quote the
   whole sentence from "Subjects lost 20.1±11.3 lbs", and add "(the 0.1 is rounding: the paper
   averages each person's shortfall)".

4. **gap** (from conductor figure note F1, first half) · `illustration.body`. "About 26.6 pounds
   predicted, close to the 27.6 pounds the rule in fact predicted". 1439 × 64.8 / 3500 = 26.64;
   the paper's 27.6 is the mean of each person's own prediction, and deficit (±784 kcal/d) and
   duration (±23.6 d, range 31–93) both vary, so a product of averages is not the average of the
   products. Nothing tells the reader why the two differ. Fix: one sentence: "The paper worked the
   rule out person by person and averaged; multiplying two averages gives a slightly different
   number."

5. **error** (from conductor figure note F1, second half) · `figures[1]` `thomas-shortfall`. The
   measured point is drawn at 20.2 (`27.6 - 7.4`) while the prose and the paper say 20.1; the
   label "7.4 lb short of the paper's 27.6 lb" sits next to a line that ends at 26.64, so the
   visible gap is 26.64 − 20.2 = 6.44, not 7.4. The static point is blue and the measured point red,
   the same red as the static line, so the legend pairs them wrongly by eye. Fix: plot the measured
   point at 20.1 from the paper's sentence (item 3); either add the paper's 27.6 as its own point
   at day 64.8 or relabel "20.1 lb measured: 6.5 below this line, 7.4 below the paper's own
   27.6"; replace the check `y2[-1] = 27.6 - 7.4` with none, or with a `derived` entry. Give the
   static point and its line one colour. **recurring** (SELFCHECK 17, 18).

6. **error** (from conductor figure note F3) · `illustration.body`, table, `figures[0]`
   `static-vs-dynamic`, caption, `retrieval_items[2]`. Prose: "a bodyweight plateau at about 75
   kilograms, reached over about three years". Hall 2011: "a bodyweight plateau at about 75 kg ...
   over a 10-year simulation taking roughly 1 year to reach half of the maximum weight loss and
   reaching 95% of this value after about 3 years". At year 3 the model is at 100 − 0.95 × 25 =
   76.25 kg, not 75; the figure plots 75 at year 3 and its check `y1[0] - y1[-1] = 25` enforces the
   misreading. The legend "points the paper states" is false for 87.5 (derived, 100 − 0.5 × 25).
   The paper's own year-one comparison, "22 kg lost in the first year ... about 100% greater weight
   loss than our model prediction", puts the model near 11 kg at year one, below the table's
   "roughly 12 to 13 kg". The static line checks out (100 − 22.61 = 77.39; 100 − 3 × 22.61 = 32.17).
   Fix: prose "levelling off towards about 75 kg, and 95% of the way there after about three
   years"; year-3 point 76.25 with `derived: "100 - 0.95*25"`, drop the `= 25` check, legend
   "dynamic model, worked from the fractions the paper states"; table row "roughly 11 to 12.5 kg
   (the paper: the static rule's 22 kg is about double)". **recurring** (SELFCHECK 17, 18).

7. **error** · `must_know[2]` (kind boundary). "The straight line from the static rule is not wrong
   on day one." For weight it is at its worst then: Hall 2008 (held) says glycogen's "effects on
   weight loss typically occur within the first week", Hall 2011 adds fluid shifts when
   carbohydrate changes, and section 8 teaches that early loss is not fat. Only the
   no-adaptation assumption is close to true on day one. As written, a reader would trust the rule
   for the first week's scale readings. Fix: "The static rule's assumption that expenditure stays
   put is closest to true in the first days, but its assumption that the loss is fat is furthest
   from true then. Trust it for neither the first week nor the long run." **recurring**
   (SELFCHECK 7: contradicts another section).

8. **error** · `exercises[1].answer`. "Over the first several weeks of a diet it gives a usable,
   roughly correct estimate; published data from supervised diets land within a plausible range
   of what it predicts", and "print ... a range for the first few months". Thomas 2013, the section's
   own data, after a mean 64.8 days: "the majority of subjects exhibited substantially less weight
   loss than the amount predicted"; abstract: "we demonstrate the risk of applying the 3500 kcal
   rule even as a convenient estimate". 20.1 against 27.6 is a 27% over-prediction at about nine
   weeks. Fix: "Even over the first two months it over-predicts: supervised dieters lost about
   three-quarters of what it promised." Drop "a range for the first few months" or say the range
   must sit below the rule's figure. **recurring** (SELFCHECK 4, 5).

9. **error** · `practice[4]` (level 7) answer. "That part holds. The break is the student's word
   'forever'." The textbook's own one-year extrapolation is already the first break: Hall 2011
   puts the static rule at "about 100% greater weight loss than our model prediction" at one year,
   and the section's Thomas case over-predicts within nine weeks. Saying the one-year figure
   "holds" contradicts the illustration. (Arithmetic checked: 3500 / 200 = 17.5; 365 / 17.5 =
   20.86.) Fix: "The arithmetic holds. The textbook's own step to a year is the first break, since
   the body answers well inside a year; 'forever' repeats the same mistake." **recurring**
   (SELFCHECK 5).

12. **error** · `illustration.numbers[3]` unit, `practice[7]` (level 10) answer. "average deficit
