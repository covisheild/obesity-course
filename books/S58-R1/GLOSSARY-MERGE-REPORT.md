## 4. Drift inside S58-R1 (no record edited; each a possible fix pass item)

1. **legend for the key in C19.** C19's illustration 1 says "A legend in the corner says which is
   which", "The legend now tells the reader nothing" and "delete the legend". Exercise 2 says "A legend
   box". But C20's must-know says "this book calls it the key", and C14, C18, C20 and C22 say "key".
   Fix: replace "legend" with "key" in C19's illustration 1, exercise 2 and its worked answer.
2. **relative change and percentage change.** C09 defines a "relative change" (the difference divided by
   the starting percentage, in per cent). C17 uses "percentage change" (the change divided by the value
   it started from, times 100), the `B0-R0-C04` row's term. They are the same quantity. Both have rows.
   C09 could say "the percentage change (Book 0, A4)".
3. **context, two senses.** C01 and C03 use "context" for the opening part of an introduction or
   abstract ("Sentence 1 is context"). C18 makes "context" the non-data ink that stays on a figure. The
   row is qualified (on a figure); the two never meet in one section.
4. **title, three objects.** C03: a paper's title is "the message in its shortest form". C20: a figure's
   title is the caption's first sentence, not text drawn across the figure. C21: a table's title sits
   above the table. Consistent, but the book never sets them side by side. The row covers the figure.
5. **gap, everyday uses.** Besides the defined gap in a paper (C01, C04), the book uses "gap" for the
   words between subject and verb (C06, C07), a difference between groups (C09, C14, C16, C17) and a
   difference between two scores (C11). These are everyday senses.
6. **zig-zag.** C10's zig-zag paragraph; C17's practice 12 has a "zig-zag break mark" on an axis.
   Everyday; minor.
7. **colour scale names.** The rows say qualitative, sequential and diverging "colour scale"; C19's text
   says "a qualitative scale", "a sequential scale", "a diverging scale", after "A colour scale shows
   values fairly ...". Same thing; the row names keep "colour" to keep them apart from the **scale** row
   (`B0-R0-C19`).
8. **standard error, two names.** C09 says "standard error (SE)"; C20 says "standard error of the mean
   (SE)" and its exercise 2 adds "SEM, another name for the SE". Consistent.
9. **hazard ratio glossed only in a practice problem.** C09's problem 11 glosses it ("compares how fast an
   event happens in two groups"); C08 has already named it as one meaning of "HR". Minor.
