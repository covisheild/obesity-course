1. **error** · illustration 2, "Most acronyms are made up for one paper and hardly used again ... So a
   reader meeting a new acronym is usually meeting it for the first time"; `common_misreading`, "Most
   acronyms in the literature are new to the reader: Barnett and Doubleday found 30% used only once
   and 49% two to ten times."
   - Source: the 30 and 49 per cent are shares of the 1.1 million *distinct* acronyms ("out of the
     1.1 million acronyms analysed ... 30% occurring only once"). Only 30 per cent occurred once, so
     "made up for one paper" fits 30 per cent, not most. And a share of distinct acronyms says
     nothing about the share of acronyms a reader *meets*: the 0.2 per cent used over 10,000 times
     may make up most occurrences. The second sentence of the illustration is circular (a new acronym
     is by definition met for the first time).
   - Fix: "Most of the 1.1 million different acronyms were rare: 30 per cent appeared once, and
     another 49 per cent two to ten times." In the misreading: "Many acronyms are rare, so a reader
     may well not know the one in front of them." Recurring (SELFCHECK 4b, overreach).
   Fixed: illustration 2 now says "Most of the 1.1 million different acronyms were rare: 30 per cent appeared once, and another 49 per cent two to ten times" (1.1 million number added with its quote); the circular sentences cut; the misreading now reads "Many acronyms are rare, so a reader may well not know the one in front of them".
   Verified: closed
