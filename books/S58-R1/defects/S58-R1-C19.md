1. **gap** · `definition.text`, `must_know[1]`, Ex 1 (from compression hole 1; confirmed)
   - Claim: "Or it picks out one element against others drawn in grey", then "Each job takes its own
     kind of scale" and Ex 1, "the kind of colour scale each job takes".
   - Source: Wilke 4.3 describes highlighting with accent colours against baseline colours. That is
     not a scale. The Ex 1 answer ("one strong colour and make the rest grey") does not name a scale
     either.
   - Fix: in the definition, "... picks out one element: one strong accent colour, with the rest in
     grey". In must_know[1], "Each job takes its own kind of colour". In Ex 1, "the kind of colour
     each job takes".

2. **gap** · `definition.text`; Ex 2, Figure 5 (from compression hole 2; confirmed)
   - Claim: "It separates groups that have no order, using a qualitative scale". Ex 2's Figure 5
     has four age groups, which are ordered, and the answer gives no scale for them.
   - Source: Wilke 4.1 says a qualitative scale's colours "should not create the impression of an
     order". The book never says what ordered groups take.
   - Fix: add one sentence to the definition: "Groups that have an order, such as age groups, take
     steps of one colour from light to dark." In Ex 2's answer for Figure 5, write "four steps of
     one colour, lightest for the youngest".

3. **gap** · `must_know[7].point` (from compression hole 3; partly withdrawn)
   - Claim: "When you review a colleague's map or heat map ... Is there a colour bar?"
   - Finding: "colour bar" is defined in illustration 2 ("the strip, like an axis, that shows which
     colour stands for which value"), and that illustration renders before the must-know points.
     That half of the hole is withdrawn. "Heat map" is defined nowhere in the book.
   - Fix: "map or heat map (a table whose cells are shaded by value)".
   - **Recurring** (undefined term; SELFCHECK 14; S01-R1 compression holes).

4. **gap** · `definition.text` (from compression hole 4; corrected)
   - Claim: "a small set of colours that look clearly different from each other and equally strong."
   - Finding: "Hue" is glossed in illustration 1, step 3, and "lightness" in plain terms ("how light
     or dark"), so the red–green rule can be followed. "Equally strong" is never explained.
   - Fix: "and equally strong: none stands out from the others". This follows Wilke's own gloss,
     "no one color should stand out relative to the others".

5. **style** · `definition.text` against section 18 (from compression hole 24; confirmed)
   - Claim: "Colour that does none of these jobs is ornament." Section 18 is titled "Decoration
     that does no work".
   - Fix: "... is decoration (section 18)". This is one term for one thing (SELFCHECK 7).

6. **error** · the book's two-series palette against `must_know[2]` (from conductor's figure note
   FIG-2; confirmed)
   - Claim: C19 says "Red against green ... At similar lightness it fails twice ... make them
     differ in lightness."
   - Arithmetic: the series colours are #b6206b (a magenta-red) and #138613 (green). Their greyscale
     lightness is 85.4 and 86.5 of 255 (Rec. 601 luma), and CIE L* is 41 against 49. They are the
     red–green pair at similar lightness that this section forbids. Section 22's figure uses them
     (see the C22 file).
   - Fix (contract, deferred): `palette_for` must force a lightness gap, and its second hue must not
     be green against a red primary. Until then, no two-series figure in this book passes C19's own
     test. Say so in `READY`/`CHANGE-RECORD`, not in the book.

7. **gap** · `figures[0]` (and every single-series figure in sections 19 to 23)
   - Claim: plain terms says "If it does none of these, take it out and draw in black or grey".

   - Finding: the section's own figure has one series, so its colour does no job. It is filled in
     a saturated magenta (#b6206b). The same holds for C20's two figures and C23's two.
   - Fix (contract): `style_for` should draw a single series in neutral dark grey or a pale tint of

9. **error** · the reference list this section ends with ([290]–[316]); book-wide
   - Claim: [294] reads "Crameri, Fabio and Shephard, Grace E and Heron, Philip J. The misuse of
     colour in science communication. PMID 33116149, PMC7595127. Open access under CC BY 4.0. Quoted
     from the PMC open-access text. The importance of colour ... <url> (accessed 2026-10-02)."
   - Source: section 5 teaches NLM. NLM gives "Crameri F, Shephard GE, Heron PJ. The misuse of
     colour in science communication. Nat Commun. 2020;11(1):5444." `library.bib` holds the
     journal, year, volume, issue and pages, and the source header confirms them. The renderer
     drops these fields, prints BibTeX's "and" and full given names, and adds licence and extraction
     notes. It writes "(accessed ...)" in place of "[cited 2026 Oct 2]". The same applies to
     Krishnamurthy ([299]–[302], [314], [316]: Indian J Ophthalmol. 2021;69(8):2021-5) and to every
     Wilke entry.
   - The definition also carries 25 reference numbers on its last sentence ("[290, 291, ... 314]").
     A reader cannot tell which claim each one backs.
   - Fix (contract): the renderer must format entries as NLM, from the .bib fields. Licence notes stay
     out of the reader's list. Until then the book contradicts its own section 5.
