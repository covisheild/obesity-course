# C9 · Stocks and flows

**Definition.** A stock is a quantity that exists at a moment and can be measured at that moment. A flow is a
rate that changes a stock and, like any rate, it is quoted over a stretch of time or at a
moment.

The stock at any time is its starting value, plus everything that flowed in up to that time,
minus everything that flowed out. Each of those two totals is a rate multiplied by a time and
added across intervals, which is the running total of the previous section.

The net flow is the inflow minus the outflow. A stock rises while the net flow is positive,
falls while it is negative, and holds steady while it is zero.

A stock and a flow carry different units. A stock carries the unit of the quantity. A flow
carries that same unit divided by a unit of time. The two are never added to each other.

A stock that holds steady is evidence that the inflow and the outflow matched over the period
measured, and is no evidence at all that either one was small.

**In plain terms.** Picture a water tank with a tap running into it and a drain running out of it.

**The stock is what is in the tank.** It is an amount, it exists right now, and you could measure
it this minute by looking. Litres in the tank is a stock. Kilograms of grain in a store is a
stock. A number of people on a waiting list is a stock.

**A flow is what goes in or what goes out.** It is a rate, so it has a time on the bottom of its
unit. Litres a minute is a flow. Kilograms a month is a flow. People joining the list each week
is a flow.

Look at the unit. If it has a "per" or an "a" with a time after it, you are holding a flow. If it
does not, you are holding a stock.

Now the one line this whole section is for. Say it in words first.

The amount in the tank now is the amount you started with, plus everything that came in, minus
everything that went out.

```working
    the stock now = the starting stock, plus the total in, minus the total out
```

Each of those two totals is built the way the last section built one. Take the flow, multiply by
how long it ran, and add the pieces up.

The difference between the inflow and the outflow has a name: the net flow.

Read that third line again, because it is the one people get wrong.

A stock that does not move tells you the two flows matched. It tells you nothing whatever about
how big they were.

**A stock can be large, steady and busy all at once.** That is the sentence to carry out of this
section.

A stock and a flow are never added together.

**Illustration.** Start where the mistake gets made, which is an inspection.

A fair price shop keeps its grain in a store room behind the counter. An inspector weighs what is on the shelves at the end of April and finds 2,000 kilograms. She comes back at the end
of May and finds 2,000 kilograms again.

Her note says this.

> The stock has not moved in a month. Nothing is being distributed here.

The first sentence is a measurement. The second is a conclusion, and it does not follow.

Work out what was going through the shop. This one serves 500 priority households and each of them has four people in it. The shop, the 500 households and the 2,000 kilograms are made
up for this example. The rate below is not.

Section 3(1) of the National Food Security Act, 2013 entitles a person in a priority household to five kilograms of foodgrains per person per month. So each household is counted for
twenty kilograms a month.

```working
    5 times 4 = 20
```

Five hundred households at twenty kilograms a month each.

```working
    20 times 500 = 10000
```

Ten thousand kilograms a month go out of the shop to households. The same ten thousand have to come in from the depot, or the shelves would empty.

Now put those two flows against the stock on the shelves.

```working
    10000 divided by 2000 = 5
```

Five times the whole contents of the store room passed through it during the month. The shelves held 2,000 kilograms on both days the inspector looked, and they held different grain.

So write the balance line out for May and watch the two flows cancel.

```working
    2000 plus 10000 minus 10000 = 2000
```

Two thousand kilograms at the end of May, which is what she measured. Nothing in that number could have told her the size of either flow, because the two flows left it exactly where
they found it.

Now make the flows differ, so that you can see the stock move.

```table
month  stock at the start  came in  went out  stock at the end
1      2,000               10,000   10,000    2,000
2      2,000               9,000    10,000    1,000
3      1,000               11,000   10,000    2,000
```

Check each row with the same line.

```working
    2000 plus 10000 minus 10000 = 2000
```

Month two, where the depot sent a thousand kilograms short.

```working
    2000 plus 9000 minus 10000 = 1000
```

The stock ends where it began, three months later. Read the first row and the last row alone and you would say nothing happened at all.

Check the units before you go on, because this is where the two kinds of quantity get mixed. The stock is in kilograms. The flows are in kilograms a month. Adding one to the other gives
nothing, and multiplying a flow by a month is what turns it into something you can add.

```working
    kilograms per month, times months
    = kilograms
```

A person's body weight is a stock.

Food energy taken in is a flow into that stock. Energy spent is a flow out of it. Both are rates and both carry a time on the bottom, in the kilojoules or the kilocalories you met in
the section on energy units.

The balance line is the same one the store room had. The weight now is the weight you started with, plus everything that came in, minus everything that went out.

A weight that has not changed over a year is a stock whose net flow came to zero across that year. It is not a person in whom nothing is happening. Both flows may be very large, and the
arithmetic says only that they matched.

This section gives you no figure for either flow and you should not take one from here. Three questions follow. How large is each flow, how is each one measured, and what happens to one
when the other changes? All three are the work of the subject booklet on energy balance. Every one of those needs evidence that this section does not carry.

What you have is the shape. The shape is what stops a steady weight being read as an absence of movement.

**Where this picture breaks.** The shop, the 500 households, the four people in each and the 2,000 kilograms on the shelves
are all made up for the example. Only the five kilograms per person per month comes from the
Act.

The store room has one door in and one door out, and every kilogram through either is counted.
Count only some of the doors and the traffic you missed turns up in the arithmetic as an error
in a flow you did count.

A store room's two flows are set from outside it. Grain arrives because a depot sent it. Grain
leaves because shops drew it. Neither answers to what is on the shelves.

That is not true of every stock. In some systems one flow moves when the other moves, or moves
with the level of the stock. Change the inflow in a system like that and the balance does not
shift by the amount you changed. The balance line still holds. What fails is the assumption
that you can alter one term and leave the others where they were.

Whether a person's two flows work that way is a question about the body, not about arithmetic.
This section does not answer it. That is why you get the shape here and no numbers.

Two readings a month apart do not make a steady stock.

**Must know points for you.**

- A stock that is not moving is not a stock with nothing going through it. The level on its own cannot tell the two cases apart.
- A time on the bottom means a flow. No time means a stock, and the two answer different questions.
- Never add or subtract a stock and a flow. Kilograms and kilograms a month are different kinds of quantity.
- Write the balance line out before you argue about any of its parts. Starting stock, plus everything in, minus everything out.
- The balance line tells you what must be true of the numbers and never what caused a flow to change. Do not use it to explain why anything moved.
- A person whose weight has been steady for a year is not a person in whom nothing is happening. Steadiness means the two flows matched over the year, not that either was small.
- Auditing a stock cannot find a matched pair of wrong flows.
- When a journalist asks whether something is getting worse, separate the level from the traffic through it.

**8.** Here is a worked answer. Find the step that broke.

```working
    a store held 2,000 kilograms in April and 2,000 kilograms in May
    the stock did not change over the month
    a stock that does not change has no flows
    so nothing came into the store and nothing left it
    so the shop distributed no grain in May
```

**9.** Here is a worked answer. Find the step that broke.

```working
    a store holds 3,000 kilograms today
    it receives 800 kilograms a month and issues 950 kilograms a month
    the net flow is 800 minus 950, which is minus 150
    3,000 minus 150 = 2,850
    so the store will be empty in 2,850 months
```
