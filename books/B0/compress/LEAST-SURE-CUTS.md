# Step 5a · the cut each cutter was least sure about

One per section, written before the cold read and kept unedited. Three of the eight were
answered by the test: A1's and A7's worries about the boundary move, and A4's about the
same thing, all came back clean. A8's was upheld and restored. A6's cutter predicted its own
result exactly.

## A1

# A1 pass 1 — the cut I was least sure about

Dropping the last paragraph of `illustration.analogy_breaks_when`: that these figures are only what this copy of the Act says, that it names no change after 2008, and that a later section teaches how to check a copy is current.

Practice problems P09 and P10 ask the reader to say what their answer does not establish. That paragraph was the worked model of exactly that move. Nothing kept replaces it.

## A2

# A2 pass 1 — the cut I was least sure about

Cutting from `definition.text`: *Two fractions may be compared or added only once they stand over the same denominator, because a part is defined by the whole it was cut from.*

The rule survives only as an instruction in must-know point 2, stripped of its reason. P03 asks the reader to say *why* you cannot add the tops and the bottoms. Nothing kept answers that.

## A3

# A3 pass 1 — the cut I was least sure about

Deleting the round-once habit twice: the closing paragraph of
`simplified_explanation` ("Keep every digit while you are working") and the
illustration's "That is why you round once, at the end, and never in the middle
of a chain." The rule now survives only in must-know point 1. The worked leak
(0.17 times 30 = 5.1) still shows the damage, but nothing next to it states the
habit, and practice problem 7 is a diagnostic on exactly that error.

## A4

# A4 pass 1 — the cut I am least sure about

Deleting the two sentences about one extra child in a village against a hundred thousand across the country, and with them the second sentence of must-know point 7.

Together they were the only place the text says what a percentage does **not** establish. Practice problems 9 and 10 ask for exactly that. What survives — "Ask for the count as well as the percentage, always" — is an instruction, not the reason behind it.

## A5

# A5 pass 1 — the cut I was least sure about

I deleted this sentence from the illustration, right after the Antyodaya figure:

> That figure applies to the extent the Central Government specifies for each State under that scheme.

It is the only qualification on the thirty-five kilogram figure, and the reader carries that figure into exercise 1 and into practice problems 5 and 8. Without it they may read the entitlement as flat across every State. Nothing in the test set asks about it, so it went.

## A6

# A6 pass 1 — the cut I was least sure about

Deleting square roots everywhere: the definition paragraph, the plain-terms paragraph, and the
sentence in `illustration.analogy_breaks_when` about most square roots not being whole numbers.

No exercise and none of the ten practice problems touch roots, so the test set will not miss them.
But the section is called "Powers, roots and scientific notation", and after this cut nothing under
that heading teaches a root at all.

## A7

# A7 pass 1 — the cut I was least sure about

Dropping "These four are not every penalty in the Act, and you picked them. Say so if you show anybody the figure." from `illustration.body`.

Practice problems P09 and P10 both end by asking the reader to say what a figure or an answer does not establish. Those two sentences were the section's only worked instance of that move, and nothing kept replaces them. The same paragraph's cut companion in `analogy_breaks_when` went too.

## A8

# A8 pass 1 — the cut I was least sure about

I deleted the end of the illustration: "Last, what you say out loud when you have
done this", and the quoted sentences that model it — the rough figure, the two
assumptions named, the invitation to correct them.

It is the only place the section shows the reader the actual wording. Practice
problems 9 and 10 and exercise E2 all ask for that wording. Must-know point 5
states the rule, but never demonstrates it.
