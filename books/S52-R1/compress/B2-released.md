# B2 · Converting units as multiplication by one

**Definition.** A conversion factor is a fraction whose top and bottom are the same quantity written in two
different units. Because the top and the bottom are equal, the fraction is equal to one.

The orientation to use is the one that puts the unwanted unit where it cancels.

**In plain terms.** One kilometre is 1,000 metres. That is an agreement, and you can write it as a fraction in two
ways.

```working
    1,000 m over 1 km
    1 km over 1,000 m
```

Both of those fractions are equal to one.

Multiplying by one does not change an amount. Only the name on it changes.

Which of the two do you use? The one that cancels the unit you want to get rid of.

Turn 7 kilometres into metres. You want the km gone, so put km on the bottom.

```working
    7 km times 1,000 m over 1 km
    = 7 times 1,000 m
    = 7,000 m
```

Metres are left, which is what you asked for.

**Illustration.** Section 3(1) of the National Food Security Act, 2013 entitles a person in a priority household
to five kilograms of foodgrains per person per month. Somebody on a call asks you what that is
in grams a day.

Here is the answer that comes back first. It is wrong.

```working
    5 divided by 1,000 = 0.005
```

Nothing is wrong with that arithmetic. What is wrong is the choice to divide.

A gram is smaller than a kilogram, so the number of grams has to be bigger than 5, not smaller.
And written out as a factor, the unit that comes out is not grams at all.

```working
    5 kg times 1 kg over 1,000 g
    = 0.005 kg squared per gram
```

Now do it properly. Put the kg where it cancels.

```working
    5 kg per person per month, times 1,000 g over 1 kg
    = 5,000 g per person per month
```

Five thousand grams. Bigger number, smaller unit, as it had to be.

There is no agreed number of days in a month, so you have to choose one and say so. Take 30
days.

```working
    5,000 g per person per month, times 1 month over 30 days
    = 166.67 g per person per day
```

About 167 grams a day.

Write the quantity, then each factor, then cancel.

```working
    5 kg per person per month
    times 1,000 g over 1 kg
    times 1 month over 30 days
    = 166.67 g per person per day
```

A store has 45,000 grams of a commodity and the register wants kilograms.

```working
    45,000 g times 1 kg over 1,000 g
    = 45 kg
```

The same factor, the other way up, chosen for the same reason: it puts the unwanted unit where
it cancels.

**Where this picture breaks.** The 30 days is a choice, not a fact.

The Act gives a monthly entitlement. The daily figure is yours, and you say so when you quote
it.

And conversion only ever works between two units of the same kind. There is no factor from
kilograms to litres, or from grams to rupees.

**Must know points for you.**

- A conversion factor is not a rule about when to multiply and when to divide. It is a fraction equal to one, and which way up you write it is settled by which unit you want to cancel.
- Check the unit that fell out of the working. If it is not the unit you were asked for, the factor was upside down.
- Write the unit beside every line while you work, not once at the end.
- Run the size check before you act on any converted dose or dispensed amount. A bigger unit gives a smaller number. A smaller unit gives a bigger number.
- There is a conversion factor only between two units of the same kind.
- A monthly entitlement becomes a daily figure only by choosing a number of days. You make that choice, and no statute makes it for you.

**5.** A chart is written in micrograms and the label on the bottle is in milligrams.

Write 0.25 milligrams in micrograms. Then write 4,000 micrograms in milligrams. State which
orientation of the factor you used each time.

**6.** Here is a worked answer. Find the step that broke.

```working
    a prescription is written as 0.5 g and the chart wants milligrams
    1 gram is 1,000 milligrams
    0.5 g times 1 g over 1,000 mg
    = 0.0005
    so the chart entry is 0.0005 mg
```

**7.** Here is a worked answer. Find the step that broke.

```working
    a plot covers 12,000 square metres and the report wants square kilometres
    1 kilometre is 1,000 metres
    12,000 divided by 1,000 = 12
    so the plot covers 12 square kilometres
```
