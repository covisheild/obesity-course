2. **`simplified_explanation` (the evidence hierarchy paragraph); `must_know[2]`; retrieval item 2**
   - **Claim:** "a ladder of study designs, called the evidence hierarchy, with the randomised controlled trial near the top. That ladder ranks designs for one question: does this treatment work, and by how much? It has no rung for 'why do patients stop coming?'" must_know[2] adds that interview findings "sit low on the evidence hierarchy".
   - **What the source says:** no held source mentions an evidence hierarchy. A search of all ten S36 source files for "hierarch" finds only social hierarchies (ICMR, Kitzinger), nothing about evidence. Green and Britten say only that "good 'evidence' goes further than the results of meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials". The record's own note says this supports the claim "only by implication". Pope and Mays 1995 call the randomised controlled trial "the epitome of the quantitative method". That is not the same as a ranking, and it says nothing about what the ranking is for.
   - **Smallest fix:** obtain and hold a source that sets out the hierarchy and the question it ranks designs for, and quote it. Until then, rewrite the paragraph to what the held sources say: "Evidence-based medicine often treats the pooled results of randomised trials as the strongest evidence that a treatment works. Green and Britten argue that good evidence goes further than that." Soften must_know[2] and retrieval item 2 to match.
   - **Tag:** error. **Recurring** (SELFCHECK 4; §7b, a claim written from what the model knows).
   - Fixer: confirmed (no held source has an evidence hierarchy). Replaced the ladder paragraph with Green and Britten's two quoted points (evidence goes further than pooled trial results; different questions need different research); must_know[2] now answers "anecdotal" from the held anecdote/rigour quotes; retrieval item 2 asks what Green and Britten say; exercise 2's answer now argues "weakest for what?" as reasoning, not from a ranking; the reference note no longer mentions a hierarchy.
