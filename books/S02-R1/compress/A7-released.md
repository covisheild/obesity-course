# A7 · Logarithms — what they are for before how they work

**Definition.** The logarithm of a number to base ten is the power to which ten must be raised to produce that
number.

It is defined only for numbers above zero, because no power of ten produces zero or a negative
number. The logarithm of one is zero.

A logarithm converts multiplication into addition.

On an axis marked in logarithms, equal distances represent equal multiplications rather than
equal additions.

**In plain terms.** You have a pile of numbers and they are wildly different sizes. Put them on an ordinary scale
and the small ones vanish into the left edge.

So ask a different question of each number. Not how big is it, but how many powers of ten is it?

Ten is 10^1, so its logarithm is 1. That is the whole definition: the logarithm is the exponent.

Numbers in between get an answer in between. Five hundred sits between a hundred and a thousand,
so its logarithm sits between 2 and 3. A calculator will tell you it is about 2.7. You do not
have to work that out by hand, and you will not be asked to.

Put logarithms on the side of a graph and equal distances up the page stop meaning equal
amounts. They mean equal multiples. One step is ten times, whether you are stepping from 1 to 10
or from 100 to 1,000.

**Illustration.** Picture the side of the graph marked like this, bottom to top.

```working
    1     10     100     1,000     10,000
```

The marks are evenly spaced up the page. Somebody looks at it and says this.

> The rise from 100 to 1,000 is the same size as the rise from 1 to 10.

Do the subtraction yourself and watch the claim fall apart.

```working
    10 minus 1 = 9
    1,000 minus 100 = 900
```

Nine against nine hundred. The gaps on the page are equal and the amounts are a hundred times
apart.

So what is equal? Do the division instead of the subtraction.

```working
    10 divided by 1 = 10
    1,000 divided by 100 = 10
```working

Both steps multiply by ten.

Open the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 again, the one you opened in section A1, and
search inside it for "twenty-five thousand". One hit this time, in the proviso to section 50: a penalty not exceeding
twenty-five thousand rupees for small food businesses.

For the other three, search the section headings rather than the money. So search for "Penalty
for misbranded food", then "Penalty for sub-standard food", then "Penalty for misleading
advertisement".

Write the four down and turn each into a power of ten. Use a calculator for the logarithm
column; that is what it is for.

```table
    25,000      2.5 times 10^4      logarithm 4.4
    3,00,000    3 times 10^5        logarithm 5.5
    5,00,000    5 times 10^5        logarithm 5.7
    10,00,000   1 times 10^6        logarithm 6.0
```

The four you wrote down run from 4.4 to 6.0. That is a span of 1.6, and 1.6 powers of ten is a
factor of about forty.

The heaviest of your four is about forty times the lightest.

```working
    100 times 1,000 = 1,00,000
    logarithm 2 plus logarithm 3 = 5
    and 10^5 is indeed 1,00,000
```

Adding the logarithms did the multiplying.

**Where this picture breaks.** A logarithmic axis makes very different numbers fit on one page, and it does that by squashing
the differences between the large ones. A tenfold rise and a hundredfold rise look similar on
the page.

It also cannot show zero, and it cannot show anything below it.

**Figure.** The four penalties on a ruler where each step multiplies by ten. The logarithm of an amount is simply where it sits on that ruler, so the distance between the smallest and the largest is a subtraction that has done a division.

*What the figure shows:* A horizontal ruler marked ten to the fourth, fifth and sixth. Four points sit on it at twenty-five thousand, three lakh, five lakh and ten lakh, labelled underneath with their logarithms of 4.4, 5.5, 5.7 and 6.0, and an arrow spanning 1.6 units.

**Must know points for you.**

- On a logarithmic axis equal distances are equal multiples, not equal amounts.
- Before reading any figure, look at the axis and decide which kind it is. If the marks go 1, 10, 100, 1,000 the steps are multiplications. If they go 1, 2, 3 they are additions.
- A logarithmic axis cannot show zero or anything below it, because no power of ten produces them. Ask what happened to those observations.
- A log axis makes a large difference look modest. If you are arguing about the size of an effect rather than its presence, that axis is working against you.
- Steps of one lakh, two lakh, three lakh are an ordinary scale. Steps of one lakh, ten lakh, one crore are a log scale. Read the steps rather than the words.
- Asked in a viva what the logarithm of one is, the answer is zero, because ten to the power zero is one.
- What you must be able to do is say what one means and read an axis built from them. And notice when a figure has been drawn on one without saying so.

**10.** Here is a worked answer. Find the step that broke.

```working
    logarithm of 200 = 2.3
    logarithm of 300 = 2.5
    2.3 plus 2.5 = 4.8

```
