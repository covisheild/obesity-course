# C5 · Graphs: axes, scale, slope and intercept

**Definition.** A graph is a drawing of a relationship between two quantities, made of points in a plane.

Two lines of reference cross at a point called the origin. The horizontal one is the x-axis and
the upright one is the y-axis. Each axis carries one quantity, the unit that quantity is
measured in, and a scale.

The scale is the rule that turns a distance along an axis into an amount of the quantity.
Whoever draws the figure chooses it.

A point is fixed by two numbers, written (x, y), which are its position along the x-axis and
its position along the y-axis.

The slope of a straight line is the change in the y quantity divided by the change in the x
quantity, measured between any two points on it.

The intercept on the y-axis is the value the y quantity takes where the x quantity is zero. It
is a property of the line as drawn. It states something about the world only where zero is a
value the x quantity can take.

**In plain terms.** Four things decide what the picture tells you. Read them in this order, every time. The first two
come before you look at the line at all.

**What is on each axis.** Look at the flat line along the bottom. That is the x-axis. Look at the
upright line up the side. That is the y-axis. Each should carry the name of a quantity and a
unit. An axis with no unit on it is a picture you cannot read.

**What the scale is.** The scale is how much of the quantity one step along the axis stands for.
Somebody chose it. They could have chosen another one. The same numbers on a taller axis look
calm, and on a shorter axis look steep, and neither drawing is a lie. So read the numbers printed
on the axis before you trust the shape.

**How steep the line is.** Steepness is called the slope. Pick two points on the line. Work out
how far up it went between them, and how far across.

```working
    the slope = the change up, divided by the change across
```

The change up is called the rise. The change across is called the run.

A slope is a rate, and it carries a unit like every rate you have met. The unit is the y unit
divided by the x unit.

A slope can be negative. A line that falls as you go right has a rise below zero, so the slope
comes out with a minus sign in front. The section on counting covers numbers like that. A slope
of minus 3 means the y quantity drops by 3 for every one step across.

**Where the line crosses.** Follow the line to the place where the x quantity is zero. The height
there is called the intercept. It answers one question: what was the y quantity when the x
quantity was nothing at all?

Sometimes zero is outside anything anybody measured, and then the intercept is a number the
drawing made.

**Illustration.** Take one rule and draw it twice.

Section 3(1) of the National Food Security Act, 2013 entitles a person in a priority household
to five kilograms of foodgrains per person per month.

Work out eight points, one for each household size from one person to eight.

```table
people in the household  kilograms a month
1                        5
2                        10
3                        15
4                        20
5                        25
6                        30
7                        35
8                        40
```

Now draw them. People go along the bottom. Kilograms a month go up the side.

Place one point by hand first. Take the household of three and its 15 kilograms. As a pair it
is written (3, 15). The number along the bottom comes first and the number up the side second.

Start at the origin, where both axes read zero. Go along the bottom until you reach 3. From
there go straight up until you are level with 15 on the side axis. Put the mark there. Every
other row goes on the same way.

The brackets in (3, 15) are not the brackets in f(x) from the section on functions. There they
held a rule's input. Here they hold a pair of numbers, and the comma between them is the sign.

Draw it once with the side axis running from 0 to a little above 40 kilograms. The line fills
the picture and the rise is the first thing you see.

Draw it again with the side axis running from 0 to 200 kilograms. Every point is the same point
as before. The line now sits in the bottom fifth of the picture and looks nearly flat.

Ask what changed between the two. Only the numbers printed up the side.

What differs is the impression, and the impression was set by a choice.

So here is the habit. Read the numbers on both axes first. Then decide whether a rise you can
see is a rise that matters. Never take the steepness of a line as a measurement, because it is
not one.

Now read a slope and an intercept off a line, which is the other thing a graph is for.

A household of four has 10 kilograms of grain in the house today. Every month it is counted for
another 20 kilograms, because five kilograms a person times four people is twenty. The 10
kilograms is made up for this example.

```working
    5 times 4 = 20
```

Put months along the bottom, starting at today. Put kilograms in the house up the side.

```table
months from today  kilograms in the house
0                  10
1                  30
2                  50
3                  70
4                  90
5                  110
6                  130
```

The points sit on a straight line. Take two of them and read the slope off the picture. Use the
point at one month and the point at four months.

Work out the rise, which is the change up the side.

```working
    90 minus 30 = 60
```

Work out the run, which is the change along the bottom.

```working
    4 minus 1 = 3
```

Divide the rise by the run.

```working
    60 divided by 3 = 20
```

Twenty. Now put a unit on it, and take the unit off the axes rather than out of your memory.
The side axis is in kilograms and the bottom axis is in months.

```working
    kilograms divided by months
    = kilograms per month
```

Twenty kilograms a month.

Pick a different pair of points and the slope comes out the same, because the line is straight.

Now the intercept. Follow the line back to month zero, where the bottom quantity is nothing at
all. The line meets the side axis at 10 kilograms.

That number means something here. It is the grain that was in the house before any of these
months began. Somebody can go and look in the house and check it.

Often nobody wrote down a reading at zero. Find the intercept anyway by stepping back from the
nearest reading, using the slope. Suppose the table had started at two months, with 50
kilograms. Each month back takes off one slope's worth of grain.

```working
    20 times 2 = 40
    50 minus 40 = 10
```

Ten kilograms, the same as the line gave.

The slope and the intercept together fix the whole line. In words, the output is the
intercept, plus the slope times the input. Here that is 10 kilograms, plus 20 kilograms a
month times the number of months. Write y for the side quantity and x for the bottom one. Write
m for the slope and k for the intercept. Then the line is this.

```working
    y = m x + k
```

You met that line in the section on rearranging, as letters with no meaning attached. This is
its meaning. For the grain in the house, m is 20 and k is 10. Try it at four months.

```working
    20 times 4 = 80
    80 plus 10 = 90
```

Ninety kilograms, which is the row for four months in the table.

Now watch an intercept stop meaning anything. Go back to the first drawing, where the bottom
axis was the number of people. Its intercept is the grain for a household of nobody. No such
household exists, so that number is something the drawing produced and nothing anybody can
check.

**Where this picture breaks.** Reading one slope off a line works while the line is straight.

An upright line has no slope, because the division has nothing to divide by.

The intercept means something only where zero is a value the bottom quantity can really take.

Two drawings of the same points are both honest, however different they look.

And a straight line through some points does not make the rule behind them true.

**Figure.** The same eight entitlements drawn twice. Nothing about the numbers changed between the two panels; only the top of the side axis did. The left panel makes the rise look steep and the right makes it look flat, and neither is dishonest.

*What the figure shows:* Two line charts side by side with identical data points rising from left to right. The left chart's side axis stops just above the highest point; the right chart's runs to five times that, so the same line appears almost flat.

**Figure.** Slope read as a rise over a run, and the intercept read where the line meets the side axis. The rise and the run are marked between the months the text works through.

*What the figure shows:* A rising straight line through seven points. A horizontal segment marks a run of three months and a vertical segment marks a rise of sixty kilograms, giving a slope of twenty kilograms a month. A filled dot on the side axis marks the intercept at ten.

**Must know points for you.**

- Read both axes, with their units, before you look at the shape of anything.
- A steep line is not a large effect.
- A side axis that does not start at zero makes every small wiggle on it look like a cliff.
- A slope carries the y unit divided by the x unit.
- An intercept is a fact about the world only where zero is a value the bottom quantity can actually take.
- When a figure alarms you, redraw it with the side axis starting at zero before you repeat it to a journalist.
- Teach a trainee to read a figure in one fixed order. What each axis measures and its unit come first, then the numbers on the scale. Only then the slope, and last the intercept.
- Before you read a value off any printed chart in clinic, find what one small division on each axis stands for.

**9.** Here is a worked answer. Find the step that broke.

```working
    a line passes through (2, 30) and (8, 90)
    the run is 8 minus 2, which is 6
    the rise is 90 minus 30, which is 60
    the slope is the run divided by the rise
    6 divided by 60 = 0.1
    so the slope is 0.1
```
