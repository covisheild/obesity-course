1. **error** · `must_know[3].point` (from compression hole 1).
   - Claim: "Add or scale rows only when every entry means the same thing, in the same unit, in the
     same place. Food grams add. Two patients' rows do not."
   - Two patients' rows pass that test: each entry means the same thing, in the same unit and
     place. So the rule lets through the case it is meant to forbid. The real test is whether the
     sum, entry by entry, is itself an amount of something. Grams of a meal are. A total of two
     ages is not. The analogy paragraph that had the same wording was cut in compression, so this
     point is the only place the defect is left.
   - Fix: "Add or scale rows only when the sum, entry by entry, is itself an amount of something,
     such as the grams in a meal. Food grams add. Two patients' rows do not: their total age
     describes nobody. The difference between one patient's rows a year apart is a change, and
     that does mean something."

2. **gap** · `illustration.body`, the patient rows, with `must_know[3]` (compression residue of
   B-C10-2).
   - The illustration now shows only that one patient's two rows can be subtracted. The
     counterexample was cut: two patients' rows added, or one row doubled, give a list that
     "describes nobody". So nothing in the section shows the "do not" in `must_know[3]`, and the
     reader is not told why the illustration opens with patient rows.
   - Fix: restore the original's two sentences before "One operation on these rows does mean
     something": "Add a second patient's row to this one, and you get a list of four numbers that
     describes nobody. Multiply this row by 2 and you get a person of 80 years and 136 kg, which
     is not a doubled version of anyone."

3. **gap** · `definition.text`, the dot-product paragraph, against P4 part 1 (from compression
   hole 2).
   - Claim: P4 answers "(1, 2, 3) · (4, 5)" with "Cannot. A dot product needs two vectors of the
     same length". The definition gives the rule only implicitly ("The dot product of two vectors
     of the same length ..."). Addition gets an explicit "cannot".
   - Fix: after "then add the products", add "Two vectors of different lengths have no dot
     product."

4. **error** · `illustration.numbers`, the three `fssai_labelling_2020` entries, and the reference
   list. **Recurring** (a numbers entry that no reader-facing sentence uses; Book 0 C29 and C34, compression defects).
   - The FSSAI paragraph ("In India this is not only a textbook exercise ... regulation 5(3)(e)(i)
     ...") was cut in compression. Its three numbers entries were left. The built page therefore
     prints "*Figures quoted in this illustration are from [75, 76]*", and [76] is the FSSAI
     labelling regulations. No FSSAI figure is quoted anywhere in the section. The reader is told
     the illustration quotes a source that it does not quote.
   - Fix: either restore the paragraph (preferred, because it is the section's only Indian
     anchor and item 5 needs it), or delete the three `fssai_labelling_2020` numbers entries.

5. **error** · `illustration.analogy_breaks_when`, first paragraph.
   - Claim: "So a · g gives the energy a label would declare."
   - The factors a = (4, 9, 4) do not give the energy an Indian label declares, in general.
     Regulation 5(3)(e)(i) of the FSSAI labelling regulations lists eight factors, not three:
     "(B) Polyols except Erythritol 2 kcal/g", "(F) Alcohol (Ethanol) 7 kcal/g", "(G) Organic acid
     3 kcal/g" and "(H)Dietary fibre 2kcal/g" as well as protein, fat and carbohydrate. The FAO
     general factors alone do not say anything about labels.
   - Fix: "So a · g gives the energy a label would declare for a food whose only energy-giving
     parts are protein, fat and carbohydrate." If item 4 restores the FSSAI paragraph, add after
     its factors: "It lists others too, such as 2 kcal/g for dietary fibre."

6. **style** · `illustration.body` (build warning).
   - The build reports "illustration.body never addresses the reader". Every step is written as
     "Subtract entry by entry", "Scale the vector", which reads as an instruction, but none says
     "you".
   - Fix: turn one step into direct address, for example "Now read the weights. Add 1 g of fat to
     the serving and hold the other two entries fixed. Watch what the total does."

7. **style** · `must_know[4].point`.
   - Claim: "Check a meal's energy in an app or a diet chart two ways. The two answers must
     agree."
   - Linearity makes the two ways agree whenever the arithmetic is right. So as written, the point
     does not say what to do when they disagree, and that is the only case where the check changes
     anything.
   - Fix: add "If they disagree by more than rounding, one item's grams or energy was entered
     wrong. Find it before you use the total."

8. **gap** · currency, `illustration.numbers` (FSSAI entries, `as_of: '2025-09-09'`).
   - The FSSAI figures are from compendium Version-VIII of 09.09.2025. A later compendium or
     amendment could change the factors. The Atwater general factors (FAO 2003) and the textbook
     rules will not go out of date.
   - Fix: if item 4 keeps the FSSAI paragraph, re-check the factors against the newest FSSAI
     labelling compendium before release. Record "next FSSAI labelling notification" as the event
     trigger in the entry's note.
