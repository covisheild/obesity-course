1. **error** · `must_know[1].point`, "For a shrinking curve, where k is negative, it also gives the
   wrong sign." (from compression hole A-C06-4; confirmed).
   - C04 fixes a shrinking curve as e^(−kt) with k above zero, and this section's own exercise and
     illustration use that form (D0 e^(−kt), "k is above zero"; 730 e^(−0.693t)). Under the book's
     convention k is never negative, so the sentence contradicts C04 and this section's worked
     examples. (The table row "e^(kt), for k not zero" is right as mathematics; the must-know point
     is where the conventions collide.)
   - Fix: "For a shrinking curve, e^(-kt), dropping the k also gives the wrong sign: the
     antiderivative is e^(-kt) divided by -k."
   - Notation (book-wide): C07 also writes "k is minus 1 divided by 385.809". See C04 item 5.

2. **error** · `must_know[4].point`, "In a committee, call such a figure an upper limit that holds
   only if the deficit never shrinks."
   - The direction is inverted. A steady-deficit total is an upper limit *because* a real deficit
     shrinks (or at best holds); it is *reached* only if the deficit never shrinks. As written, the
     limit "holds" only in the one case where it is not a limit but the exact figure.
   - Fix: "call such a figure an upper limit, reached only if the deficit never shrinks."
   - Recurring: yes (a bound whose direction is lost; SELFCHECK 13, Part D C24 and C25).

3. **gap** · `illustration.body`, "It comes back." (the last line).
   - A leftover. In the original it closed the straight-line check ("Differentiate back: 730 minus
     2 times 182.5t, which is 730 minus 365t. It comes back."), which compression cut. Placed after
     "A deficit that shrinks adds up to less.", "it" has no referent and nothing comes back.
   - Fix: delete the sentence.
   - Recurring: yes (a sentence whose referent was cut; Book 0 C28 "dangling shape sentence").

4. **gap** · `illustration.body`, "As T grows, e^(-0.693T) shrinks towards zero, so its total creeps
   up towards 1053.4 megajoules and never passes it." (from compression hole A-C06-3; confirmed).
   - T is never introduced, and neither is the total from 0 to T that "its total" refers to. The
     illustration has computed only the first year.
   - Fix: before it, "Take the total up to any later time T. It is F(T) minus F(0), which is
     1053.4 times (1 minus e^(-0.693T))." (Two sentences, both under the length limit.)

5. **gap** · `must_know[2].point`, "A deficit D0 e^(-kt) adds up to at most D0 divided by k".
   - D0 and the general deficit D0 e^(−kt) first appear to the reader in Exercise 1, which is
     printed after the must-know points. At the must-know point D0 is undefined.
   - Fix: covered by item 4 if its sentence is written in general form: "For a deficit D(t) =
     D0 e^(-kt), with D0 its value at t = 0, the total up to T is D0 times (1 minus e^(-kT)) divided by
     k. Here D0 is 730 and k is 0.693." Otherwise say "D0, its value at the start," in the point.
