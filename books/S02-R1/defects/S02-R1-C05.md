3. **error** · P7 [4] prompt and answer, "Work out the smallest error this gives in the total spent
   over the 14 days." / "At least 1,400 kilocalories over the 14 days." / "Whether the error really
   runs the same way every day is your assumption, not the paper's."
   - Hall 2012 gives an *uncertainty*: "the doubly labeled water method has a precision of;5%, which
     translates to an uncertainty of energy expenditure of .100 kcal/d". An uncertainty above 100 a
     day is not a floor on the error in any one measurement, so "smallest error" and "at least
     1,400" turn a width into a minimum. And doubly labelled water gives one average rate for the
     whole period, so the uncertainty of that average times 14 days is the uncertainty of the total
     without any "same way every day" assumption; the answer teaches a condition that does not
     apply.
   - Fix: prompt, "Work out what that uncertainty becomes in the total spent over the 14 days."
     Answer: "More than 1,400 kilocalories. The method gives one average rate for the whole period,
     so the uncertainty in that average is multiplied by the 14 days. It is 'more than' because the
     paper says more than 100 a day."
   - Recurring: yes (a bound whose direction is lost; SELFCHECK 13, Part D C24 and C25).
   - Fixer: P7 prompt now says Hall "put the uncertainty ... at more than 100" and asks what it becomes over 14 days; answer is "More than 1,400", with the one-average-rate reason pointed at `S01-R1-C03` (its FAO/WHO/UNU quote: DLW measures "the average total energy expenditure"); the same-way-every-day sentence is gone.
   - Verifier: closed
