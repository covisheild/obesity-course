1. **error** · `definition.text` last paragraph, `illustration.analogy_breaks_when` and practice
   10 (level 6) (from compression hole 5a; confirmed).
   - Claim: "A probability distribution is a model." against "the distribution is exact only
     because the whole village was listed". Practice 10 asks "say which is the model and which is
     the estimate".
   - A distribution got by listing the whole population is called both a model and exact, and
     the reader cannot tell which word governs.
   - Fix: definition, replace "A probability distribution is a model." with "A probability
     distribution is a model of the chance process; a survey gives data. When the whole
     population is listed, as in the made-up village below, the model is known exactly."
     Practice 10 may then stand.
   - Recurring: yes (a sentence that contradicts another in the same section; S01-R1-C05 item 2).

2. **gap** · `definition.text`, "The values listed must not overlap." (from compression hole 5b;
   confirmed).
   - Single numbers cannot overlap; only bands can, as `must_know[1]` and practice 13 show.
     OpenIntro's rule is about outcomes ("The outcomes listed must be disjoint").
   - Fix: "The values, or bands of values, listed must not overlap."

3. **gap** · practice 9 (level 5), "Work out the range of X" (from compression hole 5c; confirmed,
   reason corrected).
   - The hole says "range" clashes with C09's range of a function. It does not: X is a rule, and
     the set of values it gives is its range in C09's sense. The clash is with Book 0: `B0-R0-C27`
     defines the range as one number, and Book 0's style ruling M6 keeps it so (largest minus smallest), so a reader
     can answer "0.2" and be marked wrong against the answer's "−0.1 kg to 0.1 kg".
   - Fix: "Work out the interval of values X can take".
   - Recurring: yes ("range" for an interval; Book 0 C28, V5-1).

4. **style** · `definition.text`, the continuous paragraph.
   - Claim: "A random variable is continuous when it can take any value in an interval ... The
     probability of any one exact value is then 0."
   - "Then" makes the zero follow from "can take any value in an interval", which it does not (a
     variable can take every value in an interval and still put probability on one value). It
     follows from the probabilities being areas under a density.
   - Fix: "... like a weight measured with perfect precision, and its probabilities are areas
     under a curve. The probability of any one exact value is then 0." (and drop "instead" from
     the next sentence).
