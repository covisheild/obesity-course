"can overestimate the BMR by 10 -12%" and the two 5% reductions. The matrix rules match College
Algebra 2e §7.5 and Austin §2.2: the inner sizes must match, "AB = BA" is "*not* generally true",
and Ax is a combination of A's columns.

1. **error** · `practice[10].answer` (P11, level 7). **Recurring** (an unsourced claim about
   the world; Book 0 C24, C27, C29, C30; S01-R1-C07, C08).
   - Claim: "45,008 kcal/day is more than ten times any adult's daily energy use."
   - Ten times 4,500.8 kcal/day is 45,008. Some adults do spend more than 4,500 kcal a day, for
     example endurance athletes in heavy training. No held source bounds "any adult". The
     book's own sourced ceiling is ICMR-NIN Table 1a/1b: 3470 kcal/d for men doing heavy work.
   - Fix: "45,008 kcal/day is more than twelve times the 3,470 kcal/day ICMR-NIN gives for a man
     doing heavy work." (45008/3470 = 12.97.) Add `icmr_nin_2020_brief` to the problem's refs.

2. **error** · `simplified_explanation`, first paragraph.
   - Claim: "A matrix is a table of numbers with the labels taken off. Each row is one case. Each
     column is one kind of measurement."
   - This is true of a data matrix, not of every matrix. In the section's own illustration, the
     rows of B are equations, and the rows of C are "multiplies body weight" and "multiplies the
     constant 1". So the plain-terms version contradicts the illustration two paragraphs later.
   - Fix: "A matrix is a table of numbers with the labels taken off. In a data matrix, each row is
     one case and each column is one kind of measurement, always in the same place."

3. **gap** · `illustration.body`, the last paragraph, with `must_know[3].point`.
   - Claim: the illustration ends "The two readings give two scalars." It never works them out.
     They first appear as bare numbers in `must_know[3]`: "whether the scalar is 0.90 or 0.9025".
     The reader has to derive them unaided, in a must-know point.
   - Fix: end the illustration with a working block, "1 minus 0.05 minus 0.05 = 0.90" and "0.95
     times 0.95 = 0.9025", and one sentence: "Multiplying XC by 0.90 or by 0.9025 gives the two
     sets of reduced predictions."

4. **gap** · `illustration.body`, "Now three men, of 52, 65 and 80 kg." **Recurring** (made-up
   figure not said to be made up; SELFCHECK 3; S01-R1-C08).
   - 65 kg is ICMR-NIN's figure. 52 and 80 are made up, and the text does not say so. P8 does say
     it, for its own figures: "the 45 and 70 are made up".
   - Fix: "Now three men, of 52, 65 and 80 kg; the 52 and 80 are made up."

5. **style** · `illustration.body`, "That is the reference man of the Indian Council of Medical
   Research ... in its 2020 requirements", and P7 "ICMR-NIN's reference woman weighs 55.0 kg".
   - The brief note does not call the adults of Table 1b "reference" adults. It uses "reference"
     only for children, in footnote c. It gives 65.0 and 55.0 as "Body weights" in the rows for
     men and women.
   - Fix: "That is the body weight ICMR-NIN's 2020 requirements use for an adult man." Make the
     same change for the woman in P7.

6. **style** · `illustration.body`, the first sentences: "Three United Nations bodies wrote a
   joint report on it in 2004. It predicts BMR from body weight ..."
   - "it" refers back to BMR, but the report is on human energy requirements, not on BMR. The
     second "It" has to mean the report. So one pronoun has the wrong referent, and the reader
     meets two pronouns in a row with different referents.
   - Fix: "Three United Nations bodies wrote a joint report on human energy requirements in 2004.
     The report predicts BMR from body weight ..."

7. **style** · `must_know[2].point`.
   - Claim: "Leave it [the column of 1s] out of a data matrix, and every prediction silently loses
     the intercept."
   - As matrix arithmetic, this is not silent. With X being n × 1 and b = (15.057, 692.2), the
     product Xb does not exist, which the section's own size rule catches. The intercept is lost
     silently only when b is shortened too, or when a spreadsheet takes a dot product over a
     shorter range.
   - Fix: "Leave it out of a data matrix, and the sizes no longer fit. Shorten the coefficients to
     make them fit, and every prediction silently loses the intercept."

8. **style** · notation, `illustration.body` "Write his vector as w = (65, 1)". This is a
   notation drift across sections.
   - The book writes body weight as W (DRAFT-BRIEF, "Notation"). Here lower-case w is a vector
     whose first entry is a body weight. C10 uses w for a general vector (cv + dw), and C12 uses w
     for a weight on the column of 1s (P5) and w1 ... wk for weights. On this page, "w" beside
     "body weight" is easy to read as W.
   - Fix: name the man's vector x, as the definition names a vector ("A matrix A times a vector
     x"): "Write his vector as x = (65, 1)", and "Bx" for "Bw" in the text and in the caption of
     `s02-r1-c11-column-view.png`. (X is already the data matrix. If x beside X is thought too
     close, use m for "man".)

9. **style** · `figures[0].caption`, `figures[1].caption` (placement).
