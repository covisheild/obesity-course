record prints `ΔEI(t) = −kP × ΔBW(t)`, which is right apart from the subscript form (item 12). The

- 1.40 / 1.53 = 0.91503, so the fall is 8.497%, "about 8.5%" as the record says.
- Table 5.2's MJ and kcal columns agree:
  - Men: 0.063 × 239.0 = 15.06, and 2.896 MJ = 692.2 kcal.
  - Women: 0.062 MJ = 14.82 kcal, and 2.036 MJ = 486.6 kcal.
- Hall's rule, 10 kcal/d per lb, is 92 kJ/d per kg. That fits the paper's "~100 kJ/d per kg".
- kI's unit: kcal/d divided by (kg·d) is kcal/(kg·d²), so "1 kcal a day per kg per day" is
  right.
