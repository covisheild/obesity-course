1. **gap** · `definition.text`, the test paragraph, and `illustration.body` (from compression
   hole 17, with the residue of B-C12-2).
   - Claim: "With more, look for rows in which every column but one is zero, because each such row
     gives one weight on its own."
   - The illustration's third weight comes from row 3, which is not such a row: (10, 20, 60). It
     is found by putting in the two weights already known, so that one unknown is left. The rule
     as written does not cover the step the section itself takes. Book 0 C3 (`B0-R0-C17`) teaches
     two unknowns only. The book never says that it solves more than two only this way.
   - Fix: "With more, look for a row in which only one weight is still unknown. A row where every
     column but one is zero gives that weight at once. A row with the other weights already found
     gives the last one. This book solves more than two weights only this way."

2. **gap** · `definition.text`, the first four paragraphs (compression residue; §10 rule 5).
   - (a) Compression cut the original's opening sentence, which tied the section to C11 ("Take a
     table whose rows are cases and whose columns are variables, as `S02-R1-C11` sets one up"). It
     also cut the sentence linking the definition to y = Xb. So the section no longer says what it
     stands on.
   - (b) "A column of 1s counts as a column." now sits directly before "Such a column is
     redundant". "Such a column" therefore reads as the column of 1s. The example that explained
     the 1s ("a column y that equals 2 times a, plus 5 ... with weights 2 and 5") was cut.
   - Fix: restore the original's first sentence word for word. Replace "Such a column is
     redundant" with "A column that is a weighted sum of others is redundant". Restore the example
     sentence after "A column of 1s counts as a column."

3. **gap** · `illustration.body`, the test on the adults' table. **Recurring** (a derivation
   skips; SELFCHECK 9; S01-R1-C03).
   - Compression cut two working blocks. After "The u terms cancel." the reader gets "So 0.5 times
     v is 3.0, and v is 6." and never sees 2.0 − 1.5, 12.0 − 9.0 or 3.0 ÷ 0.5. After "Put v back
     into row 1." they get "So 6.0 times u is 0" without seeing 1.5 × 6 = 9 or 9.0 − 9 = 0. §4
     says every line is shown.
   - Fix: restore the original's two blocks word for word. The first is "2.0 minus 1.5 = 0.5 /
     12.0 minus 9.0 = 3.0 / 3.0 divided by 0.5 = 6". The second is "1.5 times 6 = 9 / 9.0 minus 9
     = 0".

4. **gap** · `definition.text` last paragraph but one, `exercises[0].prompt`, `practice[12]`
   (P13) and `must_know[5].point` (from compression hole 3).
   - Claim: "entered together as predictors", "fat intake had no effect on weight gain", "found no
     effect of fat once calories were accounted for", and "the model".
   - "Model" and "predicted value" come from C11 (y = Xb). "Predictor" is first defined in the

   - Fix: add to the definition, after the paragraph on moving weight: "A study that predicts an

     prediction for one more unit of that column, with every other column held fixed, as in
     `S02-R1-C10`."

6. **error** · `practice[13].answer` (P14).
   - Claim: "Take a copy without ash, or without water. Its carbohydrate column is not a weighted
     sum of what is left."
   - That is not true in every case. Without ash, carbohydrate is a weighted sum of the columns that
     remain exactly when ash is itself a weighted sum of them, and that can happen. The section's
     own test is the way to find out, and the answer asserts the result without running it.
   - Fix: "... Its carbohydrate column is then, in general, not a weighted sum of what is left.
     Run the test before you drop it."

7. **error** · `definition.text`, last paragraph.
   - Claim: "A column that is nearly, but not exactly, a weighted sum of others is the problem
     called collinearity."
   - In standard usage, the exact case is collinearity too. It is called perfect (or exact)
     collinearity, and it is the case this section is about. §11 rule 10 holds `definition.text`
     to "a specialist has to find nothing in it to correct". As written, the sentence tells the
     reader that the section's own case is not collinearity.
   - Fix: "An exact weighted sum, as here, is called perfect collinearity. A column that is nearly,
     but not exactly, a weighted sum of others is the harder problem that the next rung calls
     collinearity."

8. **style** · `practice[7]` (P8, level 5). This is a notation drift from C01 and C05.
   - Claim: "ES is how fast the body's energy stores change. ... EO is the energy spent, which this
     book writes EE." The table headings read "EO (kcal/day)" and "ES (kcal/day)", and the answer
     ends "ES is EI minus EO".
   - C01 and C05 set the book's letters. There ES is the store, in kcal, and Hall's rate is
     dES/dt. C01's own words are "In this book's letters Hall's [equation is] dES/dt = EI minus
     EE". This problem translates EO but not ES. So a reader of C01 meets ES used for a rate
     without warning.
   - Fix: keep Hall's letters, since the problem is about his equation, and add one sentence:
     "Hall's ES is the rate that this book writes dES/dt." Head the columns "EI", "EE" and "dES/dt"
     (all kcal/day), and end the answer "dES/dt is EI minus EE, a weighted sum."
