1. **`simplified_explanation`, "Two symbols side by side are multiplied." and "Side by side means
   multiply, except for a subscript."**
   - **Claim:** side by side always means multiply, and a subscript is the only exception. P9's
     answer repeats it: "A subscript is the one exception."
   - **What the source and the record say:** the section's own symbols break the rule. EI, EE, ES,
     BW, UGE are several letters that make one name. Δδ is "the change in δ", not Δ times δ. d in
     dBW_i/dt does not multiply. The section itself says f(x) is not f times x. Polidori's
     Equation 1 is exactly where a reader applying the rule as written reads BW as B times W.
   - **Smallest fix:** replace "Side by side means multiply, except for a subscript." with one
     sentence naming the exceptions the reader meets here. For example: "Side by side means
     multiply, with exceptions: a name of several letters (EI, BW, UGE), Δ or d in front of a
     symbol, a subscript, and a rule name before brackets. The 'where' clause tells you which."
     In P9's answer, change "A subscript is the one exception" to "A subscript is one of the
     exceptions."
   - **Tag:** error. **Recurring** (SELFCHECK 5: the record teaches a rule its own example breaks).

2. **`illustration.body`, Equation 1's "ε(BW_i − BW_0)" against Exercise 1's "ΔEI(t)"**
   - **Claim:** the plain-terms text teaches that brackets after a name are function notation:
     "f(x) is the output of a rule called f, not f times x".
   - **What the record does:** in Equation 1, ε(BW_i − BW_0) is ε **times** the bracket (P9's answer
     says "ε times the change in weight"). In Exercise 1, ΔEI(t) is function notation. The two look
     the same, and the section never says how to tell them apart.
   - **Smallest fix:** one sentence after the f(x) sentence: "A letter before brackets can mean
     either. If the paper calls the letter a parameter, a fixed number, it multiplies. If the
     brackets hold the input, such as t, it is a rule's output."
   - **Tag:** gap. **Recurring** (SELFCHECK 14: a reading the reader needs is never taught).

3. **`illustration.body` and its symbol table: "Search the Methods for the sentence that says what
   β is. If you cannot find one, write 'not found'", and the row "| β | beta | not found |"**
   - **Claim:** the paper does not say what β is.
   - **What the source says:** the held file is excerpts and leaves the paragraph out. The full
     Methods (PMC full text) says: "The parameter β accounts for the adaptation of energy
     expenditure during a diet perturbation, ΔEI, and was determined using data from eight human

   - **Smallest fix:** source intake first: add that Methods paragraph (β, α, η, and the UGE
     sentence of item 4) to `polidori_2016.txt`. Then the β row reads "the adaptation of energy
     expenditure during a diet change" with its quote. Until then, change the row to "not in the
     passage quoted here" and cut "If you cannot find one, write 'not found'". Needs Harsh or the
     conductor for the intake.
   - **Tag:** error. **Recurring** (SELFCHECK 1: a claim about what a source says that the held
     excerpt cannot support).

4. **`illustration.body`, "Before you put 90 into this equation, find that conversion."**
   (from compression hole A-C01-4)
   - **Claim:** the reader should find the conversion from grams of glucose to energy.
   - **What the source says:** confirmed. The held file gives no conversion and no energy per gram
     of glucose. The full Methods says the UGE term "represents the energy losses as a result of
     increased urinary excretion of glucose with canagliflozin treatment" and that "Model parameter
     values are given in Table 1". So the paper says the UGE term is an energy, and that is what
     the unit check predicts. The converted value is in Table 1, which is not held.
   - **Smallest fix:** reword as something to look for: "The paper says the UGE term is the energy
     lost in the glucose. Its values are in the paper's Table 1. Find the conversion there before
     you put 90 in." Quote the "energy losses" sentence once item 3's intake is done. Also use
     "about" once near "~90 g/day" (conductor's figure note 6).
   - **Tag:** gap.

5. **Symbol-table row "| dBW_i/dt | ... | rate of change of body weight, interval i | kg a day |"
   and practice 7, "The interval length was t = (N−1)*T"** (from compression hole A-C01-3)
   - **Claim:** d-notation, taught in Book 0 (`B0-R0-C21`) as the rate at an instant.
   - **What the source says:** confirmed. Polidori: "the moving average of the measured body
     weight time course was used to calculate the rate of change of body weight over each
     interval, dBWi/dt". It is an average over a 52-day interval. In practice 7, t is a length of

   - **Smallest fix:** add to the illustration: "Polidori's dBW_i/dt is an average rate over each
     interval, worked out from a moving average of the weighings, not a rate at one instant." In
     practice 7's answer, add: "Here t is a length of time, not a moment."
   - **Tag:** gap. **Recurring** (SELFCHECK 7: one symbol, two meanings, not flagged).
