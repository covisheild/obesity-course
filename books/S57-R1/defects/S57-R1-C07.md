**Design and timing, checked against the held texts.**

  graduate students, 48 items, four test devices each. Study 2: 33 Yale undergraduates. Study 6:
  31 undergraduates, explicit warning, the drop smaller but still significant. Study 8: 37 Yale
  undergraduates, "cookbook" procedures, no drop. All within one session. The record states the

1. **error** · `must_know[5].point`: a figure for one technique applied to both, contradicted by the
   section's own example.
   - Claim: "Explaining costs time: 30% to 100% more in the studies that reported it."
   - The source says that for self-explanation only, and only for self-paced studies: "In the few
     studies reporting time on task, self-paced administration usually yielded nontrivial increases
     (30–100%)". The section's own elaborative-interrogation example cost 4 minutes on 28, which is
     about 14%, below the stated range. The `verified.note` on `references[29]` says the point
     reads "in one study", but it does not. The note is stale.
   - Fix: "Self-explanation took 30% to 100% more time in the few self-paced studies that reported
     it; the 'why' prompts in the biology class took 4 minutes more on 28. Weigh fewer problems
     explained against more problems solved." Update the note.
   - **Recurring** (SELFCHECK 4a and 13: a figure used outside the group it was measured on; the
     record contradicts itself, SELFCHECK 5).
   - Fixer: must_know[5] now says self-explanation, few self-paced studies, and gives the biology class's 32 minutes against 28 beside it; references[29] note rewritten (source checked: "In the few studies reporting time on task, self-paced administration usually yielded nontrivial increases (30–100%)").
