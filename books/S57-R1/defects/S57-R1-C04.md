2. **error** · `definition.text`, paragraph 5 (from compression hole H-17)
   - Claim: "So the best gap grew with the retention interval, but its share of the retention
     interval fell."
   - The gaps tested do not show this. The best gap is 21 days at both 70 and 350 days, so it does
     not grow there. Its share rises from 14% (1/7) to 31% (11/35) before it falls to 30% and 6%.
     The monotone statement is a property of the authors' **fitted surface**: "Third, as RI
     increases, the optimal gap should increase ... Fourth, as RI increases, the ratio of optimal gap
     to RI must decline". Those are constraints the function was built to satisfy. The illustration's
     "Two things happen at once. The best gap grows ..." repeats the error, and its own
     analogy_breaks_when ("the share jumps from 14% to 31% and back down to 6%") contradicts it.
   - Fix: "Across the four retention intervals the share of the best gap tested was 14%, 31%, 30%
     and 6%: rough, because only six or seven gaps were tried. The authors' fitted curve smooths this
     into a best gap that grows while its share falls."
   - Recurring (SELFCHECK 4a: a finding stated beyond what was measured; SELFCHECK 5).

3. **gap** · `must_know[3].point`, and the conductor's figure note on `best-gap` (from compression hole
   H-16)
   - Claim: "The best gap grows as the test moves away, but its share falls: 23 days, 7%, for a test
     350 days away."
   - Not a contradiction but an unlabelled switch. 23 days / 7% is the fitted optimum. The tested
     best was 21 days / 6%, which is what the definition, P1 and the figure's points use. The figure
     label ("best tried 21, fitted best 23 (7%)") and illustration 1 already say which is which. The
     must-know, the part the reader keeps, does not. The same point also says "Dunlosky and
     colleagues give that fixed share", but Dunlosky gives 10-20% **as Cepeda 2008's finding**
     ("Cepeda, Vul, Rohrer, Wixted, and Pashler (2008) ... In brief, criterion performance was best
     when the lag between sessions was approximately 10–20%"). That is the more useful lesson: a
     review misreporting its primary source.
   - Fix: "... its share falls: on the authors' fitted curve, 23 days, 7%, for a test 350 days away
     (21 days was the best they tried). Dunlosky and colleagues summarise this study as a fixed
     '10 to 20%'. The study's own data bend away from that summary."
   - Recurring (SELFCHECK 1: a secondary source's reading stands in for the primary).

4. **gap** · `definition.text`, paragraph 5, and `simplified_explanation` (from compression hole H-18,
   first part)
   - Claims: "It was about 20% to 30% for a test a few weeks away. For a test a year away it was about
     5% to 7%."
   - Each end has a source, but none is named: 20% and 5% are the abstract's rounding, 30-31% the
     best tested at 35 and 70 days, 6% the best tested at 350, 7% the fitted value. The "20% to 30%"
     planning window is the book's own synthesis, and the 30% number's quote is a design sentence

     week away. The one data point there is 14%, below the window.
   - Fix: "The abstract rounds the best share to about 20% for tests a few weeks away and about 5%
     for a year. The best gaps tested were 31% and 30% at 35 and 70 days, and 14% at 7 days. This
     book uses 20% to 30% as a planning window for tests a few weeks away." Give the 7-day figure in
     the table's note.
   - Recurring (SELFCHECK 1 and 4a).

5. **gap** · `definition.text`, last paragraph; `exercises[1]` answer; P7 and P10 (from compression hole
   H-18, second part)
   - Claim: "At the best gap, final recall was 64% higher than with no gap."
   - This does not say at which retention interval, or that the 64% is relative. The manuscript's
     per-interval figures are 10, 59, 111 and 77%, and their mean is 64.25% (Python), so 64% appears

     tie it back to the definition.
   - Fix: "Averaged over the four retention intervals, recall at the best gap was 64% higher than
     with no gap, a relative increase. It ranged from 10% for a test a week away to 111% for 70
     days."
   - Recurring (SELFCHECK 4a; SELFCHECK 13, a figure for the whole applied to a part).

6. **gap** · `exercises[1]` prompt (from compression hole H-20)
   - Claim: "Explain the mechanism and the evidence ..."
   - No mechanism is taught. The answer can only say "why it works is still debated". The held
     sources carry one: Dunlosky 9.1 names deficient processing ("the processing of material during
     a second learning opportunity suffers when it is close in time to the original learning
     episode"), reminding and consolidation as "accounts currently under debate". Cepeda's Theoretical
     Implications says deficient-processing theories "would not seem to fit well" over gaps of days
     and weeks.
   - Fix: add one sentence to illustration 1 or the definition: "Why spacing works is debated. One

     is and is not known about why it works."
   - Recurring (SELFCHECK 14: an exercise asks for what the section never taught).
