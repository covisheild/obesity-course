4. **`definition.text` and `must_know[0]`, "alone", cited to Agarwal's inclusion rule**
   - **Claim:** a retrieval session has learners answering "each alone and in writing", resting on
     "Individual, not collaborative: All students engaged in retrieval practice individually under
     the supervision of researchers and instructors".
   - **What the source says:** that line is the review's rule for which studies it counted as
     classroom research, not a finding that answering alone works better. The one study the review
     describes on the point, Sennhenn-Kirchner et al. (2016), found that "students in the
     collaborative retrieval group outperformed students who had engaged in retrieval practice
     individually". Agarwal and colleagues excluded such designs because "the extent to which all
     students engaged in retrieval practice was not measured". The record's reason (every learner
     must do the recalling) matches theirs, but the quote reads as evidence that alone is better.
   - **Smallest fix:** in the two `verified.note`s: "An inclusion criterion of the review, not a
     finding. The review's own example found pairs did better, but could not tell whether both
     partners recalled." In must_know[0] keep the instruction and give the reason in the sentence:
     "Ask a question every learner answers alone, in writing, so that you know every learner did
     the recalling." "In writing" is the book's own addition; the criterion says closed-book, not
     written.
   - **Tag:** gap. **Recurring** (SELFCHECK 1).
