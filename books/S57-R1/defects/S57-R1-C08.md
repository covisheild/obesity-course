# Audit defects · S57-R1-C08 · Working memory is small: cognitive load, and effort that helps

Auditor: Task 3, batch a2, 25 Sep 2026. Field indices are 0-based. "Recurring" means the kind is on
`check/SELFCHECK.md` or appears in `books/B0/defects/`, `books/S01-R1/defects/` or
`books/S02-R1/defects/`.

**Quotes.** All 30 `definition.references` quotes and all 3 `illustrations[].numbers` quotes were
found in `sweller_2019_cognitive_load`, `weinstein_2018_science_of_learning` and
`dunlosky_2013_learning_techniques`, each read in its context. The quotes cited for numbers state
them (1 week; 24% and 12%). No quote was edited.

**Design and timing, checked against the held texts.**
- Chandler and Sweller (1991), the redundancy effect: diagram alone better than diagram plus
  repeating text. The design is not given in the held review. The record claims no more than the
  result.
- Rohrer and Taylor (2007), via Weinstein: college students, volume problems, test 1 week later.
  The record says "college students ... a week later". Correct.
- Woloshyn, Pressley and Schneider (1992), via Dunlosky: Canadian and German students, elaborative
  interrogation against reading only (Fig. 1 caption, held), a 24% against 12% increase. Whether
  these are points or relative is not stated. The `analogy_breaks_when` says so.
- The effects in Sweller's review mostly rest on randomised trials that "did not attempt to
  directly measure comparative cognitive load". `must_know[5]` turns this into the right boundary.

**Arithmetic.** Recomputed in Python: 26/40 = 0.65 and 65%; 18/24 = 0.75 and 75%; 31/50 = 62%;
24/12 = 2. All correct. `quantitative: false`, as the inventory row says. The percentages are
Book 0 A4's.

**Figures.** `knowledge-and-why` (24, 12; ratio 2) matches the table. `faded-help` (2, 1, 0 steps

stands. See item 11.

**Amendments.** `S57-R1-S1` (run a session built around retrieval rather than transmission) is the

1. **error** · `simplified_explanation`, paragraph 1: two claims no held source makes.
   - Claim: "It can juggle only a few new things at once, and it drops them within seconds."
   - Sweller says only "a limited number of information elements" and "capacity and duration
     limited". He gives no number, which the record's own note on `references[0]` points out. "A
     few" is a number of sorts. "Within seconds" is said in the source of *transient information*
     (speech that "disappears after a few seconds"), not of working memory.
   - Fix: "It can juggle only a limited number of new things at once, and only for a short time.
     The review behind this section gives no number for either."
   - **Recurring** (SELFCHECK 1 and 4; §7b: written from what the model knows).

2. **gap** · `definition.text`, paragraph 2, and `retrieval_items[1]`: the second source of load is
   never named.
   - Claim: "Cognitive load theory ... separates two sources of it. Extraneous load comes from ..."
     Retrieval item 2's answer uses "Intrinsic load".
   - Only one of the "two sources" is named. "Intrinsic" never appears in the reader's text. The
     plain-terms paragraph says "Some of the load comes from the material itself" without the term.
   - Fix: "Intrinsic load comes from the material itself and from how much the learner already
     knows; it changes only if what is taught or the learner's knowledge changes. Extraneous load
     ..." (backed by `references[4]` and `references[5]`).
   - **Recurring** (SELFCHECK 14).

3. **gap** · `simplified_explanation` and `must_know[1-2]` (from compression hole 29; confirmed,
   with a sourced fix).
   - Claim: "For a beginner, a problem shown worked out ... often teaches more than being made to
     solve it cold." and "Once the basics are held, effort helps."
   - Nothing reconciles this with C03 (retrieval for every learner) and C01 (effort is part of how
     learning works). The record already holds the reconciling passage and never uses it: "low
     element interactivity material in which students had to learn mathematical definitions
     yielded a reverse worked example effect. Students who were required to generate an appropriate
     response learned more than students who were shown the correct response" (`references[17]`).
     On when a learner stops being a novice: the source gives no criterion. Illustration 1's
     `analogy_breaks_when` already says to find out by watching whether they can do the bare
     problem.
   - Fix: add to `definition.text` after the worked-example sentence: "This holds for problems
     with many steps that depend on each other. For single facts and definitions, being made to
     produce the answer taught more than being shown it: retrieval practice (section 3) still
     applies to beginners."
   - **Recurring** (SELFCHECK 5 and 7: sections in tension).

4. **gap** · `must_know[0].point` against `definition.text` and the exercise (from compression hole
   30; confirmed).
   - Claim: "Put the steps where they stay: on the board, a chart on the wall, a handout." against
     "A diagram alone has taught more than the same diagram with text that repeats it."
   - The reader cannot tell whether written steps beside a labelled picture are the harmful
     redundancy. The exercise answer asks for both, a wall chart of steps and "keep the picture and
     drop the repeating paragraph".
   - Fix: add to `must_know[0]`: "Written steps that add what the picture cannot show (the order,
     what to check) are not redundant; a paragraph that repeats the labels is."
   - **Recurring** (SELFCHECK 5).
