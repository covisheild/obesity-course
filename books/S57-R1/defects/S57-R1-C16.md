# Audit defects · S57-R1-C16 · Words for how a team works

Auditor: Task 3 (batch a4), 25 Sep 2026. Field indices are 0-based.

**Quotes.** All 19 quotes (15 `definition.references`, 4 illustration `numbers`) were found verbatim
in `kotter_1990_what_leaders_do.txt`, with ligatures and extraction hyphens ("An-other") kept. Each
number quote states its number (12; "80 to 100"; "1,500"). None was edited.

**What Kotter does and does not say, checked against the held text.**
- The six activity definitions are fair paraphrases of pp. 4-5.
- "Kotter holds that an organisation needs both, each balancing the other" matches "use each to
  balance the other".
- The Kodak box supports these: 12 direct reports weekly; 80 to 100 people monthly "to discuss
  anything they wanted"; "business meetings" of "12 to 20 people on a specific topic" with the goal
  of all 1,500 employees each year; the copy products group from 1984.
- Illustration 1's row "Asked the tutors how they would run the next round" as motivating is
  supported: "Leaders also regularly involve people in deciding how to achieve the organization's
  vision".
- Kotter does **not** define authority, power, delegation matched to the person, role clarity, or
  the two kinds of conflict. The note on references[14] says so.

**Arithmetic.** 1500 ÷ 100 = 15 (figure check). Correct as arithmetic; see item 2.

**Amendments.**
- `S57-R2-A01` (bridge): all elements appear at vocabulary level. That covers direction, aligning
  and earning trust (credibility) against planning, staffing, budgeting and control, plus power
  against authority, delegation matched to competence and commitment, role clarity, and work
  against person conflict. But "conflict ... as normal and workable" is given as settled (item 3).
- `S57-R2-A02` (bridge): who sets direction, how work is delegated, and where decisions stall ("Where

- `S57-R1-A01`: the leading and managing distinction is served in part; C15 carries the rest.

## Defects

1. **definition.text, paragraphs 5 to 8; simplified_explanation.** The definitions of authority,
   power, delegation ("matched to that person's competence and commitment for that task"), role
   clarity and the two kinds of conflict are given without a source. They sit in the same voice as
   Kotter's sourced definitions just above them. The reader-facing text never says they are this
   book's own. The audit brief requires that label. Fix: open paragraph 5 with "The rest are this
   book's working definitions, not findings; the next book in this subject gives the evidence on
   each." **error**, **recurring** (a working definition passed off as sourced)
   Fixer: paragraph 5 now opens "The words below are this book's working definitions, not Kotter's, and not findings. The next book in this subject gives the evidence on each."; authority, delegation, role clarity and conflict each open "In this book, …" (decision 5), as does the plain-terms authority line.

   caption says "(drawn at 100)", so the conductor's note is closed. But the figure sets
   *per-meeting* counts (12 a week; 80 to 100 a month) beside a *yearly cumulative* goal (1,500). The
   alt text reads "1,500, fifteen times the monthly bar". Over a year the monthly forums alone reach
   12 × 80 = 960 to 12 × 100 = 1,200 people. So "fifteen times" compares one meeting with a year,
   and suggests a gap the source does not show. The caption's last sentence ("Aligning talks to
   many more people than organizing does") is Kotter's, but the figure shows only aligning
   meetings, with no organizing count to compare against. Fix: either plot people reached in a year
   (12; 960 to 1,200; 1,500, with the monthly bar as a range, derived under `derived`), or drop the
   "fifteen times" wording and the y[2] ÷ y[1] check. Add "Kotter's sentence" before the last
   caption line. **error**
   Fixer: took the second option. Dropped the `derived` 15 and the y[2] ÷ y[1] relation; x labels now "each weekly meeting" / "each monthly meeting" / "yearly goal"; caption says the first two bars count one meeting, the third a year, the monthly bar is "drawn at the upper end, 100", and the last line is prefixed "Kotter's sentence:"; alt drops "fifteen times". 3. **exercises[1].answer item 3; illustrations[2].body; must_know[6].** Claims: work conflict "is
   worth having", and "is what a journal club is for". Keep it "in the open". The book gives no
   source for the claim that disagreement about the work helps a team. That is an empirical claim,
   and it is contested. A meta-analysis often cited on it (De Dreu and Weingart, *J Appl Psychol*
   2003; not held, not verified here) reported that task conflict, like relationship conflict, went
   with lower team performance on average. Fix: state it as the book's working stance, with no
   benefit claimed. "Disagreement about the work is normal, and it can be settled on the evidence.
   Whether it helps a team is for the next book." Then either get and quote the evidence, or cut
   "worth having". **error** (a finding asserted with no source; SELFCHECK 4)
   Fixer: cut "It is worth having" (exercise 2, item 3) and "it is what a journal club is for" (now "to be settled on the evidence"); must_know[6] now reads "This book's stance: settle a disagreement about the work on the evidence. Whether such disagreement helps a team is for the next book." De Dreu and Weingart not held, so not cited.

4. **must_know[0].** "They are two sets of activities, and most people who run anything must do
   both." "They" has no referent inside the point, because the must-know list prints apart from
   the definition. "Most people who run anything" is a frequency claim Kotter does not make: he says
   "not everyone can be good at both" and that companies "try to develop leader-managers". The
   simplified explanation says the same thing ("Most of the time one person has to do both").
   Fix: "Leading and managing are two sets of activities. Kotter argues that anyone running a
   change needs both done, by one person or by a pair." **error** (the pronoun), **recurring**
   Fixer: must_know[0] now "Leading and managing are two sets of activities. Kotter argues that an organisation needs both done, by one person or by different people on one team." (held text: "not everyone can be good at both"; "Smart companies value both kinds of people and work hard to make them a part of the team"; the auditor's "by a pair"/"running a change" is not Kotter's wording). Deleted "Most of the time one person has to do both" from the simplified explanation.
