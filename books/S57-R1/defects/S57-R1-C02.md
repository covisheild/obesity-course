1. **error** · `definition.text`, paragraph 3 (from compression hole H-5)
   - Claim: "Its sign says which group did better, so you always state which group was subtracted
     from which."
   - The sign says which group scored higher. Whether higher is better depends on the scale. P10
     (mistakes counted) is built on exactly this, and so is Cochrane MECIR C61 ("sometimes lower
     scores reflect 'better' outcome"), which the record quotes. As it stands, the definition
     teaches the error that P10 asks the reader to catch.
   - Fix: "Its sign says which group scored higher; whether higher is better depends on the scale.
     So you always state which group was subtracted from which, and which way the score runs."
   - Recurring (SELFCHECK 5: the record contradicts itself).

2. **gap** · `illustrations[1].body`, the Freeman paragraph, and `must_know[4]`
   - Claim: "It pools 158 comparisons of exam scores between active learning and lecturing. The
     overall SMD is 0.47."
   - Freeman's 0.47 is for "examinations, concept inventories, and other assessments" (Results).
     The abstract says "examinations and concept inventories". The paper also reports that effects
     were larger on concept inventories than on course examinations. "Exam scores" narrows what was
     pooled and so inflates what an exam-setting teacher may expect.
   - Fix: "158 comparisons of scores on exams, concept inventories and similar tests". Add to
     must_know[4]: "It was larger on concept inventories than on course exams."
   - Recurring (SELFCHECK 1 and 4a).

3. **gap** · `must_know[4].point` and `illustrations[1].body` (from compression hole H-6)
   - Claims: "Freeman and colleagues pooled 158 comparisons of active learning against lecturing
     ..." and "It pools 158 comparisons".
   - "Active learning" is never defined, and nor is "pool". C01 defines only transmission. Freeman
     holds a definition: "Active learning engages students in the process of learning through
     activities and/or discussion in class, as opposed to passively listening to an expert."
   - Fix: quote that definition at the first "active learning" in C02, with its reference. Gloss
     "pooled" as "combined the results of 158 comparisons into one average".
   - Recurring (SELFCHECK 14).

4. **gap** · `illustrations[0].body` and P6: Deslauriers's groups (from compression hole H-8)
   - Claims: "It describes two groups of physics students who were allocated at random." P6: "Say
     what the two SMDs tell you about the random allocation."
   - What groups A and B were is never said. They are the two halves of a crossover (each group had
     one active class and one passive class, Table 2), not a new-method group and a comparison
     group. A reader following this section's template will take them for the latter. P6 also has
     no guide to how large a baseline SMD should worry you. The paper gives one: t tests with
     p = 0.94 and 0.59 on these rows, and its own verdict that the groups were "indistinguishable".
     The "72.02" with two decimals is how Table 1 prints it, so I withdraw that part of the hole.
   - Fix: add "Each group was later taught one topic actively and one by lecture; here you look only
     at how alike chance made them before that." In P6's answer, add that the authors tested these
     gaps and called the groups indistinguishable, and that at 0.12 SD a later gap has to be judged
     against it.
   - Recurring (SELFCHECK 4a, the design).

6. **gap** · `must_know[3].point` (from compression hole H-9, first part)

7. **gap** · `definition.text`, last paragraph, and P12 (from compression hole H-9, second part)
   - Claim: "One group tested before and after its teaching, with no comparison group, gives a

   - Nothing says why a pre-to-post SMD cannot be set beside a between-group SMD. It divides a

   - Fix: add "Divided by an SD it gives a number that looks like an SMD, but it measures one
     group's change over time, so it cannot be set beside an SMD between two groups."
   - Not recurring.
