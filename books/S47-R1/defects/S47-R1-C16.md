1. **`definition.text`, `illustrations[1].body`: the researcher's part is fixed as informing, against
   section 17**
   - Claim: "The researcher's part in it is to set out the options with the evidence for each, its
     uncertainty, and the judgements the choice still needs"; "The researcher's job here is not to pick
     between the colleagues."
   - The support is OpenStax 16.4 on policy *analysts* ("try to identify all the possible choices ...").
     Section 17 then says informing and campaigning are "Both ... legitimate" and shows a department openly
     campaigning. A reader of C16 learns that a researcher may not choose; C17 says they may, if they say so.
   - Smallest fix: "When the job is to inform, the researcher's part is to set out the options ..." and
     "When the note is meant to inform, the researcher's job here is not to pick ..."
   - **error** · **recurring** (a sentence contradicting another section; pre-empting a later one,
     SELFCHECK 4).
   Fixed: definition now "As an analyst, the researcher's part in it is to set out the options ..."; illustration 2 now "As the analyst here, your job is not to pick between the colleagues" (book-wide decision 5).
   Verified: closed

2. **`must_know[5]`: "Say how sure the evidence is, out loud, in the note."** (from compression hole H15)
   - Confirmed. No wording or scale for degrees of certainty is taught anywhere in the book. The point gives
     the reader an instruction they cannot carry out.
   - Smallest fix: add the words to use, from what this section shows: "Name the kind of evidence and its
     range: 'one modelling study, reported by others, projects 7% to 30%' is not 'several measured
     studies found'." No outside grading scheme (none is held).
   - **gap**.
   Fixed: must-know now "Say how sure the evidence is, in the note, by naming its kind and its range: 'one modelling study, reported by others, projects 7% to 30%' is not 'several measured studies found'." (registry key).
   Verified: closed

3. **`definition.text`: the definition of evidence-informed policymaking names no source in the text**
   (from compression hole H16)
   - Partly withdrawn. The definition carries reference numbers, and oxman_2009_support supports it
     ("its access and appraisal of evidence ... is both systematic and transparent"), so it is cited. The
     remaining point: Oxman et al. add "This does not imply that the overall process of policymaking will
     be systematic and transparent", and the definition's wording "a process in which" can be read as the
     whole process.
   - Smallest fix: "Oxman and colleagues use 'evidence-informed policymaking' for policymaking in which the
     evidence used, and the judgements made about it, are found and stated systematically and openly. The
     rest of the process need not be."
   - **style**.
   Fixed: now "Oxman and colleagues use 'evidence-informed policymaking' for policymaking in which the evidence used, and the judgements made about it, are found and stated systematically and openly. The rest of the process need not be."
   Verified: closed

4. **`simplified_explanation`: feasibility listed as a question evidence cannot settle, with no reason**
   (from compression hole H17)
   - Confirmed. Oxman et al. list "costs and the availability of resources" among things local evidence
     informs, and the section's own first list has "What does it cost?" as evidence-answerable. "Can it be
     done? That depends on ... the money" then sits under "cannot settle". The illustration's table marks
     Council agreement "no", which is political (cairney_oliver_2017: "without finding a politically
     feasible way to introduce them").
   - Smallest fix: "Evidence can inform this (costs, capacity, what the law allows), but whether the bodies
     with the power will commit it is a judgement."
   - **gap**.
   Fixed: feasibility bullet now "Evidence can inform it (costs, capacity, what the law allows), but whether the bodies with the power will commit to it is a judgement."
   Verified: closed

5. **`definition.text`: "Evidence is a set of facts, known through observation"**
   - Oxman et al.: "evidence concerns facts (actual or asserted) intended for use in support of a
     conclusion ... A fact, in turn, is something known through experience or observation." Dropping
     "(actual or asserted)" makes every offered fact sound established, which is the opposite of what the
     section later asks (state uncertainty).
   - Smallest fix: "Evidence is facts, actual or asserted, known through experience or observation, offered
     in support of a conclusion."
   - **style**.
   Fixed: definition and retrieval answer now "Evidence is facts, actual or asserted, known through experience or observation, offered in support of a conclusion."
   Verified: closed
