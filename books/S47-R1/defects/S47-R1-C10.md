1. **gap** · `illustrations[1].body`, "Ask the question from the definition: do you gain from one
   answer?"; also `exercises[0].prompt` ("disclosure of a conflict of interest").
   - The definition never defines a conflict of interest. It defines actor, interest, lobbying and
     disclosure. "Conflict of interest" is first defined in `simplified_explanation` ("You might gain
     from one answer ... Then you have a conflict of interest"), and the pointer sends the reader to
     a definition that does not contain the question.
   - Fix: add to `definition.text` after the disclosure paragraph: "A conflict of interest is what
     an adviser or researcher has when they stand to gain from one answer to the question they
     inform." (Ioannidis quote "with declared or undeclared financial or other conflicts of
     interest" already carries it.)
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 8 and 14: pointer to something not there; term used before defined).

2. **gap** · `definition.text`, "Lobbying is representing an organisation's case before government
   ..."; `simplified_explanation`, "Researchers, which includes you" (from compression hole H-r2-16;
   confirmed).
   - The OpenStax key term is about "a person who represents an organization", so the definition
     matches its source, but the section then lists individuals (you) among those who push and
     tells them how to push ("a meeting, a letter, a comment on a draft"). The reader cannot tell
     whether a resident's letter is lobbying.
   - Fix: add one sentence to the definition: "When you put your own or your department's case to an
     official, you are doing the same thing; this book calls any such representation lobbying."
     Or say the reverse plainly. Either way, one rule.
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 7, one term for one thing).

4. **style** · `illustrations[1].body`, "The section on how a rule is made showed the same pattern for
   rules in general."
   - General Clauses Act s.23 applies only where an Act makes a rule-making power "subject to the
     condition of the rules or bye-laws being made after previous publication" (the record's own
     note). Not every rule is published in draft first; C07 illustration 2 shows section 91(1) rules
     without a draft. "For rules in general" overstates.
   - Fix: "... showed the same pattern for rules that an Act requires to be published in draft."
   - Recurring: yes (SELFCHECK 4: no conclusion the source does not license).
   - Fixed/Partly/Rejected:
   - Verified:

5. **style** · `exercises[2].answer`, "In India, a rule published in draft is open to objections from
   anyone likely to be affected".
   - Section 23(4) of the General Clauses Act says objections "from any person"; the FSSAI recital
     says "from the person likely to be affected". The answer narrows s.23 and broadens the FSSAI
     wording into a general rule.
   - Fix: "In India, when a rule must be published in draft, anyone may object before the date the
     draft names, and a company is one of them."
   - Recurring: no.
   - Fixed/Partly/Rejected:
   - Verified:
