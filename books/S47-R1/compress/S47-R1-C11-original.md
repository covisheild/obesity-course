# S47-R1-C11 · Finding who decides: the tracing method

**Definition.** Tracing who decides is a fixed sequence of six steps that takes a proposal to the body that can
turn it into policy, and to the power that body would use.

1. Name the instrument the proposal needs: an Act; a rule or regulation under an existing Act; a
   rate, order or other notification under an existing Act. Or say none is needed: an
   executive decision, such as a scheme, can act without a new law.
2. Find whose subject it is: the Union, the States or both. The answer comes from the Seventh
   Schedule under Article 246 of the Constitution, or from a provision of an Act that already
   covers the matter. Then find whether a function has been given to a local body.
3. Find the body that holds the power to make that instrument, and the numbered provision that
   gives it the power.
4. Find the process the instrument must pass before it binds, such as previous publication of a
   draft for objections, an approval, notification in the Gazette, commencement on a notified
   date, and laying before the legislature.
5. List every other body whose agreement the process requires, and the bodies or officers who
   carry the decision out.
6. State the result in one sentence: X decides, under Y, by process Z, with W's agreement.

A trace that the instruments in hand cannot complete stops at the step it cannot answer, and
names the candidate bodies and the document that would settle the step. A trace establishes who
can act and through which instrument. It does not establish whether that body will act, or
whether the instrument would work.

**In plain terms.** Each earlier section of this book answered one piece of a single question: for this proposal,
who would have to act, and under what power? This section puts the pieces in order, so you can run
them on any proposal.

**Step 1. Name the instrument.** What would have to exist on paper for the proposal to be policy?
It might be a new Act, or a rule or regulation under an Act that already exists. It might be a rate
or an order notified under an Act. Or it might be a decision by a government with no new law at
all, such as a scheme. If you cannot name one, you have a wish, not yet a proposal.

**Step 2. Find whose subject it is.** If an Act already covers the matter, read what it says about
itself. If not, find the entry in the Seventh Schedule's three lists. Then check whether the job
has been given to a panchayat or a municipality.

**Step 3. Find the body and the provision.** Which body can make that instrument? Which numbered
section or article gives it the power? Write both down. "The government" is not a body. "The
minister" is usually not the holder of the power.

**Step 4. Find the process.** What must happen before the instrument binds anyone? A draft
published for objections, an approval, a notification in the Gazette, a date when it comes into
force, laying before Parliament. Each one is a date you can chase.

**Step 5. List who else must agree, and who carries it out.** A body whose approval the process
needs is a second decider. The officers who enforce the decision are the people your proposal will
actually meet on the ground.

**Step 6. Write one sentence.** X decides, under Y, by process Z, with W's agreement.

Two things can happen on the way. The trace can stop, because the documents you have do not
answer a step. Then write down where it stopped, the candidates, and the document that would settle
it.

Or the trace can find that the decision was taken years ago. Then your proposal is really about
carrying the decision out, and it goes to different people.

A finished trace tells you who can act. It never tells you whether they will.

**Must know points for you.**

- The error is to name the minister who announced a measure as the body that decides it. A minister heads a department. The power to make an instrument usually sits with a body an Act names. Under the FSS Act, regulations are the Food Authority's to make, with the Central Government's approval. Address the note to the holder of the power, and copy the department.
- Write the one-sentence answer, "X decides, under Y, by process Z", before you write anything else in a note. If you cannot fill Y with a numbered provision, you have not traced the proposal yet, and the note is not ready to send.
- A press release, a speech or a meeting's recommendation is not an instrument. The GST Council's own release says its decisions take effect through notifications "which alone shall have the force of law". Before you tell anyone a measure is in force, find the notification and its date.
- A treaty is not the power to act. India is a Party to the WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control, and the Convention itself leaves action to "national law". COTPA was passed before India signed. When someone cites a treaty as the reason a body can act, ask for the Indian provision.
- A trace that forks is a result. Write both candidate deciders, the step where the trace stopped, and the document that would settle it. Do not pick one to make the note look tidy; the person who reads it will act on whichever body you name.
- Deciding and carrying out are different jobs, often done by different people. Parliament and the Central Government decided on smoking in public places. The long list of officers in Schedule III of the 2008 Rules enforces it, school principals and panchayat representatives among them. If your proposal is about enforcement, send it to the people who enforce.
- The central GST rate on sugar-added drinks is {{n:cgst_sugary_drinks_rate_pct}} per cent, in Schedule III of Notification 9/2025-Central Tax (Rate). Section 9(1) of the CGST Act caps the central rate at {{n:cgst_sugary_drinks_rate_pct}} per cent. The {{n:gst_demerit_rate_pct}}% in the GST Council's release is the GST rate it recommended, not the Union's share. Never say the Union levies {{n:gst_demerit_rate_pct}}%.
- When a trainee's proposal says "the government should", hand it back with three blanks: X, Y and Z. Ask them to fill each from a document they can name. Most will find that step 1, the instrument, is where they had not decided anything.
- A trace tells you who can act and through which instrument. It does not tell you whether that body will act, how soon, whether the instrument would work, or how the actors around it will push. It is also only as current as the copy of each instrument you read. Before you rely on a trace, check the newest amendment each copy names.

**Exercise 2** (teaching). A journalist has three minutes and asks: "Who can raise the tax on sugary drinks in India?"
Answer without jargon, give one sentence they can quote, and say what you are not sure of.

**1.** Here is a made-up provision from a made-up Act.

"The Board may, with the previous approval of the State Government and after previous
publication, by notification, make regulations to carry out the purposes of this Act."

Name the body that makes the instrument, the instrument, the other body that must agree, and
the process.

**2.** Here are three entries of the Seventh Schedule.

a. Union List, entry 52: "Industries, the control of which by the Union is declared by Parliament
   by law to be expedient in the public interest."
b. State List, entry 6: "Public health and sanitation; hospitals and dispensaries."
c. Concurrent List, entry 18: "Adulteration of foodstuffs and other goods."

For each, say which legislature can make laws on it, under Article 246.

**3.** The parts below come from a made-up trace, in no order. Write the one-sentence answer. Then say
which step the parts leave unanswered.

- provision: section 12 of the made-up State Food Act
- must agree: the State Government, by previous approval
- body: the State Food Commission
- instrument: regulations

**4.** Trace the proposal "Packaged foods high in sugar, salt or fat should carry a warning label" as
far as these provisions take you.

- FSS Act, section 16(1): "It shall be the duty of the Food Authority to regulate and monitor the
  manufacture, processing, distribution, sale and import of food so as to ensure safe and
  wholesome food."
- FSS Act, section 92(1): "The Food Authority may, with the previous approval of the Central
  Government and after previous publication, by notification, make regulations consistent with
  this Act and the rules made thereunder to carry out the provisions of this Act."
- FSS Act, section 93: every regulation made under the Act "shall be laid, as soon as may be
  after it is made, before each House of Parliament".
- Allocation of Business Rules, Second Schedule, Department of Health and Family Welfare, item
  3(a): "The Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006".

Then say what your trace does not settle.

**5.** A made-up district meeting proposes: "Shops near schools in the district should be stopped from
selling tobacco."

Use these facts. Section 6 of COTPA, as enacted in 2003, forbids selling cigarettes or other
tobacco products to anyone under eighteen. It also forbids it "in an area within a radius of one
hundred yards of any educational institution". The National Tobacco Control Programme's list of
"Notifications on date of effect of various provisions of the Act" includes G.S.R. 687(E), dated
18 September 2009, "For Section- 6(b)". Section 12(1) lets certain officers enter and search
where they suspect the Act is being broken. They are police officers not below sub-inspector,
and officers of the State Food or Drug Administration, authorised by the Central or State
Government.

Trace the proposal. Then say what your trace does not settle.

**6.** Here are four parts of the preamble of a real instrument, the Food Safety and Standards
(Packaging) Regulations, 2018, notified on 24 December 2018. The dots mark words left out.

- "Whereas the draft of certain regulations ... dated the 19th March, 2018 was published as
  required under Sub-section (1) of section 92 of Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 ... inviting
  objections and suggestions from the person likely to be affected thereby, before the expiry of
  a period of thirty days from date on which the copies of Gazette containing the said
  notification was made available to the public"
- "And whereas copies of the said Gazette were made available to the public on the 2nd April,
  2018"
- "And whereas objections and suggestions received on the said draft regulations have been
  considered by the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India"
- "Now, therefore, in exercise of the powers conferred by clause (k) of sub-section (2) of
  section 92, read with section 23 of the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 ... the Food Safety
  and Standards Authority of India hereby makes the following regulations"

Work backwards. Write the trace this instrument completed, step by step, with its dates. Then
say which part of the trace the preamble does not show.

**9.** A made-up newspaper headline reads: "Government sets 10% cut in cooking oil."

The words it rests on come from the Prime Minister's radio address, Mann Ki Baat, of 23 February

consumption of edible oil by ten percent (10%)'. Decide that you will use 10% less oil every
month."

Trace the headline. Then say what your trace does not establish.
