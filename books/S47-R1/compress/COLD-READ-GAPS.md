## 2. Who decides, and under what authority

### (a) "A higher tax on sugar-sweetened drinks"

Trace from the text:

1. **Instrument.** A rate notification under an existing Act (C11 step 1, C09).
2. **Subject.** GST sits outside the three Lists. Article 246A gives the power to Parliament and to every State Legislature (C04).
3. **Body and provision.** The GST Council recommends the rate (Article 279A(4); C09). The Central Government notifies the central rate under section 9(1) of the CGST Act (C09). Section 9(1) caps the central rate at twenty per cent (C09, C18).
4. **Process.** A Council decision needs three-fourths of the weighted votes cast, with the Centre at one-third and the States together at two-thirds (Article 279A(9)). Then notification and a date of effect. One example from the text: recommended 3 September 2025, notified 17 September, in force 22 September, amended in force 1 May 2026.
5. **Others.** No State can be outvoted in any way I was taught except within the Council vote.
   - The central notification appears to be at the cap already: Schedule III is headed "20%".
   - So a further rise in the central share would seem to need an amendment to section 9(1) by Parliament. That would be a Bill, and as a tax-only Bill probably a Money Bill (C06). This is my inference, not the text's.
   - Inside the Union, the Department of Revenue holds tax (C05). C10 and C17 say the Ministry of Finance and the GST Council "determine tax design and rates", and that health ministries are advisory.
6. **Sentence.** "The Central Government notifies the central rate, under section 9(1) of the CGST Act, on the recommendation of the GST Council under Article 279A(4) (three-fourths of weighted votes), up to a cap of twenty per cent. The State half is not yet traced."

**Where the text stops me.**
- It never says how the other half of the 40% is set. Which Act? Which body in each State? Is it capped? The text only says "central rate", which implies a State rate exists (C09-3).
- It never says whether the Central Government may change its rate without a Council recommendation (C09-6).
- The current rates are hidden behind placeholders. I recovered 20% from a table heading and 40% only in C18 (C09-1).
- "Cess" is unexplained, and I do not know whether the cess is a separate instrument with a separate decider (C18-2).
- What changed on 1 May 2026 is not stated (C09-8).
- Which office actually signs as "the Central Government" is never said (C02-4).
- Whom to address is unclear, because the text gives three conflicting rules: every body (C02), the first mover (C09) and a member of the deciding body (C18) (C18-4).

### (b) "A rule on what school canteens may sell"

1. **Instrument.** It forks. The candidates are:

   - (ii) State rules under FSS Act section 94(1), with the Food Authority's approval;

## 3. What the text taught me that changes what I do, say, accept or refuse

**What I would do**
- Before writing "policy", name the body that decided and where the decision is written down. Otherwise write "proposal" (C01).
- Treat inaction by an empowered body that has been asked as that body's current policy (C01).
- Name the losers of my own proposal before anyone else does (C01).
- For any decision, write four lines: propose, advise, decide, carry out. Fill each with a named body and its source (C02).
- Answer "under what authority?" with a numbered provision, never with "the government's powers". Read a notification backwards from "in exercise of the powers conferred by" (C03).
- Before writing "the Act requires", check commencement. An Act that is passed may not be in force (C03, C06).
- Give "whose subject" as a List and entry number, never "a Central subject". Check the Union Act before proposing a State rule on a Concurrent subject (C04).
- Name the department, and the Allocation of Business Rules item that gives it the subject. Send food-standards matters to Health, not to the food ministry or to Food Processing Industries (C05).
- Expect cross-department agreement or a Cabinet decision when two departments' business is touched (C05).
- Do not count on the pre-legislative consultation period, because the Policy is not a law. Do target the consultation and the committee stage as the two doors for evidence (C06).
- Do not treat a Private Member's Bill as a likely route to law (C06).
- Open the power-giving section before planning a comment. Find "by notification", "previous approval" and "previous publication". Count the objection period from the date the notice names. Do not wait for Parliament. Compare the draft and the final text clause by clause (C07).
- Ask four questions of any scheme: who approved it, for what period, with what money from whose budget, and whether an Act sits under it. Check whether it was continued past its end date (C08).
- Never write "the GST Council set" a tax. It recommends, and the Central Government notifies. Never write that the Union levies the full GST rate. Quote the date of the instrument behind each sentence (C09, C11, C18).
- Name actors individually and record each one's interest. Do not exclude objectors. Check advocacy figures as hard as industry figures. Disclose my own interests, money or not, at the front of the note (C10).
- Run the six-step trace, and do not send a note until Y is a numbered provision. Write "not yet traced" where needed. Send enforcement proposals to enforcers (C11).
- Read every passage I quote with the five frame lines, copying words and writing "not stated" where there are none. Write the counter-frame. Run the frame reading on my own draft (C12, C15).
- Quote frame findings with country, years and sample. Do not export a 2006-07 United States survey to India (C13).
- Match the frame of the request to the frame of the decider. Do not cite the PM's 10% oil call as support for tax or regulation (C14).
- Split any choice into questions evidence can answer (size, effect, cost) and questions it cannot settle on its own (values, fairness, feasibility, timing). Write the value premise out (C16).
- Decide whether a document informs or campaigns before writing it, and say so in it. Give values their own marked sentences (C17).
- Date every note, name the newest amendment I opened, and list what I did not open (C18).

**What I would say**
- "Recommends", not "sets".
- "X has power under Y", not "X can lawfully do Z" unless a source says so.
- "An important explanation among several", with country, year and sample.
- "This note sets out the options and does not recommend one."

**What I would accept**
- An actor's right to be heard despite its interest.
- That disclosure does not remove a conflict.
- That a forked or stopped trace is a valid result.

**What I would refuse**
- "Health is a State subject, so the State decides."
- "The government should" with no body named.
- A stakeholder list reading "industry / the public / the government".
- "The evidence shows we must."
- An unsourced "one in eight" as the opening of an informing note.
- A minister's announcement or a press release treated as an instrument.
- A treaty cited as a power.
- Doubt manufactured about evidence that agrees.
- Calling a missing consultation "unlawful" on the strength of the 2014 Policy alone.

## 6. Same word or number, two meanings

1. **"Agenda setting".** In C01 it is a policy-process stage: problem identification plus specifying alternatives. In C12 it is choosing which issues get talked about at all, a media and communication sense.
2. **"Section 23".** FSS Act section 23 is labelling (F5). General Clauses Act section 23 is previous publication (C07). C11 P6 then has "read with section 23 of the FSS Act" right after C07 trained the reader to read "section 23" as the General Clauses Act.
3. **"Second Schedule".** The Allocation of Business Rules' list of subjects (C05). The Transaction of Business Rules' "entry (a)" (C05, unlabelled). The NFSA's second schedule of meal standards (A4).
4. **"Rule" or "rules".**
   - An instrument under an Act, one rung below Acts (F5, C03).
   - The Allocation and Transaction of Business Rules, made directly under Article 77(3), which C05 also calls "orders".
   - "The menu rule" (C14) and "this section's rule" (C17) are general principles.
5. **"Order".** A rung of instrument (C03), the 1961 Rules called "two orders" (C05), and "by order" in Article 279A (F3).
6. **"Notification".** An instrument level (F5), the act of publishing by which a regulation is made ("by notification", C07), and an event date ("notified 17 September", C09).
7. **"Department".**
   - The user's academic department ("your department").
   - A government department.
   - The Allocation of Business Rules, which "calls them all 'departments'" (ministries included).
   - So "your department's third proposal" and "the department that leads" collide.
8. **"Policy".**
   - What government actually decided and did (C01).
   - A document of intention that decides nothing (National Health Policy 2017, which "could consider").
   - A non-statutory administrative decision (the Pre-legislative Consultation Policy).
   - C08 ("A national policy is not a law") uses it in the second sense right after C01 used the first.
9. **"Proposal".** Any course of action someone wants a public body to take (C01), versus something that names an instrument, without which it is "a wish, not yet a proposal" (C11).
10. **"Government", "the Union", "Union Government", "Central Government", "the Centre".** Used interchangeably and never declared synonyms. "Central Government" is also a statutory decider.
11. **"Frame" and "account".** C13 gives five frames, then Barry's seven "accounts". Whether an account is a frame is never said. C12 also says "frame" is used in four senses (values, problems, metaphors, arguments).
12. **"Tax on sugary drinks" / "the rate".** Sometimes the 20% central rate, sometimes the 40% GST rate, sometimes 28% plus a 12% cess. Each is "the tax", with no consistent label.
13. **"12".** The 12% cess (C18) and the 12-point apparent rise from 28 to 40 (C18). The text warns against reading the rise as 12 points, but the coincidence of numbers is itself confusing.
14. **"30" or "thirty".** The objection period and the laying days. The text flags this itself (C07). Both are placeholders.
15. **"20".** The 20% central GST cap (C09) versus the 20% NFHS planning figure (D6, background). Minor.
16. **"Approve" or "approval".** As the decide job (CCEA "approved" MSP, Cabinet "approved" NHP and the oilseeds mission) and as a second body's precondition ("previous approval of the Central Government"). So an approver is sometimes the decider and sometimes a second decider (C11 step 5).
17. **"Release".** The NHP release (C01), the oilseeds release (C08, C11), "the GST Council's release" / "the GST release" (C11, C14), introduced only in C17 P4. The definite article is used before any of them is identified.
18. **"Recommend".** The adviser's job (CACP, C02), and the GST Council's constitutional role (279A(4)), which C09 treats as not deciding even though the Council's vote is where the substantive choice happens. Only partly reconciled by C09's "neither side reaches it alone".
19. **"Previous".** Used in the legal sense of "prior" ("previous approval", "previous publication"), never flagged as a special usage.
20. **"Scheme", "programme", "mission".** The C01 programme ("organised activity through which an agency carries a policy out") versus the C08 scheme ("a programme the executive runs, with a name, a period, money and guidelines") versus "mission" (NMEO, Saksham). The relationship is partly given, and "mission" is never placed.
