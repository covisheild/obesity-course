# S55-R1 · Question selection and research strategy, rung 1 · concept inventory

Task 1 of `PIPELINE.md`, by the method in the specification §5, against frozen map `fc19c8bc-a216-4131-99fb-ebe3f19cec4a`; `python check/amendments.py --rung S55-R1` prints no amendment for S55-R1 and two for the bridge rung, S55-R2-A01 (P1 revised: the four assets, plus the unit or centre built on purpose) and S55-R2-A02 (P4 revised: implementation money — CSR, programme grants, tenders — screened for conflict of interest), both carried in `bridge_ref` below; A02 is `verify_at_intake`, so the CSR instrument is in `READY.md`. Serves: O1–O3 rung-1 outcomes, S1–S2 skills, B build target (ten candidate questions, each scored against the who-is-waiting test), G gate (explain why you rejected nine of ten); P1–P4, R1–R3, B2 and G2 of rung 2 only as far as rung 2 presupposes them. S55 has no prerequisite subject, and Book 0 teaches no study design, no research question and nothing about citation, so everything here is built from the floor; what Book 0 lends is the population against the person (D5 `C28`), the sample (D6 `C29`), bias as error that points one way (D7 `C30`), percentages (A4 `C04`), checking a reference (F3 `C41`), and premise, inference and conclusion (F4 `C42`). S36-R1 (Book 6) is not a prerequisite and is not assumed. Routed to S55-R2 and not taught here: the four assets and the portfolio ladder's costs and returns (P1, P2), documented systematic searching (P3, R2), the funding sequence and grant writing (P4, R1, R3); rung 1 carries only the doorstep of each: the names of the main study designs (C03), that a PECO sentence is what a search is built from and that you look for an existing answer before asking again (C02, C05), that a funder is one of the people who can be waiting for an answer and that research money and implementation money are different money (C07), and that the question you keep is the seed of the one question you will own (C08). **Two map sentences are not taught as written.** O3 ("most descriptive cross-sectional studies are never cited") is an empirical claim, and the open record does not support it: the "more than half of papers are never cited" figure goes back to a 1990 *Science* report that counted letters, corrections and meeting abstracts over a five-year window; Web of Science analyses by Larivière and Sugimoto put biomedical papers published in 2006 at 4% uncited by 2017, and medical-science papers from 1990 at 12% uncited after 27 years; no open study was found that measures uncitedness for descriptive cross-sectional studies as a design. C06 therefore teaches what the measurements show, why citation is a poor test of whether research was worth doing, and why the who-is-waiting test is the better one; the map's sentence is quoted only as a claim that was checked. O1's "most are made by accident" is likewise not a measured proportion: C01 teaches the routes by which a question arrives unchosen and asserts no share. Quantitative is decided by §7a (a concept is quantitative when the reader must carry out a technique whose result can be checked; S36-R1 C05 is the precedent for a non-arithmetic technique). Two qualify. C02 (population, exposure, outcome) owes a drill set of taking questions apart and writing them in one sentence: level 1–3, mark the population, exposure and outcome in a bare question; 4–6, write a one-sentence PECO question from an Indian obesity topic ("obesity in school children in Pune"); 7–8, a worked PECO sentence that is wrong (the outcome is really the exposure, the population has no boundary, two outcomes) for the reader to break; 9–10, a line from a thesis protocol or a meeting turned into one sentence, and what that question still cannot answer. About 8–10 problems. C08 (scoring ten candidates) owes a drill set of applying the scoring sheet: scoring a bare candidate on each item; totalling and ranking a list of five; the diagnostic where a high total hides a zero on the who-is-waiting item and the question was kept anyway; and a candidate proposed in a department meeting to score, rank and give a one-line reason for rejecting. About 6–8 problems. The scoring sheet is the course's own construction from the map's build target and from the published criteria in C04, C05 and C07; it must be presented as a working tool, never as a validated instrument. Everything else is stated, reasoned or judged, and is demonstrated by exercises and the build. Type: d derivable, e empirical, i institutional.
