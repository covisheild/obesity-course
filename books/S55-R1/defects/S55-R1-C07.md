# S55-R1-C07 · Who is waiting for this answer, and what will they do differently · audit defects

Auditor: Task 3, batch a4, 25 September 2026. Sources opened: `jhangiani_2019_methods.txt`, `ratan_2019.txt`,
`ioannidis_2016_useful.txt`, `ioannidis_2014_waste.txt`, `chalmers_2014_priorities.txt`. All 13 quotes were found
in the mapped files (whitespace and curly quotes normalised), and every locator matches its paragraph. No quote
states a number, and none needed one. No quote was edited. The definition's three attributions (Jhangiani:
practical implications; Ratan: decisions made by stakeholder groups; Ioannidis: a favourable change in decision
making) say no more than their sources. Kinds are right: Jhangiani is a textbook, the three journal papers are
`primary`, and the concept is derivable with a textbook anchor. Not quantitative, no practice set, no figure
(`figure_note`). No CSR, NMC thesis rule, Companies Act or obesity cut-off is stated as fact. Nothing here is dated
or institutional, so there is nothing to re-check against a newer instrument. The two Lancet papers are quoted a
sentence at a time, for the audit only, which is within their licence note. "Recurring" means a kind already found
in `books/B0|S01-R1|S02-R1|S36-R1/defects/`.

2. **error** (from compression hole H13) · `illustration.body`, second candidate ("what share have a waist
   measurement above the camp's cut-off?"), and the same wording in exercise 3's answer and in C08 candidate B. The
   officer is deciding whether the camp should *start* measuring waists. So the camp holds no waist data and has no
   waist cut-off. As written, the passing example fails feasibility (C04), and C08 still scores it 2 on item 2.
   Fix: "... whose body mass index is below the camp's cut-off, what share have a waist, measured by the study team
   for this study, above the cut-off the officer names?" Make the same change to C08 candidate B and exercise 3.
   Do not state a waist cut-off as fact (none is held).
