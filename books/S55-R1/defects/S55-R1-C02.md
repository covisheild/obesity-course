1. **`definition.text`, `simplified_explanation`, illustration 2 (draft 5), Practice 2b, 6 and 8: whether "person" requires an age is applied both ways** (from compression holes r1-H6 and r1-H8)
   - **Claim:** definition: "bounded ... by person (age, and any other trait that decides who is
     in)"; simplified: "Person: what ages, and anything else that decides who is in."
   - **What the text actually does:** it applies the rule both ways.
     - Treated as fenced with no age: Practice 2b ("patients of one clinic, 2026"), draft 5 in the
       second illustration (marked "yes" for its fenced population), rewrite 3, and exercise 1
       answers 3 and 4 ("first-year students at your college, 2027"; "adults at that clinic").
     - Faulted for having no age: Practice 6 ("unfenced in time and in person. The sentence names no
       year of camp and no age range") and Practice 8 ("names no ages and no year").
     - Practice 6's prompt asks for one part ("Say which part the report leaves unfenced"), and its
       answer names two.
   - **Smallest fix:** say in the definition and in the simplified line that the person side is
     fenced by whatever trait decides who is in. That trait may be an age band, or "patients of this
     clinic", or "first-year students"; add an age band when the trait alone lets in people of very
     different ages. Then change Practice 6's answer to "unfenced in time: it names no year of the
     camp". That fits the singular prompt, because "adults screened at the camp" is a person fence.
     Practice 8 can keep "no ages", because "school children" spans 5 to 18.
   - **Tag:** error. **Recurring** (a record contradicting itself, SELFCHECK 5; S36 defects).

2. **`definition.text`; exercise 2: no one-exposure rule, and "risk factor" is never defined** (from compression hole r1-H4)
   - **Claim:** "A question has one main outcome." Exercise 2 asks the reader to teach "why 'risk
     factors' is not an exposure". must_know[5] and Practice 9 use "risk factors" and "determinants".
   - **What the text actually does:** it states the one-outcome rule and never the one-exposure
     rule. The rule appears only inside exercise 2's answer ("one study is built round one"). "Risk
     factor" is not defined anywhere in C01 or C02.
   - **Smallest fix:** add to the definition: "A question also has one main exposure. 'Risk factors'
     is a name for many possible exposures, so it is not yet an exposure." Aslam and Emmanuel's
     single-question quote (already in C01) supports the one-main-thing rule.
   - **Tag:** gap.

3. **`simplified_explanation` ("Obesity judged from measured weight and height is an outcome"); Practice 4: no cut-off** (from compression hole r1-H5)
   - **Claim:** obesity is "judged from measured weight and height". Practice 4 asks the reader to
     "say how the outcome is judged".
   - **Audit finding:** partly closed as the text stands. Illustration 1's limits block now says the
     phrase is a placeholder, and that "a real protocol must name the cut-off it uses for children".
     No obesity cut-off is held in `sources/` for this book (audit brief, point 4), so the hole's
     proposed fix (give a named, dated cut-off) must **not** be applied here. Practice 4's model
     answer asks for nothing the text withholds. The overweight/obesity distinction is used in C07
     and C08, not here, and goes to that batch.
   - **Smallest fix:** add one clause to the same limits block saying where the reader will meet
     cut-offs (the subject that teaches them, by its reader-facing name). Do not state a cut-off.
   - **Tag:** gap.

4. **`simplified_explanation`: "short names made from their first letters"** (from compression hole r1-H7)

   - **Tag:** style.

5. **must_know[4]: Morgan's 54% reworded, and the underlying review undated**
   - **Claim:** "over half (54%) of 313 research studies did not report all four PICO parts".
   - **What the source says:** "a review of 313 research studies reported that over half (54%) of
     the studies did not report on the four PICO components [6]". Reference 6 is Thabane et al.,
     Canadian Journal of Anesthesia 2009, 56(1):71-79, which is not held. "Did not report on the
     four" does not settle whether the studies left out some of the parts or all of them. "All
     four" chooses one reading. The figure is also from 2009, a fact the reader cannot see.
   - **Smallest fix:** "Morgan and colleagues report, from a 2009 review they cite, that over half
     (54%) of 313 research studies did not report on the four PICO parts." Leave the rest as is.
   - **Tag:** gap. **Recurring** (a paraphrase stronger or narrower than the quote, SELFCHECK 1).
   - **Currency:** the figure is from 2009. Re-check it only if the review itself is obtained.
